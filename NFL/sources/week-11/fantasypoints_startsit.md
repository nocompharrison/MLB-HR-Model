# 2026 Week 11 Fantasy Football Start 'Em, Sit 'Em Advice
Joe DolanNOV. 21, 2026 11:10AM EST
Welcome to Week 11 of Fantasy Football Start ‘Em, Sit ‘Em!
I’m the new author of this column this year, so I’ll introduce a new format! I will break down every game by position and player. After listing the player (and perhaps some interesting stats or news I’ve found in my research), I’ll pop that player into one of several buckets, which are as follows:
  * **Crown Him** — This player is an every-week starter, someone you almost never take out of your lineup _(90-100% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Start Him** — More than likely, this player should be in your lineup because of his role, recent production, or matchup, but you might have to think about it just a little _(70-90% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Stream Him** — This player isn’t a weekly starter (at least not yet), but circumstances could lead to him being in your optimal lineup this week _(50-70% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Stash Him** — This player is worthy of being on your roster, but ideally I want to see more before putting him in a lineup _(25-50% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Sit Him** — This player should not be in your lineup because of lack of recent production, a difficult matchup, or injury _(10-25% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Drop Him** — This player shouldn’t be on your roster _(0-10% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_


For simplicity’s sake, this column will focus on a 12-team PPR league with a base lineup of **1 QB, 2 RB, 3 WR, 1 TE, and 1 W/R/T FLEX.** Obviously, different league sizes and formats will change recommendations… and that’s what our [Fantasy Points Front Office](https://www.fantasypoints.com/fphq/import), with its multiple start/sit tools, is for.

### **Indianapolis at Houston**
> Thursday, November 19, 2026, 8:15 PM Eastern
##### Quarterbacks
**CJ Stroud** (Hou) — The Texans’ defensive struggles and 3-6 record belie how well Stroud is playing and, subsequently, coming through for fantasy. After another 3 TD passes in Week 10, he’s now accounted for multiple touchdowns in six consecutive games. He’s 10th among all QBs in EPA/DB and has the 7th-lowest turnover-worthy-throw rate despite dropping back at the 10th-highest rate — the Texans cannot run the ball _at all_. Strangely, this Colts defense in Week 3 was the last time Stroud did not account for 2+ touchdowns in a game. I think he’ll have a more successful time this week. _**[Start Him]**_
**Daniel Jones** (Ind) — Anybody who owns a PlayStation or Xbox is thankful for Jones’ level of play this year on this decimated team so they can play _Grand Theft Auto VI_ instead of watching this Thursday night game. _**[Drop Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**Jonathan Taylor** (Ind) — With Taylor’s role shrinking — ostensibly to avoid beating him up in a lost season — there are officially ZERO Colts you can feel confident in starting for fantasy. From this Week’s [Everything Report](https://www.fantasypoints.com/nfl/articles/2026/the-everything-report-2026-fantasy-football-week-11): “Over the past four weeks — which now includes a game after the NFL trade deadline had passed — Taylor is averaging just 12.4 FPG (~RB21) on 14.3 XFP/G (~RB18), as his backfield XFP share has fallen to just 67.8% and his inside-the-five carry share has fallen to just 60%, mostly thanks to **Seth McGowan** getting 36% of the carries over this span.” So Taylor is a lead — but _not_ bell-cow — back in what could be the NFL’s worst offense over the next two months. That makes him a middling RB2 in games the Colts aren’t favored in. Looking ahead, Indy's only chance to be favored is at Tennessee in Week 15. On the bright side, the Texans’ disappointing defense has allowed the 5th-most FPG to opposing RBs over the last five games. _**[Start Him]**_

Montgomery is expected to return six weeks after suffering a groin injury. While he should get some decent volume, it’s easy to remember that he was _awful_ before the injury, ranking dead last among all qualified RBs in YPC (2.65) and EPA/rush (-0.345). Fortunately, if you need him, the Texans are massive favorites. He’s just an RB3. _**[Stream Him]**_

**Cardinals WRs** (Ari) — With all due respect to **Reggie Virgil** , **Zay Jones** , **Simi Fehoko** , **Devin Duvernay** , and **Harrison Wallace III** , none deserve a spot on your roster this week. _**[Sit Him]**_

That remains McCarthy’s biggest weakness — his 22.7% pressure-to-sack ratio is 10th worst among qualified QBs. He’s a viable bye-week fill in. _**[Stash Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**Bhayshul Tuten** (Jax) — In a relatively competitive Week 10 game against Tennessee, Tuten handled season-highs in backfield XFP share (94.0%), total XFP (29.0), and snap share (70.8%). He received all four of the Jaguars’ carries inside the five-yard line in Week 10, and he now averages 19.1 FPG (~RB6) on 17.1 XFP/G (~RB9) since Week 5. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Cam Skattebo** (NYG) — After **Devin Singletary** flopped in Week 9, Skattebo saw his highest snap share (89.1%) and backfield XFP share (97.2%). Skattebo averages 17.5 FPG in games that _either_ **Tyrone Tracy** or the now-cut **Najee Harris** misses. Tracy might be back in two weeks, but Skattebo should have a clear runway until then. The bad news is that the Jags allow the 5th-fewest FPG to opposing RBs, and they just bolstered their run defense with **Jordan Davis**. Nonetheless, I doubt you have two better options in this bye-heavy week. _**[Start Him]**_
**Chris Rodriguez** (Jax) — Merely a handcuff/closer now that the Jags have fully committed to Tuten. If you’re using Rodriguez, you better hope that the Jags blow the Giants out. _**[Sit Him]**_
**Devin Singletary** (NYG) — Banished to the shadow realm after his Week 9 stinker, and **Tyrone Tracy** is scheduled to return in two weeks. _**[Drop Him]**_
##### Wide Receivers
**Malik Nabers** (NYG) — Nabers had a three-week run as a top-12 fantasy WR from Weeks 4 through 6, but he’s finished outside that bracket in each of his other six games. It’s not for lack of opportunity; he’s led the Giants in first-read target share six times, and he’s the WR7 by XFPG — his -3.21 difference between XFPG and FPG (WR16) is 115th out of 117 WRs. He’s middling in both average separation score and win rate, and he’s poor after the catch, so it’s just possible that coming off a gnarly knee injury, he’s just not himself yet. Oh, and **Travis Hunter** might shadow him. It’s just really hard to sit a player who has Nabers’ opportunity. _**[Start Him]**_
**Parker Washington** (Jax) — Like with **Trevor Lawrence** , just hope the Jaguars have to throw to keep the game competitive, because when there’s volume, Washington is _elite_ (see below). _**[Start Him]**_
**Jakobi Meyers** (Jax) — The Jaguars have developed into an extremely “narrow” fantasy team — they’ve concentrated so much around **Bhayshul Tuten** and **Parker Washington** and are so strong defensively that it’s hard for anyone else to really get theirs. Meyers has finished as a top-24 weekly WR just once this year — back in Week 3. And his floor isn’t great either. He has just three additional finishes as a top-36 WR. Mostly, he’s a low-end bench option who probably can be dropped for someone with more upside. Meyers would need either a Washington injury or an unlikely Jaguars defensive collapse to gain relevance. Maybe he sneaks into your WR3 spot because so many teams are on bye. _**[Stash Him]**_
**Malachi Fields** (NYG) — Mostly, Fields has been a cardio king (16% target-per-route rate). But very quietly, he’s had 14 targets for 10 catches and 185 yards in **JJ McCarthy** ’s three starts. He posts 2.27 YPRR against two-high looks, which the Jags run at the NFL’s 12th-highest rate if you’re dying for a WR3 this week. _**[Sit Him]**_
**Brian Thomas Jr.** (Jax) — If you thought that holding BTJ through the trade deadline would mean his role would increase, you were wrong. He ran just a 47.4% route share in Week 10. _**[Drop Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**Isaiah Likely** (NYG) — Has finished the week as _the_ overall TE1 four times this year, and in three of his last five games. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Brenton Strange** (Jax) — While his route share does fluctuate a bit from game to game, the Jaguars do deploy Strange within the context of their offense with remarkable consistency — he has been exactly third on the club in route share in every game. He hasn’t been higher than that in 1st-read target share since Week 5, however, and he’s caught exactly 2 passes in three consecutive games. He’s a usable but mediocre TE1 in an awful week for byes. _**[Stream Him]**_
### **Las Vegas at Denver**
> Sunday, November 22, 2026, 4:25 PM Eastern
##### Quarterbacks
**Bo Nix** (Den) — As hard as it is to trust **Bo Nix** in fantasy, Week 11 is a great opportunity. The Raiders allow 21.0 QB FPG (third-most) and allow the fourth-highest EPA per dropback. Nix is also adding 2.4 FPG on the ground, and the Raiders’ short-pass defense and four-man-rush defense are the league’s worst. Nix is a high-end QB2 and a solid streamer this week. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Fernando Mendoza** (LV) — The debut of **Fernando Mendoza** as a starter had a lot to like, including a rushing line of 6/22/1. But this week could be a tough one for the young star: the Broncos have the third-best deep defense, Mendoza is facing pressure on 50% of his dropbacks, **Brock Bowers** will be out or limited, and the Raiders are only implied to score 15.3 points. Mendoza is a high-end QB3. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**Jonah Coleman** (Den) — The race to be Denver’s RB1 has been won by **Jonah Coleman**. Coleman’s snap share climbed in three straight games (rising from 50% to 82%) while the snap share for **JK Dobbins** has gone in the other direction, falling from 54% in Week 6 to 14% in Week 9. The Raiders allow 25.7 FPG to opposing RBs (fifth-most), and the Broncos are a 10-point favorite. Coleman is a mid-range RB2 and an easy start. _**[Start Him]**_
**Ashton Jeanty** (LV) — It has been over a month since **Ashton Jeanty** recorded 10 carries in a game. But it’s too early to abandon hope: being a 10-point underdog should help Jeanty in the passing game, and his 16.4% target share ranks second among backs. Despite his struggles, Jeanty averages 15.0 FPG (RB16) and remains a must-start RB2. _**[Start Him]**_
**Mike Washington** (LV) — After earning 25 carries in Week 8, **Mike Washington** has 14 carries for 20 total rushing yards over his last two games combined. Unless Jeanty is injured, Washington is a low-end RB4 and a bench stash. _**[Stash Him]**_
**JK Dobbins** (Den) — The declining snap share isn’t the only bad news for **JK Dobbins**. His carries have also dipped, with Dobbins seeing six, seven, and four carries in his last three games. His -0.92 rushing yards over expected per attempt ranks RB75, and he is stuffed 56.1% of the time. Dobbins is a low-end RB4. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Wide Receivers
**Jaylen Waddle** (Den) — In his last outing before the bye, **Jaylen Waddle** ran only 13% of routes and earned one target before leaving with a concussion. The Raiders are one of the 10 most generous defenses against opposing WRs (34.7 FPG) and allow short passes at the league’s highest rate, and Waddle is well-positioned to capitalize with his 40.7% slot rate. Waddle is a high-end WR3 in a friendly matchup. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Tre Tucker** (LV) — Fernando Mendoza’s first start saw **Tre Tucker** run 82% of routes, but Tucker saw only two targets and notched 1.9 fantasy points. Denver allows only 27.7 FPG to opposing WRs and is one of the six toughest defenses against the deep ball. If Bowers sits, Tucker is a mid-range WR3, but he’s a mid-range WR4 if Bowers starts. _**[Sit Him]**_
**Courtland Sutton** (Den) — Unless he finds a TD, **Courtland Sutton** has few paths to a fantasy roster. Those hoping for a TD may be in luck: the Raiders have the worst red-zone defense in the NFL, and Sutton has six end-zone targets and five inside the 10. Sutton is a TD-dependent, low-end WR4. _**[Sit Him]**_
**Jalen Nailor** (LV) — The leader for Las Vegas in routes in Week 10 was **Jalen Nailor** (90%), but the production did not follow. Nailor earned four targets and had 0.9 fantasy points. On the season, Nailor’s 0.59 YPRR ranks 166th among WRs, and he’s a sit regardless of whether Bowers plays. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**Michael Mayer** (LV) — It could be a big week for **Michael Mayer** : when Brock Bowers sits, Mayer has a strong case as a [mid-range TE1](https://x.com/SideHustleFF/status/2113668914827948153). Mayer leads the Raiders in targets on 1st-and-10 this season (22%), and he has fully earned his generous offseason contract. Even if Bowers goes, he figures to be limited. Mayer is a TE1 if Bowers sits and a mid-range TE2 if he goes. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Evan Engram** (Den) — With a 9.1% target share and 4.4 FPG, it has been a season to forget for **Evan Engram**. Those seeking relief during this week’s byemageddon may find solace in the Raiders allowing the seventh-most FPG to opposing TEs and Engram earning five targets in Week 9. But Engram is a low-end TE3, and this glass should only be broken in case of emergency. _**[Sit Him]**_
**Brock Bowers** (LV) — Early speculation suggests **Brock Bowers** has a [chance](https://www.nbcsports.com/nfl/profootballtalk/rumor-mill/news/brock-bowers-should-be-good-to-go-next-week) to play in Week 11. If Bowers makes it back, he’s a no-brainer start: he’s the TE1 in FPG when healthy (19.0 FPG), and Denver allows 19.3 FPG to opposing TEs (third-most). _**[Start Him]**_
> **UPDATE** : Bowers is QUESTIONABLE.
### **Minnesota vs. San Francisco**
> Sunday, November 22, 2026, 8:20 PM Eastern, Mexico City, Mexico
##### Quarterbacks
**Brock Purdy** (SF) — The Vikings defense avenged its relatively poor (by their standards) Week 1 performance against **Jordan Love** by living in the Packers’ backfield last week. Can they now establish themselves as Super Bowl favorites against Purdy, who is having an MVP-type season? Purdy and **Kyle Shanahan** have been wizards against both the blitz and pressure. Among 40 QBs who have 20 or more blitzed dropbacks, Purdy ranks 5th in EPA/DB and is tied with **Jared Goff** with 9 TD passes. I understand the trepidation of playing Purdy in this spot, but I just can’t galaxy brain myself to plug someone like **Cam Ward** into a lineup despite the matchup. _**[Start Him]**_
**Kirk Cousins** (Min) — It stinks for Kirkie that he doesn’t have **Jordan Addison** , but this is a sneaky-good matchup. The Vikings should have to drop back and throw 35+ times as 3-point underdogs against a 49ers secondary that’s allowed a league-high +5.4 schedule-adjusted passing FPG over the last five weeks. The total of 46.5 is the third-highest on the slate. He’s a viable bye-week fill-in. _**[Stash Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**Christian McCaffrey** (SF) — I have been watching CMC roast a helpless **Von Miller** on repeat. _**[Crown Him]**_
](<https://x.com/UnderdogNFL/status/2122138867545624914}}>) 
**Aaron Jones** (Min) — Jones had fewer carries than **Jordan Mason** last week and barely out-routed him, but two stats that work in his favor — he out-snapped Mason on third downs, and saw 7 of the 8 snaps inside the 10-yard line. Mason is basically a non-factor as a receiver, so if the projected gameflow holds up with the Vikings as 3-point underdogs, I prefer Jones as a low-end RB2/FLEX. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Jordan Mason** (Min) — In **Kirk Cousins** ’ first start back in Minnesota, Mason saw 16 carries to **Aaron Jones** ’ 11, and narrowly trailed in routes (8 to 11). But he saw just 38% of the backfield XFP to 60% for Jones, because Jones scored a touchdown on a reception down near the goal line. It’s clear Minnesota wants Mason to be its early-down grinder, but he’s going to need more work around the goal line to come through as anything more than a low-end RB3; he’s been targeted just 6 times on 43 routes. The Vikings are underdogs this week, to boot. _**[Stash Him]**_
**Kaelon Black** (SF) — Black has seen 11+ carries in three consecutive games and just had the first multi-reception game of his career in the shootout with Dallas in Week 10. I can see a scenario in which a fantasy manager has no choice but to play Black and hope for significant play volume, but the matchup is brutal — the Vikings allow the 4th-fewest FPG to opposing RBs. Is it possible the 49ers take the same approach the Lions did a few weeks ago, and just run the hell out of the ball to counteract Minnesota’s nasty blitz? _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Wide Receivers
**Justin Jefferson** (Min) — He didn’t explode, but Jefferson’s 13 targets in **Kirk Cousins** ’ first game back were his most since 2024. The 49ers are allowing the 2nd-most schedule-adjusted FPG to opposing outside WRs over the last five weeks. _**[Crown Him]**_
](<https://x.com/ScottBarrettDFB/status/2122778046461812865}}>) 
**Deebo Samuel** (SF) — Minnesota is a brutal matchup for just about anyone, and that includes slot WRs — they’ve allowed the 2nd-fewest FPG to slot WRs this year. But Deebo is not an ordinary slot receiver, and **Kyle Shanahan** is not an ordinary coach. Among 190 receivers and tight ends with 20 or more routes run against a blitz this year, Deebo is 18th in YPRR (2.91). I’d be absolutely stunned if there wasn’t at least one screen designed for Deebo when Shanahan anticipates the Vikings bringing the house. A viable WR3 this week. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Keenan Allen** (SF) — In the least shocking news ever, Allen continued to be an elite target-earner in his first game with the 49ers. He was targeted on 7 of his 21 routes, which actually _increased_ his 29% target-per-route rate — he’s now 6th among 117 WRs in TPRR. The question is just how valuable his role will be if and when **Mike Evans** returns, as early as this week. With six teams on bye, you might not have the luxury of putting Allen on your bench to find out. _**[Stash Him]**_
**Jauan Jennings** (Min) — Jennings has just 6 catches on the season and has missed the last two weeks, but he’s trending in the right direction for this REVENGE GAME. You know he’s aching to make a play against his former team… which he reportedly blocked a trade back to earlier this season. I’d have to be _really_ desperate to put him in a lineup, however. _**[Drop Him]**_
**Mike Evans** (SF) — It looks like Evans (Achilles) has a chance to play this week. If he goes, you really have no choice but to acquiesce and get him in your lineup. He’s 2nd among all WRs in YPRR (3.65), 3rd in TPRR (.34), 11th in separation score, and 4th in win rate. He’s been legitimately elite when on the field. _**[Start Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**George Kittle** (SF) — You know that stat I had for **Deebo Samuel** against the blitz? Yeah, Kittle is even better. He averages _5.08 YPRR_ against blitzes, #2 among 190 qualified WRs and TEs. _**[Crown Him]**_
**TJ Hockenson** (Min) — The Vikings are hoping to get Hockenson (chest/wrist) back this week after a one-game absence. Hock’s career 14.3 FPG with Kirkie would rank him as the TE4 this year, tied with **George Kittle** and ahead of **Travis Kelce** , **Tyler Warren** , and **Sam LaPorta**. And he has an excellent role this year, ranking as the TE8 by XFPG. A QB upgrade and an excellent playoff schedule make him one of the most appealing TEs to roster right now. _**[Start Him]**_

### **Cincinnati at Washington**
> Monday, November 23, 2026, 8:15 PM Eastern
##### Quarterbacks
**Joe Burrow** (Cin) — The MVP favorite has accounted for 4 or more touchdowns in FIVE CONSECUTIVE GAMES. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Jayden Daniels** (Was) — The Commanders are 6-point underdogs in a game with a 54.0 implied total, the highest on the entire Week 11 slate. I’d like to see Daniels do more with his legs, but there should be enough play volume here for Daniels to come through in a big way for fantasy. The Bengals allow the 8th-most schedule-adjusted FPG to opposing QBs over the last five weeks. _**[Start Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**Chase Brown** (Cin) — From this week’s [Everything Report](https://www.fantasypoints.com/nfl/articles/2026/the-everything-report-2026-fantasy-football-week-11): Brown “averages 17.8 XFP/G (would rank ~RB5) and a 70% backfield XFP share (~RB11) over that split thanks to an elite receiving role that has finally started paying off (to the tune of 16.4 FPG | ~RB13 since Week 7).” He has just 10.5 carries per game over that span, but the Bengals know **Joe Burrow** butters their bread, and any pass catcher attached to Burrow is gold right now. _**[Start Him]**_
**Rachaad White** (Was) — White hilariously ceded 55.2% of the backfield XFP share to the completely washed **Austin Ekeler** last week. Unbelievable. He _did_ run a higher route share, narrowly — 35.9% to 33.3%. If **Jacory Croskey-Merritt** returns this week, White is going to have to usurp that receiving role from Ekeler to maintain any sort of fantasy relevance. The Commanders are big underdogs this week, so in theory a pass-catching RB role could fuel a valuable FLEX finish. _**[Stash Him]**_
**Jacory Croskey-Merritt** (Was) — “Bill” is eligible to return from his groin injury this week. He had his two best games of the season — RB22 in Week 1, RB18 in Week 5 — in his only two full games with **Jayden Daniels** this year, which makes sense given defenses have to respect the threat of Daniels’ legs. I’m not sure I’m thrilled to play “Bill” this week with the Commanders big underdogs given he’s a zero in the passing game, but there’s a chance he’s Washington’s goal-line back in a game with a projected total of 54.0. _**[Stash Him]**_
**Samaje Perine** (Cin) — Perine has outcarried **Chase Brown** 7 to 4 inside the red zone over the last four games, and touchdowns are the only way he’ll come through for fantasy — his only two games as a top-24 RB came in the two games he scored. Mostly, he’s a handcuff. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Wide Receivers
**Ja’Marr Chase** (Cin) — No lower than the WR4 over his last three games. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Tee Higgins** (Cin) — Has a bit of a knee concern, but it doesn’t sound like he’ll miss any time. He’s attached to **Joe Burrow** , so start him. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Stefon Diggs** (Was) — Diggs has played five full games with **Jayden Daniels** this year. He’s ranked inside the top 24 at WR in four of those five games, and his Week 10 performance was his best of the year — WR8 with season highs in targets (10), catches (6), and yards (83). But he’s also exceeded his XFP in each of his last four games — he’s the WR17 with 14.4 FPG since Daniels returned to the lineup, but just the WR_44_ by XFPG (10.12). But the Commanders are big underdogs in this game, and Diggs crushes man coverage — the Bengals run man at the NFL’s 2nd-highest rate, and Diggs averages 2.42 YPRR against man coverage. Also, he runs two-thirds of his routes from the slot, so he can avoid **DJ Turner** ’s shadow coverage. _**[Start Him]**_
**Antonio Williams** (Was) — Williams scored his first touchdown since Week 1 last week, but he also had his fewest targets of the season (2). He’s averaged just 7.7 XFPG since **Terry McLaurin** went down despite running over a 70% route share in each game. Because he plays in the slot more than **Treylon Burks** , I like him better than Burks in this matchup — the Bengals are a massive slot funnel, allowing the 3rd-most FPG to that alignment vs. the 2nd-fewest to outside WRs. He’s a low-end WR3 on Byemaggedon. _**[Stash Him]**_
**Treylon Burks** (Was) — Burks isn’t a big fantasy factor anyway, but he runs over 70% of his routes from the perimeter, and if anyone will _potentially_ draw **DJ Turner** 's shadow coverage, it’s him. That makes him a low-end WR4 at best, even in a matchup with the Commanders' big underdogs. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**Chig Okonkwo** (Was) — The Bengals have been a glorious TE matchup for years now, and this year has been no exception — they’ve allowed the 9th-most FPG to opposing TEs. Okonkwo missed some time in Week 10 while being dinged up, so he ran just a 59% route share (**Ben Sinnott** scored a TD), but he tied **Stefon Diggs** with a 29.2% first-read target share despite missing some snaps, and set a new season-high with 8 total targets. He’s a legit TE1 option this week. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Mike Gesicki** (Cin) — Gesicki has just 20 scoreless yards over the last two weeks. He’s easy to break down — he doesn’t play a full-time role, but he’s tied to **Joe Burrow** , so he can turn in a touchdown at any point. He’ll kill your lineup otherwise, though — in the three games in which he hasn’t scored a TD this year, he’s _totaled_ 5.0 FP. _**[Sit Him]**_
