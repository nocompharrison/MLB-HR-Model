#### **Strong Starts**
_Here are players that should be in starting consideration for most fantasy teams._
**[Breece Hall (RB)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/breece-hall.php)**
Last week, the Jets’ backfield tilted back further in favor of [Breece Hall](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/breece-hall.php). Hall finished with a 58.8% snap rate, 47.8% of the rushing work, and a 43.8% route share. Those numbers all eclipsed [Braelon Allen](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/braelon-allen.php)‘s usage. Hall finished with 15 touches and 108 total yards. Hall has only managed a woeful 1.66 yards after contact per attempt and a 13% missed tackle rate this season. He’s a volume play at running back again this week. Over their last five games, the Bolts have fielded a middle-of-the-road run defense, allowing the eighth-most yards after contact per attempt while ranking 16th in rushing yards over expectation per attempt, 15th in rushing success rate, and 18th in missed tackle rate.
**[Garrett Wilson (WR)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/garrett-wilson.php)**
[Garrett Wilson](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/garrett-wilson.php) is the WR20 in fantasy points per game, sporting a 27.5% target share with 2.18 YPRR, eight red zone targets, 13 deep targets, and a 34.7% first-read share. There was some debate in the middle of the season, but Wilson is their undisputed WR1. The on-paper matchup is glorious. Over their last five games, the Bolts have allowed the eighth-most yards per target and the tenth-most fantasy points per opportunity to perimeter wide receivers. With Geno Smith back, Wilson is a top-15 wide receiver this week with WR1 upside.
#### **Fantasy Football Flex & Deep Plays to Consider**
_Let’s fill out those flex spots._
**[Geno Smith (QB)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/geno-smith.php)**
Geno Smith will be back in the huddle this week. Smith (ankle) got knocked out of last week’s game, and his status has been up in the air all week. He had limited practices all week until he upgraded to a full practice on Friday. Smith is QB26 in fantasy points per game with two QB1 games (QB4, QB5) in weekly scoring this season. Among 31 qualifying quarterbacks, Smith is 11th in yards per attempt, 13th in passing success rate, fourth in hero throw rate, and 12th in catchable target rate. Smith is a strong QB2 this week who could be a QB1. The Bolts’ pass defense has been lacking all season. Over their last five games, the Chargers have allowed the 11th-most yards per attempt, the tenth-highest passing success rate, and the 14th-highest EPA and fantasy points per dropback.
**[Justin Herbert (QB)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/justin-herbert.php)**
Last week, [Justin Herbert](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/justin-herbert.php) had his best game of the season from a fantasy lens. The sad thing is that he only produced 16.3 fantasy points as the QB15 for the week. Most of that fantasy production was tied to his seven rushing attempts, 51 yards on the ground, and a rushing score. That was Herbert’s third game with more than 40 rushing yards, but it was his first rushing score. I don’t think we can bank on the rushing output weekly from him. Herbert had a middling day as a passer with only 18 passing attempts, 129 passing yards, and zero passing touchdowns. It was nice to see him break the streak of consecutive games with an interception, though. I don’t have any faith in Herbert, but I do have plenty of faith that the Jets’ pass defense is horrible. If any woeful pass defense can coax a decent QB2 week out of Herbert, it’s the Jets. Over their last five games, they have allowed the third-most yards per attempt, the third-highest passing success rate, and the most EPA and fantasy points per dropback.
**[Omarion Hampton (RB)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/omarion-hampton.php)**
Last week, [Omarion Hampton](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/omarion-hampton.php) had EASILY his best game of the 2026 season as the RB13 for the week. Hampton had 23 touches (22 carries), 105 total yards, and a score. That’s the positive news. Ok, now it’s time for the bad. Hampton played 63% of the snaps in a game that they played with a lead in. He had 64.7% of the rushing work, BUT he had only a 33% route share, which equaled the route share for [Keaton Mitchell](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/keaton-mitchell.php) and was behind [Kimani Vidal](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/kimani-vidal.php) (40.7%). This all quietly says that Hampton is game script sensitive and could still be crushed in any game moving forward in which the Bolts are trailing. It’s sad, but true. The good thing for Hampton is hopefully the Bolts are playing with a lead this week, and it’s a nice matchup for him on the ground. Hampton has 2.22 yards after contact per attempt and a 13% missed tackle rate this season (OOF!). The Jets still have a woeful run defense, so hopefully Hampton can take advantage. Over their last five games, the Jets have allowed the seventh-highest explosive run rate, the fourth-highest rushing success rate, and the third-most yards before contact per attempt.
**[Braelon Allen (RB)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/braelon-allen.php)**
Last week, [Braelon Allen](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/braelon-allen.php) saw his workload cut further as [Breece Hall](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/breece-hall.php) resumed his lead back duties for New York. Allen played 47.1% of the snaps with 34.8% of the rushing work and a 37.5% route share (2.4% target share). Allen finished with nine touches and 46 total yards. The reduction in work has put him back in the touchdown-dependent flex area code again. Allen has still been amazing on a per-touch basis with 2.70 yards after contact per attempt and a 20% missed tackle rate, but with his volume being limited, it’s tough to expect more than flex production out of him. Over their last five games, the Bolts have fielded a middle-of-the-road run defense, allowing the eighth-most yards after contact per attempt while ranking 16th in rushing yards over expectation per attempt, 15th in rushing success rate, and 18th in missed tackle rate.
**[Ladd McConkey (WR)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/ladd-mcconkey.php)**
Mike McDaniel runs his passing attack essentially through one receiver. We saw that with Tre Harris last week, so with Ladd McConkey back this week. I don’t have any interest in starting another receiving option in the Chargers’ passing attack. McConkey (back) practiced on a limited basis all week and has been listed as questionable. When McConkey has been on the field, he has had a 19% TPRR with 2.34 YPRR, 0.107 first downs per route run, and a 15.5% first-read share. He could return solid flex value this week. Across their last five games, the Jets have allowed the ninth-most yards per target and the fourth-most fantasy points per opportunity to perimeter wide receivers.
**[Adonai Mitchell (WR)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/adonai-mitchell.php)**
[Adonai Mitchell](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/adonai-mitchell.php) is the WR49 in fantasy points per game, with two games this season with more than 12 PPR fantasy points. Mitchell has a 21% target share (17.4 aDOT) with 1.95 YPRR, five red zone targets, 15 deep targets (35.7% of his target volume), and a 24.3% first-read share. Mitchell could have another massive day in Week 11 if his quarterback can get him the ball consistently. Over their last five games, the Bolts have allowed the eighth-most yards per target and the tenth-most fantasy points per opportunity to perimeter wide receivers. Also during that stretch, this pass defense has allowed the seventh-most deep passing yards, the sixth-highest passer rating to deep passing, and the second-highest deep passing success rate. With Mitchell’s role in the offense, there’s a ton of volatility week-to-week, but the payoff could be huge this week.
**[Mason Taylor (TE)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/mason-taylor.php)**
[Mason Taylor](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/mason-taylor.php) is on the streaming radar this week. Since Week 6, he has had a 64.1% snap rate, a 54.5% route share, a 13.9% target share, 1.42 YPRR, five red zone targets, and a 14.2% first-read share. The Bolts have been a favorable matchup for tight ends this season. Over their last five games, the Chargers have allowed the most yards per target and the sixth-most fantasy points per opportunity to tight ends. Taylor could flirt with TE1 production this week. Taylor has one TE1 outing this season (TE2 in Week 7).
