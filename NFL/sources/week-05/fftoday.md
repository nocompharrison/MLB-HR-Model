**Favorites:** QB [Jalon Daniels](https://www.fftoday.com/stats/players/19961/Jalon_Daniels)
Considering a rookie quarterback on the road in his second career start invites disaster, but Dallas’ pathetic defense presents a rare opportunity for Jalon Daniels to exceed expectations. The Cowboys defense was one of the most charitable to opposing offenses in 2025 and they’ve only continued that trend in 2026, ranking near the bottom of the league in defensive efficiency and points allowed per drive. They’ve surrendered the fourth-most fantasy points to opposing QBs so far this season.
Daniels struggled pushing the ball downfield in his first start, relying on quick throws near the line of scrimmage as he averaged just under five yards per attempt. His passing ceiling remains practically nonexistent, but his rushing ability gives him Justin Fields-like fantasy upside. Daniels ripped off 55 yards on eight carries last week and Dallas has coughed up a league-high 188 rushing yards to quarterbacks -- over 40 yards more than any other team. If he doesn’t produce on the ground, fantasy managers are looking at a dud, but his rushing ability still offers genuine upside as a fill-in QB2/Superflex starter.
**On the Fence:** WR [Emeka Egbuka](https://www.fftoday.com/stats/players/19351/Emeka_Egbuka)
Emeka Egbuka remains Tampa Bay's primary target earner, but leaning on him as an every-week starter just isn’t a recipe for success right now. Sure, he leads the receiver room with 24 targets through four contests, but that raw volume has not translated into high-end production, as he has yet to post a top-30 fantasy week. The quarterback change further clouds an already murky outlook as Jalon Daniels looked scared to attack downfield last week. As a result, Egbuka was held to just two catches for 13 yards on four targets.
Despite the obvious offensive limitations, Dallas provides a beautiful opportunity for a get-right game. The Cowboys secondary has bled explosive plays, yielding 20 gains of 20+ yards on the season and they also give up more than two fantasy points per target to opposing wideouts. Egbuka has the athletic ability to take advantage of Dallas's defensive holes, but he does present an unstable floor which keeps him in WR3 range.
**Fades:** RB [Bucky Irving](https://www.fftoday.com/stats/players/18997/Bucky_Irving)
Yuck. That’s about all that can be said about Bucky Irving’s recent production as he now heads into a matchup against Dallas with serious red flags all over the place. He handled 16 touches for 61 yards against Green Bay, but the high-value touches were split between Kenneth Gainwell in the passing game and Sean Tucker near the goal line. Irving has suddenly been relegated to low-potential touches between the twenties.
The quarterback shift to Jalon Daniels only makes matters worse. Mobile passers tend to tuck the ball and run instead of checking down to running backs, which strips away Irving's receiving floor. Daniels could also snipe rushing opportunities near the end zone even if Irving doesn’t cede them to Tucker again.
Dallas allows plenty of running back production, but Irving lacks access to the type of touches that produce difference-making fantasy days. You drafted him to be your RB2, but he’s barely alive as a Flex option right now.

**Favorites:** QB [Dak Prescott](https://www.fftoday.com/stats/players/15681/Dak_Prescott)
Dak Prescott enters Week 5 as a locked-in fantasy starter already, but this matchup offers the potential for a true “boom” game, so he’s being called out as a “Favorite” beyond his normal mid-to-low-QB1-level.
Tampa Bay’s defense is living and dying by the blitz as they’re doing so on over forty percent of defensive snaps this season. The problem is that their rushers aren’t actually getting home as they sit near the bottom of the league in overall pressure rate. Dak is the kind of QB who has absolutely shredded this type of defense throughout his career, as long as he’s had a clean pocket. Despite their low pace of play, Dallas also throws at one of the highest rates over expectation in the league, ensuring that the Cowboys will keep the gas pedal down even with a positive game script. Tampa Bay will also be without star Antoine Winfield in an already depleted secondary.
Don’t be scared by the potential positive game script -- Prescott has top-five upside in this one. Start him with confidence.
**On the Fence:** RB [Javonte Williams](https://www.fftoday.com/stats/players/17753/Javonte_Williams)
Javonte Williams is coming off a monster three-touchdown performance, but fantasy managers should brace for a much more difficult evening on Thursday night. Tampa Bay has long been a brick wall against the run, holding opposing rushers to 3.3 yards per carry this season. They’ve consistently sold out against the run while leaving their secondary vulnerable.
Williams continues to dominate backfield opportunities in Dallas, taking over eighty percent of running back touches, but his touches have masked some glaring efficiency concerns. He averages just 3.7 yards per carry, and 44 percent of his entire fantasy output comes from touchdowns. You’re essentially relying on multiple scores to deliver a true ceiling game and that’s unlikely against this defensive front.
Dallas is expected to have a positive game script as substantial home favorites, so there’s potential volume here, but recognize that there’s also a risk that he gets you single-digit PPR fantasy points even in a dominant role in a good offense.
**Fades:** TE [Jake Ferguson](https://www.fftoday.com/stats/players/18072/Jake_Ferguson)
Streaming tight ends often comes down to chasing recent touchdown scores and relying on Jake Ferguson this week is not a great option. Ferguson has already found the end zone three times, so it's easy to expect more of that, but his underlying involvement in the passing game is worrying.
Ferguson has totaled only 83 receiving yards across four games while averaging under seven yards per reception. He's essentially a complete touchdown-or-bust player right now and the truth is that, with CeeDee Lamb and George Pickens healthy, Dallas is not designing looks for Ferguson.
When an offense has had as much success as Dallas' has this season, there's really no reason to change up the script. Unless Ferguson falls into the end zone, you're likely looking at a single-digit PPR day. 
**Prediction:** Cowboys 30, Buccaneers 17 [^ Top](https://www.fftoday.com/articles/ff/26-ff-wk5.html#top)

**On the Fence:** RB [Will Shipley](https://www.fftoday.com/stats/players/19104/Will_Shipley)
Shipley went from an interesting waiver claim to potentially leading Philadelphia's backfield in a matter of days. Tank Bigsby is headed to injured reserve, while Saquon Barkley has missed both practices this week with the hamstring injury that knocked him out Sunday. If Barkley sits, Shipley gets the first crack at a backfield that suddenly has very few healthy options.
The opportunity is better than the setup. Jacksonville has allowed the third-lowest explosive run rate and fewest yards after contact per attempt, while Shipley has generated just 1.63 yards after contact per carry in his NFL career. Philadelphia's offensive line is changing on the fly after Lane Johnson retired Tuesday and Cam Jurgens was traded Wednesday, and new center Drew Kendall has missed consecutive practices.
If Barkley is out, Shipley is a volume-based RB3/Flex. I wouldn't automatically push him into RB2 territory just because the depth chart emptied out.
**Fades:** QB [Jalen Hurts](https://www.fftoday.com/stats/players/17116/Jalen_Hurts)
This is a fade of the ceiling, not a bench call.
Jacksonville was already favored in the look-ahead market around -4.5 and opened the week around -5.5. The Jaguars are now sitting at -7.5. The personnel picture explains why. Barkley and DeVonta Smith have missed both practices and have been ruled out, Dallas Goedert remains limited, Dontayvion Wicks was added Thursday with a hamstring injury, and the offensive line has been reshuffled in less than a week. The current team total for Philadelphia is only around 17 points.
Hurts can still rescue the fantasy day with his legs, which is why you're starting him in most leagues. I just don't see the setup for the usual top-five ceiling. He belongs closer to the bottom of the QB1 range this week.

He has not topped five targets since the season opener and he even completely disappeared for a zero-target game in Week 2. Cincinnati is also running a heavy tight end rotation, which has led to more playing time for Drew Sample and Tanner Hudson while Gesicki watches from the sidelines.
