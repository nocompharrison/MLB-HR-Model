**Strong Starts**
_Here are players that should be in starting consideration for most fantasy teams._
**[Emanuel Wilson (RB)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/emanuel-wilson.php)**
Last week, [Emanuel Wilson](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/emanuel-wilson.php) was the RB6 in fantasy as Seattle’s lead back. I expect him to hold that role again this week. Last week, Wilson played 58.6% of the snaps with a 63.6% rushing share, a 34.6% route share, and an 18.2% target share. He finished with 24 touches and 120 total yards (two scores). Wilson has been fairly efficient with this workload. Among 46 qualifying rushers, he ranks 23rd in yards after contact per attempt and 18th in missed tackle rate. Wilson should have success again this week against a 49ers’ run defense that has allowed the 13th-highest rushing success rate, the tenth-highest yards before contact per attempt, and the 11th-highest gap rushing success rate (Wilson: 41.5% gap).
**[Mike Evans (WR)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/mike-evans.php)**
[Mike Evans](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/mike-evans.php) is an absolute DAWG! Evans gutted it out last week with a myriad of injuries, including broken ribs. Evans grimaced and was slow to get up after every catch, but that didn’t stop him. Evans is the WR24 in fantasy points per game, and he’s been awesome when on the field for the 49ers. Last week, Evans had a 67.6% route share, a 26.7% target share, 3.30 YPRR, and a 31.3% first-read share. Overall, this season Evans has a 60.8% route share, a 21.2% target share (32% TPRR), 2.80 YPRR, four red zone targets, four deep targets, and a 24.7% first-read share. This week, he’ll face a Seattle secondary that has utilized two high at the ninth-highest rate in the NFL (59.1%). Against two high, Evans has a 33% target-per-route run rate and 3.10 YPRR, which are absolutely elite numbers. Seattle has allowed the sixth-fewest yards per target and the seventh-fewest fantasy points per opportunity to perimeter wide receivers. Evans has the talent to overcome a bad matchup, though. In most leagues, I’m still starting him.
_*I’m not starting any Seattle pass catchers this week outside of[Jaxon Smith-Njigba](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/jaxon-smith-njigba.php). The 49ers have utilized single-high with 55.5% of their defensive snaps. Only Smith-Njigba has more than a 15% TPRR or 1.00 YPRR against single-high this season for Seattle. I also don’t have a ton of hope for [AJ Barner](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/aj-barner.php) as a streaming option at tight end. The 49ers have limited tight ends to the fifth-fewest yards per target and the third-fewest fantasy points per opportunity.*_

Among 29 qualifying passers, he is 13th in yards per attempt, 17th in passing success rate, 22nd in highly accurate throw rate, and 18th in hero throw rate. Stafford could easily have a bounceback game this week with strong QB1 production as he attempts to keep pace with [Josh Allen](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/josh-allen-qb.php). Buffalo has allowed the fifth-most passing yards, the 14th-highest yards per attempt, the seventh-highest passing success rate, and the sixth-most fantasy points per dropback.
**[Davante Adams (WR)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/davante-adams.php)**
In his two games played with [Puka Nacua](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/puka-nacua.php), [Davante Adams](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/davante-adams.php) has had a 19% target share with 0.81 YPRR (58 total receiving yards), two red zone targets, three deep targets, and a 23.2% first-read share. His other two games of the season have loudly displayed that Adams isn’t near the end of the line, so this feels like small sample variance with his production dipping so much. His target share and first-read share were still strong in those games. I’m expecting a bounce-back performance for Adams in Week 5. Buffalo has the 11th-highest two-high rate (56.2%). This season against two-high, Adams has a 21% target-per-route run rate with 1.88 YPRR. His numbers are much better against single-high, but these aren’t horrible metrics by any stretch of the imagination. Buffalo has allowed the eighth-most fantasy points per opportunity to perimeter wide receivers.
**Fantasy Football Flex & Deep Plays to Consider**

**[Khalil Shakir (WR)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/khalil-shakir.php)**
[Khalil Shakir](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/khalil-shakir.php) is the WR64 in fantasy points per game. His best performance of the season came last week as the WR24 in weekly scoring. Overall, Shakir has a 19.3% target share with 1.98 YPRR (65.4% route share), five red zone targets (zero scores), two deep targets, and a 23.4% first-read share. Shakir is flex-worthy again this week. The Rams are tied for the most receiving touchdowns allowed to slot receivers while also surrendering the seventh-most fantasy points per opportunity to the position.
