**[Drake Maye (QB)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/drake-maye.php)**
[Drake Maye](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/drake-maye.php) bounced back in a big way last week as the QB1 for the week. Josh McDaniels changed up the offense, and Maye responded in a big way. In Weeks 1-3, New England had the seventh-lowest motion rate and the fifth-lowest play-action rate. Well, last week, New England was fourth in motion rate (75.3%) and ninth in play-action rate (33.3%). Last week, Maye had the 12th-best passing success rate and 11th-best EPA per dropback when New England utilized motion. I expect the Pats to continue to utilize these playcalling cheat codes moving forward. Maye also ran seven times for 47 yards on the ground, which were the most rushing attempts and rushing yards for Maye since Week 1. Last week, among 32 qualifying quarterbacks, Maye was 17th in yards per attempt, 12th in passing success rate, and 20th in highly accurate throw rate. The Raiders have been a mixed bag as a pass defense, allowing the 13th-highest passing success rate, the fifth-lowest EPA per dropback, and ranking 16th in yards per attempt. The heavy inclusion of motion won’t be as big of a cheat code for Maye and the Pats this week, though. The Raiders have allowed the fourth-fewest yards per attempt and the second-lowest EPA per dropback to motion. Maye could continue his bounce back this week, but it’ll be a tougher climb up that hill this week.
**[Romeo Doubs (WR)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/romeo-doubs.php)**
[Romeo Doubs](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/romeo-doubs.php) fulfilled all of the offseason hope and promise last week as the WR8 for the week. Last week, Doubs had a 21.6% target share, 58 receiving yards (2.07 YPRR), three red zone targets (two scores), one deep target, and a 26.3% first-read share. Since Week 2, Doubs has had a 17.8% target share with 2.78 YPRR, four red zone targets, three deep targets, and a 19.2% first-read share. We’ll see if Doubs can continue the production this week, but the coverage structure works in his favor. The Raiders have the tenth-highest rate of two-high (56.2%). Since Week 2, against two-high, Doubs has a 19% target-per-route run rate (leads the team) with 4.54 YPRR (WOW!). The Raiders are a middle-of-the-road matchup for Doubs, ranking 17th in yards per target allowed to perimeter wide receivers. Doubs could have another strong week against Vegas as Drake Maye’s main weapon against two-high coverage.

I’d expect similar usage or a full-on split between them, with a slight edge to Stevenson on passing downs. Among 46 qualifying rushers, Stevenson ranks 31st in explosive run rate, 22nd in yards after contact per attempt, and 38th in missed tackle rate. He hasn’t been his usual efficient self through four weeks. With him being less than 100%, I’m not expecting that to change this week. He has split the red zone rushing work evenly with Henderson, with each back getting two carries inside the 20-yard line. Stevenson should see double-digit opportunities this week as a flex play in a good matchup. Las Vegas has allowed the ninth-highest explosive run rate, the second-most yards before contact per attempt, and the tenth-highest rushing success rate.

**[Deshaun Watson (QB)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/deshaun-watson.php)**
[Deshaun Watson](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/deshaun-watson.php) is the QB13 in fantasy points per game with at least 19.7 fantasy points in two of his four starts. Watson has averaged 7.8 rushing attempts and 31.8 rushing yards to help pad his ceiling and floor weekly in fantasy. Watson continues to operate as a low-aDOT point guard. Among 29 qualifying passers, Watson has the 11th-most yards per attempt and the ninth-highest passing success rate, but he also has the lowest aDOT, the second-highest percentage of his passing yards arising from yards after the catch, and the tenth-highest turnover-worthy throw rate. Watson should have another serviceable QB2 outing this week against a pass defense that has given up the sixth-highest passing success rate, the 13th-most EPA per dropback, and has ranked 17th in CPOE.
**[Braelon Allen (RB)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/braelon-allen.php)**
I expect [Braelon Allen](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/braelon-allen.php) to draw another start this week for the Jets with [Breece Hall](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/breece-hall.php) sidelined. Last week, Allen only finished as the RB33 in weekly scoring with 16 touches and 67 total yards, but he operated as the Jets’ bell cow. Last week, Allen played 93.8% of the snaps with all of the rushing attempts and a 66.7% route share. Allen still hasn’t been impressive on a per-touch basis with only a 3.1% explosive run rate, 1.88 yards after contact per attempt, and a 15% missed tackle rate. When he’s seeing this type of volume and workload, it almost doesn’t matter, though. The Browns’ run defense will be more giving this week. Cleveland has allowed the second-highest explosive run rate, the sixth-most yards after contact per attempt, and the 12th-highest missed tackle rate. Allen should be a solid RB2 this week.
**[Quinshon Judkins (RB)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/quinshon-judkins.php)**
[Quinshon Judkins](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/quinshon-judkins.php) is the RB21 in fantasy points per game now after seeing a bump in the passing game last week and getting into the end zone. Judkins has averaged 18.6 touches and 68 total yards. Judkins has a 36% route share this season with a 13.9% target share. Last week, his route share was still in line with this usage with a 36.1% mark, but he had a 21.9% target share. I don’t expect that type of passing game usage to repeat, but I could easily be wrong. Judkins did see a 16.7% target share in Week 2, so maybe he does see somewhere in this area code of passing usage weekly moving forward. Judkins remains inefficient with his workload. Among 46 qualifying backs, he is 43rd in explosive run rate, 31st in yards after contact per attempt, and 21st in missed tackle rate. Much like with [Braelon Allen](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/braelon-allen.php), Judkins is seeing enough volume that it doesn’t matter most weeks, especially when he gets in the end zone. Judkins should have another solid RB2 week. The Jets have allowed the seventh-highest rushing success rate, the sixth-most yards before contact per attempt, and the third-highest missed tackle rate.
**[Denzel Boston (WR)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/denzel-boston.php)**
[Denzel Boston](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/denzel-boston.php) is the WR20 in fantasy points per game, while commanding an 18.3% target share with 2.39 YPRR, three red zone targets, seven deep targets, and a 22.5% first-read share (second on the team). Boston has excelled so far this season, ranking 17th in separation and YPRR while sitting at seventh in route win rate among 77 qualifying receivers. This week, he’ll tangle with a Jets’ secondary that has the fifth-highest two-high rate (62.2%). Against two-high, Boston leads the team with a 21% target-per-route run rate against two-high while logging 4.47 YPRR. This is a tougher matchup for Boston, but I think he can overcome it. The Jets have allowed the tenth-fewest yards per target to perimeter wide receivers while ranking 15th in fantasy points per opportunity allowed to the position.

**[Aaron Rodgers (QB)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/aaron-rodgers.php)**
[Aaron Rodgers](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/aaron-rodgers.php) has flashed signs of fantasy life over the last two weeks with QB8 and QB3 performances in weekly scoring. Rodgers has been doing his best [Jameis Winston](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/jameis-winston.php) impression since Week 3. Over the last two weeks, among 27 qualifying passers, Rodgers has been seventh in yards per attempt and has had the second-highest deep ball rate (third-highest aDOT), but he has also been 12th-worst in passing success rate, fifth-lowest in highly accurate throw rate, and the highest off-target rate. Essentially Rodgers has been saying every play, “well, ______ is down there somewhere…let it rip.” We’ll see how it works out for Jameis Rodgers this week, but the matchup is quite nice. Indy has allowed the fifth-most yards per attempt, the 11th-highest CPOE, and the ninth-most EPA per dropback.
**[Daniel Jones (QB)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/daniel-jones.php)**
[Daniel Jones](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/daniel-jones.php) continues to look like a statue in the pocket for the Colts as the QB36 in fantasy points per game. Jones hasn’t been able to move in the pocket as he looks like 2025 [Kirk Cousins](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/kirk-cousins.php). Among 29 qualifying passers, Jones has the second-fewest yards per attempt, the third-lowest aDOT, the lowest deep ball rate, and the ninth-highest turnover-worthy play rate. If Jones couldn’t look competent against the Commanders last week, I don’t know if he will during the 2026 season. After last week’s face plant, I have zero faith that Jones can take advantage of a nice matchup this week. Pittsburgh has allowed the sixth-most yards per attempt, the fifth-highest passing success rate, and the 12th-highest CPOE. Jones is a low-end QB2 again this week.
**[Jaylen Warren (RB)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/jaylen-warren.php)**
Jaylen Warren has been operating as Pittsburgh’s bell cow in Rico Dowdle’s absence. It sounds like Dowdle will be back this week. I have no clue how Pittsburgh will divvy up the workload between these two backs, but I lean toward Warren retaining the lead role at least for this week, with Dowdle likely being eased back in. Could it be a full-blown committee this week? It’s possible, which is why I can’t put Warren in the strong start category despite the fact that he’s awesome and should retain the clear lead role, if not bell cow usage, even with Dowdle back. In Week 1, Warren had only a 36.5% snap share with 47.6% of the rushing attempts and a 31% route share (14.6% target share). Warren was the RB25 for the week with 15 touches and 73 total yards. Among 46 qualifying backs, Warren is 14th in explosive run rate, sixth in yards after contact per attempt, and fourth-best in missed tackle rate. Indy has allowed the 11th-highest rushing success rate, the seventh-most yards before contact per attempt, and the seventh-highest missed tackle rate. Warren could easily be anywhere from a solid flex to an RB1 this week. There’s a wide range of outcomes here, as we have no clue about the division of labor for the backfield this week.
**[Keenan Allen (WR)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/keenan-allen.php)**
Keenan Allen missed last week’s game with a groin injury. This week, he had limited practices on Wednesday and Thursday before upgrading to a full practice on Friday. He has been listed as questionable. Allen is the WR44 in fantasy points per game with one top-24 weekly finish this season (WR20). Allen has a 20.4% target share with 1.33 YPRR, three red zone targets, zero deep targets, and a 26% first-read share. Daniel Jones’ level of play is a big limiting factor for the Indy passing attack, and I don’t see that changing this week. Despite that, Allen is a viable flex play this week as Pittsburgh has allowed the fourth-most yards per target and the sixth-most fantasy points per opportunity to perimeter wide receivers.
