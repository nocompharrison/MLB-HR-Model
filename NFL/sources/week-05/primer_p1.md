**Strong Starts**
_Here are players that should be in starting consideration for most fantasy teams._
**[Jalon Daniels (QB)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/jalon-daniels.php)**
[Jalon Daniels](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/jalon-daniels.php) was the QB21 last week on the strength of his eight carries for 55 yards. If he had scored a rushing touchdown, Daniels would have put up 19.4 fantasy points and finished as the QB11 for the week. The power of rushing production in fantasy can swing the day for quarterbacks even when their passing stats are mediocre. Daniels finished with only 148 passing yards, 4.9 yards per attempt, a 73.3% catchable target rate, and a 23.3% off-target rate (horrible). If Daniels can maintain this level of rushing production and marginally clean up the passing output, he could easily be a QB1 this week with a cakewalk matchup. Dallas has allowed the third-most yards per attempt, the highest passing success rate, and the fifth-highest CPOE.
**[Bucky Irving (RB)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/bucky-irving.php)**
Last week, [Bucky Irving](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/bucky-irving.php) maintained his lead back role with a 55.7% snap rate, 38.9% route share, 16 touches, and 61 total yards. He didn’t draw a target last week. The most worrisome part of his usage last week was that he split snaps with [Kenneth Gainwell](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/kenneth-gainwell.php) (25%) and [Sean Tucker](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/sean-tucker.php) (37.5%) in the red zone. Irving had a 37.5% snap rate inside the 20-yard line last week while logging two red zone rushing attempts, with Tucker finishing with three. This is a horrible outcome for Irving’s touchdown prospects weekly if it continues. Irving’s per-touch metrics have started to dip from a strong start, which tells me he likely isn’t 100% healthy. Among 45 qualifying backs, he is 29th in yards after contact per attempt and 28th in missed tackle rate. We’ll see if Irving can bounce back this week. He has a nice matchup in front of him. Dallas has allowed the fourth-highest explosive run rate, the ninth-highest rushing success rate, and the 14th-most yards after contact per attempt.
**[George Pickens (WR)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/george-pickens.php)**
Through four games, this has been a disappointing season for [George Pickens](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/george-pickens.php) as the WR39 in fantasy points per game. He has only one game with more than 15 PPR points. Last week, I thought we’d see the bounceback game for Pickens, but it didn’t happen. What I didn’t anticipate for Pickens was that Houston would absolutely abandon their single high ways for two-high (66% of their defensive snaps). We’re going back to the single-high well with Pickens this week against a Bucs defense that has utilized single-high at the third-highest rate (66.2%). Overall, Pickens has an 18% target share, 1.65 YPRR, two red zone targets, five deep targets, and a 20.2% first-read share. Against single-high this season, Pickens has seen his target per route run rate climb from 14% to 35% and his YPRR increase from 1.41 to 2.30. If the Bucs continue their single high ways, Pickens should shred them. Tampa Bay has allowed the 11th-most fantasy points per opportunity to perimeter wide receivers while ranking 15th in yards per target against the position.
**[Cade Otton (TE)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/cade-otton.php)**
Last week, in [Jalon Daniels](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/jalon-daniels.php) first start, [Cade Otton](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/cade-otton.php) led the team with a 28.6% first-read share while also logging a 22.2% target share (second on the team). Otton was only the TE24 for the week despite the strong usage. He finished with 37 receiving yards, 1.42 YPRR, and zero red zone and deep targets. If Otton sees this type of market share in Week 5, he’ll be a strong streaming option. Dallas has allowed the eighth-most yards per target and the third-most fantasy points per opportunity to tight ends. We have seen random big games from Otton in the past, and that could happen again this week.

Among 29 qualifying passers, he has the 13th-lowest yards per attempt, the third-lowest passing success rate, the sixth-lowest catchable target rate, and the 11th-best hero throw rate. The arm talent is there, but the situation is gruesome, and he has faced the 12th-highest pressure rate. Willis has the potential for a solid game this week (if he can pile up some rushing production) against a Cincy pass defense that has allowed the 12th-most yards per attempt, the eighth-highest passing success rate, and the 14th-highest passer rating.
**[Malik Washington (WR)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/malik-washington.php)**
[Malik Washington](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/malik-washington.php) is the WR45, but over the last two games he has been the WR39 and WR35 in weekly scoring with volume alone. If you toss a touchdown on top of either of those stat lines, Washington would have been a WR2 in weekly fantasy scoring. Washington has a 27.2% target share, 1.99 YPRR, one red zone target, six deep targets, and a 28.3% first-read share. Washington is a volume-fueled flex again this week. The matchup isn’t great, but I expect him to see a heavy target load, and if he finally gets in the endzone, he’ll pay off for fantasy this week. Cincy has allowed the seventh-fewest yards per target and the ninth-fewest fantasy points per opportunity to receivers.
