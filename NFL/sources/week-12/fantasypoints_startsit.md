# 2026 Week 12 Fantasy Football Start 'Em, Sit 'Em Advice
Joe DolanNOV. 25, 2026 4:30PM EST
Welcome to Week 12 of Fantasy Football Start ‘Em, Sit ‘Em!
I’m the new author of this column this year, so I’ll introduce a new format! I will break down every game by position and player. After listing the player (and perhaps some interesting stats or news I’ve found in my research), I’ll pop that player into one of several buckets, which are as follows:
  * **Crown Him** — This player is an every-week starter, someone you almost never take out of your lineup _(90-100% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Start Him** — More than likely, this player should be in your lineup because of his role, recent production, or matchup, but you might have to think about it just a little _(70-90% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Stream Him** — This player isn’t a weekly starter (at least not yet), but circumstances could lead to him being in your optimal lineup this week _(50-70% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Stash Him** — This player is worthy of being on your roster, but ideally I want to see more before putting him in a lineup _(25-50% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Sit Him** — This player should not be in your lineup because of lack of recent production, a difficult matchup, or injury _(10-25% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Drop Him** — This player shouldn’t be on your roster _(0-10% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_


For simplicity’s sake, this column will focus on a 12-team PPR league with a base lineup of **1 QB, 2 RB, 3 WR, 1 TE, and 1 W/R/T FLEX.** Obviously, different league sizes and formats will change recommendations… and that’s what our [Fantasy Points Front Office](https://www.fantasypoints.com/fphq/import), with its multiple start/sit tools, is for.

### **Green Bay at Los Angeles Rams**
> Wednesday, November 25, 2026, 8 PM Eastern
##### Quarterbacks
**Jordan Love** (GB) — Love will look to bounce back from his season-worst 11.8 fantasy performance in Week 10 after a bye-week refresh. He’ll face a Rams defense with one of the league's best secondaries, which allows the 7th-lowest FPG to opposing QBs (15.3). The Rams also play two-high safeties at the 5th-highest rate in the league (61.1%), which Love averages 0.51 FP/DB against (QB10). The over/under has dropped to 43.5 (opened at 49.5), but I don’t see a world where Love doesn’t attempt at least 30 passes in a game script that might be favorable, with the expectation that the Rams' run game torches the Packers' defensive line. _**[Start Him]**_
**Matthew Stafford** (LAR) — With no Puka Nacua or Davante Adams in Week 10, Stafford went 18-of-26 for 249 yards and 3 touchdowns (20.8 fantasy points). This came against a Cardinals defense that allows the 3rd-most FPG to opposing QBs — the Packers allow the 5th-fewest (15.2). The Packers DBs have also been playing at a surprisingly high level this season, and I think they can completely shut down the Rams' lackluster receiving corps. I expect this Rams offense to run the ball 10+ more times than they pass the ball in this matchup. _**[Sit Him]**_

**Tucker Kraft** (GB) — Kraft has been wildly disappointing this season, with the 2nd-most drops among TEs (5). He’s had brutal matchups in back-to-back games, leading to him logging 3.5 targets per game, 0.42 YPRR, and 2.4 FPG in that span. He’ll face a Rams defense that allows only 10.8 FPG to opposing TEs (27th). There are likely better streaming options available than Kraft this week. _**[Stash Him]**_
### **Chicago at Detroit**
> Thursday, November 26, 2026, 1 PM Eastern
##### Quarterbacks
**Caleb Williams** (Chi) — It’s probably worth filing away that two of Caleb’s three worst games by fantasy points this year came in breezy, wet conditions — 8.7 FP against Minnesota in Week 2 and 15.3 FP against New Orleans in Week 11. There will be no such issues inside Ford Field on Thanksgiving. The Lions have allowed the 2nd-most FPG to opposing QBs this year, and Caleb threw for 4 TD and 2 INT in two matchups with Detroit last year. The 55.5 projected total is the highest on the entire Week 12 slate. _**[Start Him]**_
**Jared Goff** (Det) — Goff takes his deep shots selectively — we define a “deep throw” as one with a depth of target of 20 yards or more. He has just an 8.4% such rate, bottom-10 among all QBs. But among QBs with 20 or more deep throws this year, Goff ranks behind only **Joe Burrow** and **Brock Purdy** in EPA/DB. The Bears are the worst team in the NFL by EPA/DB on deep passes. This might be a Goff stat, but I’m also certain it’s a **Jameson Williams** stat. _**[Start Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**Jahmyr Gibbs** (Det) — Has played just one game without scoring a touchdown this year. He still had 18.3 FP in that contest. _**[Crown Him]**_
**D’Andre Swift** (Chi) — Swift saw 61.1% of the Bears’ backfield XFP in Week 11, the first game with **Kyle Monangai** back in the fold since Week 4. Coincidentally, it tied Swift’s lowest XFP backfield XFP share since Week 5, his first game _without_ Monangai. It’s possible Swift will need to make his hay in the passing game this week. Over the last five games, the Lions have allowed the 6th-most FPG to opposing RBs in the passing game, and the 2nd-_fewest_ to them on the ground. Swift’s route share in Week 11 of 29.6% was his lowest since Monangai’s last game. Still, an RB1 in a high-scoring environment. _**[Start Him]**_
**Kyle Monangai** (Chi) — Monangai returned from a seven-week absence in Week 11, posting just 8/18 rushing and running 7 targetless routes. It was only the second time in any game this season — with Monangai healthy or not — that the Bears gave a carry or target to a _third_ running back, which they did with **Salvon Ahmed** , who saw a 16.6% backfield XFP share to Monangai’s season-low 22.4%. If this is _not_ just a one-week blip as he gets his feet under him, then Monangai is going to be hard-pressed to return the FLEX value it looked like he’d be consistently producing before he got hurt. It’s also a tough matchup, to boot — over the last five games, the Lions have allowed the 2nd-fewest FPG to RBs on the ground, and Monangai has just 5 catches all year. He’s a low-end RB3/FLEX. _**[Stash Him]**_
##### Wide Receivers
**Amon-Ra St. Brown** (Det) — Has gone over 100 receiving yards just twice this year, but has scored in back-to-back games, his first touchdowns since scoring 5 times over the first three games. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Luther Burden** (Chi) — Yikes. While an overall lack of passing-game volume hurt matters for Burden in Week 11, he ran a 59.3% route share against the Saints, which ranked 4th on the Bears and marked his 2nd-lowest rate of the season. His 8.7% target share was his lowest of the year, as well. I’m unsure why Burden’s route share was so low _and_ the Bears decided to start refocusing on **Kalif Raymond** , who saw 4 targets to Burden’s 3 despite the fact that Raymond has topped 20 receiving yards just once in his last seven games. Another factor here is **Zavion Thomas** , who has a small, part-time role but continues to make plays when called upon. The Lions were a slot funnel for much of this season, and Burden has been primarily a slot receiver, but his 31.3% slot route rate in Week 11 was his lowest of the year. Sometimes, I’m at a loss for words to describe what happened, and this counts as one of those times. Most likely, it’s just a blip on the radar — Burden was a top-10 weekly WR in each of his three games prior to the bye. Burden is a WR2. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Jameson Williams** (Det) — As I mentioned above in the **Jared Goff** blurb, the Bears are an extreme deep funnel. Only the Raiders (70.7) allow more yards per game on targets of 20 or more air yards, and Jamo’s aDOT of 15.61 is actually the highest of his career in a full season. The Bears were much worse in this department earlier in the season, but I think there will be enough opportunities for Jamo to take one to the house that I love him as a WR2 in a game with a projected 55.5-point total. _**[Start Him]**_
**Rome Odunze** (Chi) — The Bears just had a bad offensive game in Week 11, and Odunze finished the week as the WR40. Burden has exactly _one_ finish inside the top-20 PPR WRs this year, and it came in the Bears’ Week 9 win over the Bucs — the only time this year he’s caught a TD pass. He has exactly 4 catches in four straight games, and has seen 5 or more catches only once. The scoring environment should be strong here, but Odunze has given zero indication he has **Luther Burden** 's ceiling. _**[Stash Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**Colston Loveland** (Chi) — Loveland has finished as a top-10 TE in four of his last five games, and as a top-12 TE in five of his last seven after an impossibly slow start to the season. The Lions have allowed the 2nd-fewest FPG to opposing TEs over their last five games… and are _still_ allowing the most over the course of the whole season, which speaks to how awful they were in their first five games. The straight games with the Dolphins, Patriots (with **Tommy DeVito**), and the Bucs help. I’ll still trust Loveland to get it done here. An easy TE1. _**[Start Him]**_
**Sam LaPorta** (Det) — LaPorta had his first game with 40 or more receiving yards since Week 5 in Week 11 against the Bucs, but his 62 scoreless yards came on just 3 catches. Because so many teams were off in Week 11, it was enough to rank him as a top-10 weekly TE, his first such game since Week 5 as well. It’s overall a strong matchup, as the Bears have allowed the 12th-most FPG to opposing TEs over the last five games, but I think it’s a better draw for the Lions’ WRs. _**[Stream Him]**_
### **Philadelphia at Dallas**
> Thursday, November 26, 2026, 4:30 PM Eastern
##### Quarterbacks
**Dak Prescott** (Dal) — After ranking as a top-10 QB just once in his first six games, he’s now finished in the top _10_ in five consecutive weeks, including back-to-back weeks as the overall QB1. The Eagles will have a key player, as top CB **Quinyon Mitchell** (hamstring) got injured late in Week 12 but isn’t on the injury report. You’re starting Dak regardless. _**[Start Him]**_
**Joe Milton** (Phi) — Can Milton keep the streaming run going against Dallas’ defense? The Cowboys allowed a QB5 performance to **Cam Ward** last week despite **Mike Pettine** taking over the playcalling. One area where Milton could really fail — he has a ridiculous 41.7% pressure-to-sack ratio, which would easily rank as the worst in the league if he had enough dropbacks to qualify. Despite the Cowboys’ overall defensive woes, they’re 10th in the NFL in sacks (27) and are 5th in pressure rate (38.2%). The matchup remains juicy, and Milton’s skill-position group is relatively healthy, but it wouldn’t be shocking to see him take 5+ sacks here. He’s a viable streamer in a projected high-scoring environment, but the sacks and the Eagles’ constant second-half turtling give me pause. _**[Stash Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**Javonte Williams** (Dal) — Williams’ 82.0% backfield XFP share in Week 11 was his highest since Week 5. The Cowboys put **Tyler Goodson** on IR and have used **Israel Abanikanda** and **Jashaun Corbin** to spell him the last two weeks, but only just a bit. He’s a true bell cow. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Saquon Barkley** (Phi) — Barkley scored in his fourth straight game in Week 11, posting his first signature “long Saquon TD run” of the season against the Steelers. Unfortunately, he aggravated the shoulder injury he picked up in Week 2, and with a short week, he’ll likely be less than 100% to go against the Cowboys despite not being on the injury report. He saw every single Eagles carry before his injury, so if he does play, he’ll be a borderline bell cow, but there is an added risk. On the other hand, he played just 9 snaps against the Cowboys in Week 7 and still ranked RB20 that week. _**[Start Him]**_
##### Wide Receivers
**CeeDee Lamb** (Dal) — Lamb is on pace for 206 targets. **Rob Moore** holds the NFL record, with 209 in 1997 for the Cardinals (he caught just 97 of them — 46.4%). _**[Crown Him]**_
**George Pickens** (Dal) — Ranked as the WR18 against the Eagles in Week 7, and has been the WR1 and WR2 in his last two games, respectively. The Eagles’ top perimeter CB, **Quinyon Mitchell** , may be hobbled. Pickens’ ceiling is too high to sit him. _**[Crown Him]**_
**DeVonta Smith** (Phi) — Smith got wide open twice deep last week in his return from a hamstring injury, but **Joe Milton** overthrew him both times. Deep-ball accuracy is certainly something this Eagles’ offense misses without **Jalen Hurts**. If the Eagles had more trustworthy QB play, the matchup with Smith and his route prowess versus the lockdown defense of **Joey Porter Jr.** would be must-see — Porter has allowed 4 catches for 23 yards on 13 targets _all season_. Fortunately for Smith (and maybe for Porter?), Porter doesn’t travel. I still saw enough from Smith in his return — a ridiculous _55.6%_ first-read target share — that he’s on the WR2 radar. _**[Start Him]**_
**Darius Cooper** and **Makai Lemon** (Phi) — The Eagles played almost exclusively 11 personnel from the shotgun in Week 11. Cooper (95.8%) actually led the team in routes and was 2nd on the team behind Smith in 1st-read target share (22.2%). Lemon continues to struggle with drops, potentially because **Joe Milton** throws so damn hard. Cooper is an underrated player, but with Milton completing just 29 passes in his two starts, it’s hard to trust either of these two guys. _**[Sit Him]**_
**Ryan Flournoy** (Dal) — Flournoy ran just a 41.7% route share in Week 11, as the Cowboys ran 11 personnel 41.7% of the time against the Titans, their second-lowest rate of the season, as they posted their highest rate of 12 personnel (23.8%). I don’t know if they’ll do the same thing against the Eagles, who have some injuries in the secondary, but the usage was pretty staggering last week. It’s possible that the Cowboys were up so big for much of the day that they just decided to pound the rock with **Javonte Williams** and FB **Hunter Luebke**. Flournoy is a low-end WR3. _**[Stash Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**Dallas Goedert** (Phi) — Goedert had just 1 target against the Steelers in Week 11, but did turn it into a 38-yard catch. It is a bit concerning that his lowest target share of the season came in **DeVonta Smith** ’s return from injury, because **Joe Milton** has been erratic at best as a thrower — Goedert likely will need a TD to come through for fantasy at this stage. The Cowboys have been a mediocre matchup for TEs all year. _**[Stream Him]**_
### **Kansas City at Buffalo**

**Josh Allen** (Buf) — Pouring rain should be no problem for **Josh Allen** , who is the QB1 in rushing FPG (9.4). KC is a tough matchup, allowing just 13.1 FPG to opposing QBs, but the Chiefs play man at a top-five rate (29.0%) — Allen’s EPA per dropback climbs to +0.58 against man from +0.15 against zone, and he’s a sure-fire QB1. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Patrick Mahomes** (KC) — **Patrick Mahomes** averaged 26.0 FPG over his last three games, and he is now QB6 on the season with 20.7 FPG. Mahomes didn’t get the best seat at the Thanksgiving table this year: the Bills allow only 15.7 FPG and -0.02 EPA per dropback to opposing QBs, and the weather forecast looks ugly. But no QB has exceeded his expected FPG by a greater mark (+4.0) than Mahomes, and he’s a high-end QB1 despite the obstacles. _**[Start Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**Emmett Johnson** (KC) — Week 11 was another record-setter for **Emmett Johnson** , who tallied career highs in carries (23) and rushing yards (105). In two games with **Kenneth Walker** out, Johnson is averaging 29.7 FPG, and he leads all RBs with 100+ routes run at 2.76 YPRR. Buffalo allows 20.3% of targets to go to RBs, the fifth-highest rate in the league. With a close game, tough weather, and his recent surge, Johnson is a sure-fire RB1. _**[Start Him]**_
**James Cook** (Buf) — With **James Cook** averaging 18.9 FPG (RB6) and the Bills implied for 26.5 points as home favorites, he is a no-brainer start. But one mind-blowing note each way: Cook’s 31 carries in Week 9 are one of the 20 highest single-game RB marks since 2021, but his 1.65 yards after contact per carry this season is the lowest of any RB with 100+ carries in Data Suite history (2021-2026). _**[Start Him]**_
**Kenneth Walker** (KC) — **Kenneth Walker** will sadly sit out Week 12. Does KC still need Walker with Johnson playing out of his mind? Walker’s 88.2% share of Kansas City's inside-the-5 carries, the highest mark in the league, says yes. Walker will be a sight for sore eyes when he returns. _**[Sit Him]**_

**DJ Moore** (Buf) — **DJ Moore** set a season high in receptions (8) and tied his season high in targets (10) in Week 11, and he is on a mini-heater over his last three games, averaging 16.4 FPG on 7.0 targets while running 71.1% of routes. Moore also wins on 33.3% of his post routes, which is tied for sixth of 114 WRs to run over six post routes. Moore is a mid-range WR2 and solid start. _**[Start Him]**_
**Rashee Rice** (KC) — The production for **Rashee Rice** has been remarkably steady: 11.5 FPG over his last three games, 11.3 FPG on the season (WR29), and 11.3 PPR fantasy points in Week 11 — credit to Rice for staying in his lane and going one steady speed. Buffalo plays zone at a slightly above-average rate (76.2%), and Rice posts a 2.73 YPRR vs. zone (only 0.96 vs. man). Rice is a high-end WR2. _**[Start Him]**_
**Khalil Shakir** (Buf) — Topping 100 yards on nine targets in Week 11 brought **Khalil Shakir** up to a smooth 10.0 FPG (WR40) on the season. Shakir has the highest YAC per reception (9.6) among all WRs with 20+ catches, and his numbers have picked up over the last three games: Shakir is averaging 10.9 FPG while running 69.1% of routes and seeing 5.0 targets per game. But the Chiefs allow a TD on only 13.1% of drives (league-best) and 24.4 FPG to opposing WRs (second-lowest). Shakir is startable in some formats, but he is a low-end WR3. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Keon Coleman** (Buf) — The target share for **Keon Coleman** has risen from 12.7% to 14.1% over the last three games, but his expected fantasy points per game (XFP) have gone from 8.5 to 4.5. Can Coleman get vengeance against a Chiefs team that traded up to draft **Xavier Worthy** instead of him? Coleman does post 2.60 YPRR vs. man, and the Chiefs run man at a top-five rate, but Coleman is still a low-end WR4 any week that Moore is healthy. _**[Stash Him]**_
**Josh Downs** (KC) — After dazzling on a limited route share in his Week 10 Chiefs debut, **Josh Downs** remained highly efficient in his second outing by turning five targets into four catches for 59 yards. The Bills are also a top-five defense vs. slot receivers, and Downs’ YPRR falls to 1.31 against zone coverage, which Buffalo plays 76.2% of the time. Downs remains a high-end WR3, but temper expectations. _**[Start Him]**_

### **Denver at Pittsburgh**
> Friday, November 27, 2026, 3 PM Eastern
##### Quarterbacks
**Bo Nix** (Den) — Nix looked to be turning the season around after a 26.2 fantasy-point performance in Week 7, but then followed it with declining outings of 19.3, 16.7, and 12.5 points. And we’ve seen no rushing upside from Nix this season, outside of the Week 7 game against the Cardinals — he’s had 10 or fewer rushing yards in every other game. He’ll face a Steelers defense allowing the 5th-fewest FPG to opposing QBs (14.7). _**[Sit Him]**_
**Mason Rudolph** (Pit) — In an absolutely shocking turn of events, the Steelers have benched Aaron Rodgers in his final season to start Rudolph instead. The same Rudolph with career averages of 123.4 passing YPG, an 85.6 passer rating, and 8.0 FPG. This decision downgrades every player in this offense. _**[Drop Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**Jonah Coleman** (Den) — In his 3 games as the Broncos' starting RB, Coleman has earned an 85.6% backfield XFP share and 20.4 XFP/G, for 24.7 FPG. None of his efficiency metrics stand out, but he’s certainly passed the eye test right off the bat. He’s also averaging 4.0 targets per game and 1.90 YPRR. He’s seeing a true dual-threat bellcow workload and faces a Steelers defense that has allowed the most receiving FPG to opposing RBs in the league (11.9). _**[Crown Him]**_
**Jaylen Warren** (Pit) — When playing with a lead, Warren averages 0.56 fantasy points per snap, compared to 0.23 when playing from behind. The Steelers have gotten off to slow starts in their previous two matchups, leading to disappointing numbers for Warren (9.1 and 4.9 fantasy points). Maybe the Steelers will finally realize that feeding the ball to Warren helps them win football games. The Broncos defense allows the 8th most FPG to opposing RBs this season (24.4), but how often will the Steelers be leading in this game with Rudolph at QB? _**[Start Him]**_
**Rico Dowdle** (Pit) — Over his last four games, Dowdle has cut in for a 39.2% backfield XFP share (9.1 XFP/G), averaging 9.8 FPG. Until last week, he was extremely efficient with those touches, making a solid argument that he deserved the backfield share he was getting. He’s seen only 1 rush attempt inside the 5-yard line in that span (to Warren’s 2), but is earning nearly the same targets per game (3.3 compared to 3.5 for Warren). The Steelers clearly want Dowdle involved in this offense, but it could look awful with Rudolph at QB. _**[Stash Him]**_
##### Wide Receivers
**Jaylen Waddle** (Den) — We saw signs of life from Waddle in Week 11: 6 targets, 2.26 YPRR, and 15.5 XFP. **Jaxon Smith-Njigba** (Sea) — Smith-Njigba is averaging 19.2 FPG over his last three games against the 49ers, including a 22.8-point performance in Week 5. All three of those games have been with Sam Darnold at QB. What else is there to say? Smith-Njigba is the best receiver in the league with a 45+ point fantasy ceiling on any given week, going up against arguably the best team in the NFL right now. Should be a fun one. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Mike Evans** (SF) — I salivate at what Evans could achieve if his body could handle a full route share this season. He ranks 2nd in YPRR (4.04), 1st in 1D/RR (0.193), 1st in XFP/RR (0.75), and 2nd in FP/RR (0.84). Guess where Jaxon Smith-Njigba ranks in each of those efficiency metrics? 1st, 2nd, 2nd, and 1st. This should be one hell of a matchup, but the Seahawks do allow the fewest FPG to opposing WRs this season (21.9). Still, Evans touched this defense for 26.3 fantasy points back in Week (his best game this season). _**[Crown Him]**_
**Keenan Allen** (SF) — Allen saw roughly the same route share in each of his first two games on the 49ers (60.0% and 64.3%), but his target share dropped from 25.0% in Week 10 to 14.8% in Week 11 with Mike Evans returning to the lineup. Allen is a nice safety blanket receiver, but he doesn’t offer much upside in terms of explosive plays or touchdown potential. _**[Stash Him]**_
**Deebo Samuel** (SF) — Samuel hasn’t exceeded an 11.1% target share in either of his two games after returning from injury and has only totaled 61 yards in the air in that span. He’s done most of his damage on the ground, with 5 rush attempts for 105 yards and a touchdown. Samuel still has explosive upside in open space with a full head of steam, but he’s a volatile start, and he logged just 3.5 fantasy points against the Seahawks in Week 5.
**Cooper Kupp** (Sea) — Kupp is averaging just 6.8 FPG on 7.6 XFP/G this season and has only eclipsed 12.0 fantasy points once. He totaled just 3.5 fantasy points against the 49ers back in Week 5. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**George Kittle** (SF) — Thanks to Scott Barrett, we knew that George Kittle would be a smash last week against the blitz-heavy Vikings defense. He ran only 16 routes in Week 11, but he made them count: 3 receptions for 34 yards, two TDs, and 18.4 fantasy points on 6.8 XFP. The Seahawks only blitz 26.9% of the time (23rd) and allow the 9th-fewest FPG to opposing TEs (10.9). Kittle is still one of the very matchup-proof TEs in the league. _**[Crown Him]**_
**AJ Barner** (Sea) — Barner is as about as TD-dependent a TE as it gets. He’s only exceeded 3 receptions or 20 receiving yards in two games this season. The 49ers have only allowed 3 TE TDs all season. _**[Sit Him]**_
### **Washington at Arizona**
> Sunday, November 29, 2026, 4:25 PM Eastern
##### Quarterbacks
**Jayden Daniels** (Was) — Though he didn’t throw a touchdown against the Bengals in Week 11, Daniels played an excellent game and made several big-time throws late to set up an upset win. And because he added 55 yards on the ground, he still ranked QB12 for the week, even without accounting for a score. The Commanders are touchdown favorites this week in a great matchup — the Cardinals have allowed the league’s 3rd-most FPG to opposing QBs, and the 2nd-most schedule-adjusted FPG to QBs as runners over the last five weeks. Daniels has an underrated receiving corps and a fantastic matchup, so he’s a slam-dunk QB1. _**[Start Him]**_
**Josh Johnson** (Ari) — Threw for 155 yards and a TD, with just 5 rushing yards, against the Chiefs last week. That was far more than you could reasonably expect from a 40-year-old Johnson on his 15th (!) team. _**[Drop Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**Jacory Croskey-Merritt** (Was) — Bill has played three full games with **Jayden Daniels** this year. He has finished as the RB22, RB18, and RB12 in those games, in that order. His 66.1% backfield XFP share in Week 12 was the highest of any Commanders’ RB this season, as **Rachaad White** played a limited role through a heel injury (0 touches) and **Austin Ekeler** mixed in a bit. With the Commanders 7-point favorites, I don’t really see a reason for their approach to change this week. I would anticipate “Bill” to have close to a 60% designed carry share again, and he’ll probably function as the goal-line back. He’s a low-end RB2 because of his lack of work in the passing game. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Tyler Allgeier** (Ari) — After crushing the Cowboys’ pathetic defense en route to NFC Offensive Player of the Week honors in Week 8, Allgeier has totaled 29 carries for 83 yards and 2 catches for no yards. That’s 10.3 FP… he had 36.1 in Week 8 alone. In Week 11, he fell behind **Bam Knight** in backfield XFP. He’s been a top-24 fantasy RB exactly once this year, when he finished as the overall RB1 in Week 8. _**[Sit Him]**_
**Austin Ekeler** (Was) — I maintain that Ekeler is toast, but with **Rachaad White** playing through a heel injury, Ekeler got all of the touches behind **Jacory Croskey-Merritt** in Week 11’s win over the Bengals. Ekeler has had over a 30% route share in four consecutive games, but the Commanders might not need to throw much as touchdown favorites in this game. On top of that, could White make this an ugly three-headed backfield if he’s feeling healthier this week? White’s 16.7% route share in Week 11 was a season low. I’d have to be absolutely dying to consider playing Ekeler this week. _**[Sit Him]**_
**Bam Knight** (Ari) — Knight led the Cardinals’ backfield in XFP share in Week 11, the first time he accomplished that since Week 7. It was still only enough to rank as the RB34 in fantasy for the week. He’s reached double-digit fantasy points exactly once this year, and the Cardinals are touchdown underdogs. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Wide Receivers
**Stefon Diggs** (Was) — In the Commanders’ five games without **Terry McLaurin** this season, Diggs slightly leads the Commanders in targets (35) over **Antonio Williams** (32) and **Chig Okonkwo** (27). But Diggs also hasn’t led the Commanders in route share since Week 4, their first game without McLaurin, and even that week, he was tied with Williams. It’s been working well — he’s 16th in targets per route run and 28th in separation score, but his performances for fantasy have been generally underwhelming. In the five games without McLaurin, he’s finished as a top-24 weekly WR just twice. He’s a much better NFL player than fantasy asset at this point. To boot, he’s an exceptional man-coverage beater, but the Cardinals run man at the NFL’s 3rd-lowest rate. He’s a low-end WR2. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Antonio Williams** (Was) — Williams’ 40.9% 1st-read target share in Week 11’s win over the Chiefs — in which he made two huge plays to set up the game-winning field goal — was the highest mark by any Commanders WR in any game this year, surpassing **Terry McLaurin** ’s previous season-high in Week 3 (39.1%). Moreover, he’s led the Commanders’ WRs in route share in every game McLaurin has missed this year. 15th in separation score and 16th in win rate, I wonder if the Williams breakout is officially here. The Commanders might not have to throw much, but Williams is an exciting WR3 option. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Harrison Wallace** (Ari) — Wallace ran a full-time (75%) route share in Week 11, and his 9.7 FP and 11.1 XFP ranked behind only **Trey McBride** in this receiving corps. He was the first read on 22.7% of **Josh Johnson** ’s targets, the only Cardinal above 10% outside of McBride. You’re probably not starting anyone here not named McBride, but if I had to, Wallace would be the guy, with the Cards likely to play from behind all day. _**[Stash Him]**_
**Treylon Burks** (Was) — Burks is separating extremely well — he is #1 among _all WRs_ in route win rate — but it hasn’t translated to fantasy production. He’s finished just twice as a usable WR3, and each time ranked _exactly_ as the week’s #36 PPR WR. The Commanders are much more committed to **Antonio Williams** at this stage. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**Trey McBride** (Ari) — McBride’s massive ceiling is gone with **Jacoby Brissett** and **Gardner Minshew** , but in **Josh Johnson** ’s first start as a Cardinal, he had a _massive_ 50.0% first-read target share and ranked as the TE4 for the week. _**[Start Him]**_
**Chig Okonkwo** (Was) — Chig has finished as a top-12 TE in three of his last five games — all since **Jayden Daniels** returned to the lineup — despite not scoring a single touchdown all year. He’s clearly third on the Commanders’ food chain behind **Stefon Diggs** and **Antonio Williams**. A viable TE streamer in a good matchup — Arizona allows the fourth-most schedule-adjusted FPG and the most FP/opportunity to TEs. _**[Stream Him]**_
