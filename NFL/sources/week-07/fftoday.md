**Favorites:** RB [Rhamondre Stevenson](https://www.fftoday.com/stats/players/17716/Rhamondre_Stevenson)
I wouldn't read too much into Corey Kiner logging 11 carries in Week 6. The Pats knew they had a game Thursday night and didn't want to overload Stevenson or TreVeyon Henderson, who suffered an ankle injury the week prior and then lost a fumble during the game. Stevenson will likely shoulder the load in Chicago. During the team's three-game winning streak, the bruising back has averaged 65 yards and scored twice. While Chicago has done a nice job of containing the run, much of that has to do with being able to keep the other team's offense on the sidelines. If New England's defense can make some stops, I look for Stevenson to approach 20 touches on TNF. He could be used as a low-end RB2 or strong flex.
**On the Fence:** WR [Demario Douglas](https://www.fftoday.com/stats/players/18506/Demario_Douglas)
With both Romeo Doubs (shoulder) and Mack Hollins (calf) on IR, Douglas looks to fill in as the No. 2 option behind Brown. Douglas has been tremendous the past two weeks, hauling in 11 passes for 151 yards and three touchdowns. Prior to that, however, he managed a combined 9-82-0 line over the first four games of the season. He's trended toward being more of a possession receiver in his career as well, whereas the Bears have been susceptible to downfield attacks, most recently getting lit up by the Packers in Week 5. Douglas has done enough lately to offer WR3 or flex value but given his early-season struggles, he's far from a sure thing.
**Fades:** RB [TreVeyon Henderson](https://www.fftoday.com/stats/players/19389/TreVeyon_Henderson)
Henderson turned eight touches into 40 yards in Week 6 but vanished after he lost a fumble against the Jets. It was a testament to his toughness that he was even out there, though, after he sustained an ankle injury the previous week that was expected to put him on the shelf. It's the ankle that gives me pause here as the Ohio State product is looking to bounce back on short rest. There's always a puncher's chance at value with Henderson, who is the designated big-play option in New England's backfield, but I wouldn't roll the dice on him here as more than a flex.

**On the Fence:** QB [Caleb Williams](https://www.fftoday.com/stats/players/19147/Caleb_Williams)  
  
Williams made his return from a three-game absence last Sunday and put up good numbers: 30-for-36, 305 yards, 1 TD. He did minimal running, however, and most of his passes were near or at the line of scrimmage -- his average depth of target (ADOT) was 3.3 yards, the second-lowest figure from a quarterback this season. Was that purely a tactical move or a concession to lingering concerns about Williams' hamstring? We should find out more on Thursday night against a tough Patriots defense that has allowed the sixth-fewest fantasy points to QBs this season. They've also yielded less than 10 rushing yards per game to the position. This is a tough matchup for Williams. Proceed with caution.
**Fades:** TE [Colston Loveland](https://www.fftoday.com/stats/players/19428/Colston_Loveland)
In Week 6, Loveland finally posted a stat line that resembled what fantasy owners were expecting when the season began: 65 yards and a score on six catches. While I recognize the impulse to get him in your lineup based on that performance and the early draft pick you doubtless invested on him, you should pump the brakes. New England is one of the toughest teams against tight ends this year. Through six games, the Pats are giving up just 4.8 catches and 46.5 yards per game with one total touchdown. It might be back on the struggle bus for Loveland in Week 7.

**Favorites:** WR [Germie Bernard](https://www.fftoday.com/stats/players/19806/Germie_Bernard)
Bernard's 11 catches last week were unusual but his involvement isn't.
He has now seen 21 targets over the last two games. Against Tampa Bay, he ran a route on nearly every Aaron Rodgers dropback and Pittsburgh repeatedly manufactured easy touches for him underneath.
Some of that usage was almost comically short, but fantasy points still count when the ball travels two yards.
Michael Pittman Jr. and Roman Wilson remain on injured reserve, leaving Pittsburgh's passing game concentrated around Metcalf and Bernard. The Steelers have also been one of the more pass-heavy offenses relative to game situation this season.
New Orleans has struggled against the pass, and Pittsburgh should have opportunities to move the ball in Paris. The international setting introduces some uncertainty, but Bernard's high-percentage targets make his role less dependent on explosive plays.
Bernard has moved beyond waiver-wire stash territory. I'm starting him as a PPR WR2/WR3.

The two-touchdown game in Week 5 made Freiermuth look more secure than his role actually is. He followed it with three targets against Tampa Bay while Darnell Washington drew six. 
Freiermuth still ran more routes, but he hasn't reached 50 receiving yards in a game this season. That makes the touchdowns responsible for too much of his fantasy value.
Pittsburgh should move the ball against New Orleans, so another red-zone opportunity is absolutely possible. I'm just not willing to treat that as a weekly plan when Bernard and Metcalf are dominating the receiver targets and Washington remains involved at tight end.
Freiermuth is a touchdown-dependent TE2 rather than a player I'm forcing into a lineup after the two-score game.

Miller handled 20 of New Orleans' 33 running back carries against the Giants, but he ran only four routes. Alvin Kamara and C.J. **Fades:** RB [Alvin Kamara](https://www.fftoday.com/stats/players/15924/Alvin_Kamara)
Kamara caught a 31-yard touchdown last week, which made the box score look a lot better than the role.
He played only 26% of New Orleans' offensive snaps, carried seven times and ran eight routes. Miller started and handled most of the early-down rushing, while Donaldson remained part of the passing-down rotation.
Kamara is also still managing the back injury and remained limited Wednesday and Thursday.
He can absolutely make one reception matter, as we saw against the Giants. That's a difficult way to live in fantasy when the overall workload has fallen this far.
Kamara is a risky PPR Flex for me now, not an automatic RB2 because of the name on the roster.

This week he faces a Jets defense that has held all but one running back they've faced to fewer than 60 yards on the ground and has only given up four running back rushing touchdowns so far this season. New York has an elite pass defense which should theoretically set up Gordon for a nice workload, but the problem lies with the Miami offense itself. Malik Willis has been predictably awful as Miami's starter and he now steps into an absolute nightmare of a matchup against a Jets defense that has completely suffocated opposing passing games this season. The game script offers little potential, either, as both teams are among the slowest pace-of-play offenses in the league, which severely limits the potential for a shootout. Miami has a pathetic implied team total of just 17 points.
Willis can occasionally break off a big run or get into the end zone with his legs, so it's not that he has no possibility of delivering a usable fantasy performance, it's just that his scrambling floor is nowhere near enough to offset his passing deficiencies. He's not a streaming option this week.

The overall offensive environment also limits his scoring potential, as the Jets consistently fail to score points. A low-volume tight end in an offense that fails to finish their drives in the end zone is a terrible fantasy wager. The matchup might look good on paper, but keep Sadiq on your bench.

Fannin already needed better target volume before drawing one of the least attractive tight end matchups on the slate. A full complement of Browns receivers doesn't make finding additional targets any easier.
He's a touchdown-dependent TE2 for me this week.
