Patriots @ Bears _- (Green)_  
**Line:** CHI -3.0  
**Total:** 50.5  
  
![](https://www.fftoday.com/common/logo_ne_50.gif) CHI FPts Allowed vs. QBs: No.16  
CHI FPts Allowed vs. RBs: No.28  
CHI FPts Allowed vs. WRs: No.14  
CHI FPts Allowed vs. TEs: No.24 **No Brainers:** QB [Drake Maye](https://www.fftoday.com/stats/players/19046/Drake_Maye), WR [A.J. Brown](https://www.fftoday.com/stats/players/16527/A.J._Brown) **Favorites:** RB [Rhamondre Stevenson](https://www.fftoday.com/stats/players/17716/Rhamondre_Stevenson) I wouldn't read too much into Corey Kiner logging 11 carries in Week 6. The Pats knew they had a game Thursday night and didn't want to overload Stevenson or TreVeyon Henderson, who suffered an ankle injury the week prior and then lost a fumble during the game. Stevenson will likely shoulder the load in Chicago. During the team's three-game winning streak, the bruising back has averaged 65 yards and scored twice. While Chicago has done a nice job of containing the run, much of that has to do with being able to keep the other team's offense on the sidelines. If New England's defense can make some stops, I look for Stevenson to approach 20 touches on TNF. He could be used as a low-end RB2 or strong flex. **On the Fence:** WR [Demario Douglas](https://www.fftoday.com/stats/players/18506/Demario_Douglas) With both Romeo Doubs (shoulder) and Mack Hollins (calf) on IR, Douglas looks to fill in as the No. 2 option behind Brown. Douglas has been tremendous the past two weeks, hauling in 11 passes for 151 yards and three touchdowns. Prior to that, however, he managed a combined 9-82-0 line over the first four games of the season. He's trended toward being more of a possession receiver in his career as well, whereas the Bears have been susceptible to downfield attacks, most recently getting lit up by the Packers in Week 5. Douglas has done enough lately to offer WR3 or flex value but given his early-season struggles, he's far from a sure thing. **Fades:** RB [TreVeyon Henderson](https://www.fftoday.com/stats/players/19389/TreVeyon_Henderson) Henderson turned eight touches into 40 yards in Week 6 but vanished after he lost a fumble against the Jets. It was a testament to his toughness that he was even out there, though, after he sustained an ankle injury the previous week that was expected to put him on the shelf. It's the ankle that gives me pause here as the Ohio State product is looking to bounce back on short rest. There's always a puncher's chance at value with Henderson, who is the designated big-play option in New England's backfield, but I wouldn't roll the dice on him here as more than a flex. ![](https://www.fftoday.com/common/logo_chi_50.gif) NE FPts Allowed vs. QBs: No.24  
NE FPts Allowed vs. RBs: No.20  
NE FPts Allowed vs. WRs: No.16  
NE FPts Allowed vs. TEs: No.22 **No Brainers:** RB [D'Andre Swift](https://www.fftoday.com/stats/players/17220/D%27Andre_Swift), WR [Luther Burden III](https://www.fftoday.com/stats/players/19331/Luther_Burden%20III) **Favorites:** N/A  
  
**On the Fence:** QB [Caleb Williams](https://www.fftoday.com/stats/players/19147/Caleb_Williams)  
  
Williams made his return from a three-game absence last Sunday and put up good numbers: 30-for-36, 305 yards, 1 TD. He did minimal running, however, and most of his passes were near or at the line of scrimmage -- his average depth of target (ADOT) was 3.3 yards, the second-lowest figure from a quarterback this season. Was that purely a tactical move or a concession to lingering concerns about Williams' hamstring? We should find out more on Thursday night against a tough Patriots defense that has allowed the sixth-fewest fantasy points to QBs this season. They've also yielded less than 10 rushing yards per game to the position. This is a tough matchup for Williams. Proceed with caution. **Fades:** TE [Colston Loveland](https://www.fftoday.com/stats/players/19428/Colston_Loveland) In Week 6, Loveland finally posted a stat line that resembled what fantasy owners were expecting when the season began: 65 yards and a score on six catches. While I recognize the impulse to get him in your lineup based on that performance and the early draft pick you doubtless invested on him, you should pump the brakes. New England is one of the toughest teams against tight ends this year. Through six games, the Pats are giving up just 4.8 catches and 46.5 yards per game with one total touchdown. It might be back on the struggle bus for Loveland in Week 7. **Prediction:** Patriots 27, Bears 23 [^ Top](https://www.fftoday.com/articles/ff/26-ff-wk7.html#top)
