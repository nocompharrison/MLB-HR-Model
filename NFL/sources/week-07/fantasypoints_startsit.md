# 2026 Week 7 Fantasy Football Start 'Em, Sit 'Em Advice
Joe DolanOCT. 23, 2026 8:07AM EDT
Welcome to Week 7 of Fantasy Football Start ‘Em, Sit ‘Em!
I’m the new author of this column this year, so I’ll introduce a new format! I will break down every game by position and player. After listing the player (and perhaps some interesting stats or news I’ve found in my research), I’ll pop that player into one of several buckets, which are as follows:
  * **Crown Him** — This player is an every-week starter, someone you almost never take out of your lineup _(90-100% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Start Him** — More than likely, this player should be in your lineup because of his role, recent production, or matchup, but you might have to think about it just a little _(70-90% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Stream Him** — This player isn’t a weekly starter (at least not yet), but circumstances could lead to him being in your optimal lineup this week _(50-70% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Stash Him** — This player is worthy of being on your roster, but ideally I want to see more before putting him in a lineup _(25-50% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Sit Him** — This player should not be in your lineup because of lack of recent production, a difficult matchup, or injury _(10-25% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Drop Him** — This player shouldn’t be on your roster _(0-10% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_


For simplicity’s sake, this column will focus on a 12-team PPR league with a base lineup of **1 QB, 2 RB, 3 WR, 1 TE, and 1 W/R/T FLEX.** Obviously, different league sizes and formats will change recommendations… and that’s what our [Fantasy Points Front Office](https://www.fantasypoints.com/fphq/import), with its multiple start/sit tools, is for.

### **New England at Chicago**
> Thursday, October 22, 2026, 8:15 PM Eastern
##### Quarterbacks
**Drake Maye** (NE) — It certainly appears Maye’s three-game swoon to begin the year was a blip. Over the last three games, he’s 1st among all QBs in XFP and EPA/DB and 2nd in FPG and YPA. That’s _with_ **AJ Brown** actively sabotaging him in Week 6. Maye has been especially effective against man coverage, which the Bears deploy at the NFL’s 4th-highest rate. _**[Start Him]**_
**Caleb Williams** (Chi) — The Bears had an interesting approach with Caleb in Week 6’s return from injury — his 3.13 aDOT was tied for the second-shortest of his career. He was also incredibly efficient, with an 86.1% catchable throw rate. I do wonder if the gameplan was to specifically deny Caleb opportunities to extend plays and break the pocket to protect his hamstring — he had just 1 scramble for 2 yards. His level of play this year makes him hard to sit, but the potential lack of rushing upside and the matchup (Patriots allow 6th-fewest FPG to QBs) makes him a slight downgrade to a low-end QB1. _**[Stream Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**D’Andre Swift** (Chi) — Whether it’s because Swift is banged up _or_ because they don’t want him to get banged up, the Bears seem committed to a backfield rotation even with **Kyle Monangai** (toe) out. Swift still takes the calorie-rich touches, especially when Monangai isn’t in the lineup. This is a neutral matchup with the Bears slightly favored, so Swift is a must-start. _**[Start Him]**_
**Rhamondre Stevenson** (NE) — The Pats appear to have a full-blown three-man committee going. Stevenson’s 40.3% backfield XFP share in Week 6 was his 2nd-lowest of the season despite **TreVeyon Henderson** fumbling, and he didn’t see a single target for the first time all year. His route share is still 11th among all RBs, however — it appears if you want to use Stevenson, you had better hope the Pats are at least in a competitive game, because **Corey Kiner** looks like their closer. The Bears allow the 3rd-fewest FPG to opposing RBs, but the Patriots are 3-point underdogs, which sets up a Rhamondre game. He’s a low-end RB2. _**[Stream Him]**_
**TreVeyon Henderson** (NE) — Somehow played through a high-ankle sprain last week, but lost a fumble, and now he’s sandwiched in this backfield — **Rhamondre Stevenson** runs significantly more routes than he does, and **Corey Kiner** appears to be the closer (while being a more explosive runner). Henderson’s 6.01 XFPG are 42nd of 45 qualified RBs this year. He’s an empty-calorie asset. _**[Sit Him]**_
**Corey Kiner** (NE) — Surprisingly got a lot of run in Week 6 after fumbling in Week 5, and it doesn’t appear his usage was fluky, given **Mike Vrabel** ’s comments. (See below.) He carried 8 times for 41 yards to close the game out in the fourth quarter last week — all 11 of his carries came in the final frame with the Pats leading. Tough to start given the Pats are underdogs this week, but hard not to add in deeper formats. _**[Sit Him]**_
**Kyle Monangai** (Chi) — Questionable to play on Thursday night with his thumb/toe injuries. The Bears didn’t put him on IR, but this would be his third consecutive game missed. If he doesn’t play, **Roschon Johnson** is once again on the FLEX radar — Johnson has backfield XFP shares of 38.9% and 37.9% in the last two games. Monitor his status, but I’m not expecting him to go. _**[Stash Him]**_
##### Wide Receivers
**AJ Brown** (NE) — We charted Brown with a ridiculous 4 drops on his 9 targets in Week 6. That took him from 22.05 XFP to just 5.70. _Yikes_. People are obviously going to worry that he hasn’t played truly dominant football since 2024, but the Pollyanna case is that Brown’s 42.9% 1st-read target share was the first time all year any Pats receiver was above even _30%_ , and now **Romeo Doubs** is on IR. The Bears allow the 10th-most FPG to opposing outside WRs. A strong WR2. _**[Start Him]**_
**Luther Burden** (Chi) — The Bears played a ton of 13 personnel against the Falcons last week, which might have been gameplan-specific. Burden ran a season-low 17 routes on a 41.5% route share… but was targeted on an absurd _8_ of those routes, catching them all for 95 yards. He also tied his season-high with 2 designed targets. Burden will be frustrating given his inconsistent usage, but **Caleb Williams** throws him the ball when he’s out there. A WR2. _**[Start Him]**_
**Rome Odunze** (Chi) — Odunze was the Bears’ WR who stayed on the field the most despite their season-high 13 personnel usage, running a team-high 78% route share. But he caught just 3 passes for 39 yards as **Ben Johnson** ’s focus was on getting the ball out of **Caleb Williams** ’ hands quickly. I also worry that Odunze will catch a **Christian Gonzalez** shadow — Gonzalez followed **Garrett Wilson** around last week and allowed just 2 catches for 20 yards in his primary coverage. I’m downgrading Odunze to a low-end WR3. _**[Stash Him]**_
**DeMario Douglas** (NE) — His 74.3% route share in Week 6 (when **Romeo Doubs** got injured) was his highest since Week 1, when another Patriot WR got injured (**AJ Brown**). Douglas has now scored in back-to-back games, with at least 65 yards in each. If I'm playing another Patriot WR on Thursday night, it’s him. He’s a low-end WR3. _**[Stash Him]**_
**Kalif Raymond** (Chi) — Ran a season-low 46.3% route share in Week 6 and failed to clear even 5.0 FP for the third time in a row. It might have been gameplan-specific to use a lot of 13 personnel against the Falcons’ quick-to-home pass rush, but it’s still hard to trust Raymond right now. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**Colston Loveland** (Chi) — One question I asked myself last week was, “What if we haven’t seen what this Bears offense was supposed to look like, given **Caleb Williams** got hurt in Week 2 in a monsoon?” So I think it’s interesting that Loveland had his best game of the year in Week 6 with Caleb back — He posted 18.5 FP on 21.2 XFP as the Bears ran a season-high 21 snaps of 13 personnel. **Ryan Heath** ’s research this off-season suggested that Loveland absolutely broke fantasy in 13 personnel last year. My brain is a little more open to Loveland being an every-week fantasy starter now than it was two weeks ago. The Patriots are a tough matchup, however — 7th-fewest FPG to opposing TEs. A low-end TE1. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Hunter Henry** (NE) — Has scored in back-to-back games. During **Drake Maye** ’s three-game hot streak, Henry ranks 10th in FPG and 11th in XFPG among TEs. The Bears have allowed the 8th-fewest FPG to opposing TEs, but Henry is one of Maye’s boys and should keep absorbing looks with **Romeo Doubs** on IR. _**[Stream Him]**_

A low-end QB1. _**[Stream Him]**_

Warren is probably going to need more than the 4 targets he saw in Week 6 to get back to consistent RB1 production. Maybe he gets more targets with New Orleans an underdog, but I wouldn’t count on it. A desperation FLEX. _**[Sit Him]**_
**Rico Dowdle** (Pit) — The Steelers are a top-5 team in PROE, which is going to make Dowdle a tough fantasy start most weeks, given he’s the #2 to **Jaylen Warren**. But he’s a better receiver than you might think, and he was more involved in Week 6 than Warren managers would have preferred — his 36.8% backfield XFP share was down just slightly from Weeks 1 and 2. **Chris Olave** (NO) — Has merely 13 targets over the last two games as **Tyler Shough** ’s dropbacks have plummeted, but one of those games was against the Vikings. He kills zone coverage, which the Steelers run at the NFL’s 8th-highest rate. _**[Crown Him]**_

The Steelers are one of the NFL’s pass-heaviest teams, and Metcalf is benefiting — he’s run more routes than any receiver in football. The Saints have allowed the 9th-most FPG to opposing WRs aligned out wide. He’s a WR2. _**[Start Him]**_
**Germie Bernard** (Pit) — There is a _whole lot of green_ in Bernard’s profile over the past two weeks with **Michael Pittman** out (see below). Yes, that says he’s a WR1 by XFPG. And it’s a good matchup, as New Orleans allows the 9th-most FPG to opposing slot WRs, and Bernard has been **Aaron Rodgers** ’ zone-beater. Bernard’s 2.22 YPRR against zone is +0.48 over the NFL average, and the Saints run zone looks at the NFL’s second-highest level. A locked-in WR2. _**[Start Him]**_

Vele is still a viable WR3 this week, though, if you think that volume will tick up with New Orleans underdogs in Paris. ##### Tight Ends
**Pat Freiermuth** (Pit) — Had a 3.1-FP stinker in Week 6 as the Steelers kept Freiermuth in to protect against **Todd Bowles** ’ 50% blitz rate — ‘Muth’s 71.1% snap rate was his 2nd-highest of the year, but his 57.4% route share was 3rd-lowest. I expect a higher route share with the Saints underdogs in Week 7. _**[Stream Him]**_

Unfortunately, it’s a bad matchup — the Ravens have allowed the 6th-fewest FPG to opposing RBs this year. More of an RB2. _**[Start Him]**_

If you’re desperate, the Ravens allow the 5th-most FPG to slot WRs, which Gesicki effectively is (85.7% slot route rate). _**[Sit Him]**_

**Deshaun Watson** (Cle) — Watson has gone under 200 yards passing in back-to-back weeks and hasn’t accounted for multiple TDs since Week 4. Despite all the talk of Watson “playing well”, he’s ranked as a top-12 QB weekly just once this year, and he’s been outside the top 20 three times. Boston averages 2.39 YPRR against zone, and _3.33_ YPRR against two-high looks. Comparatively, **KC Concepcion** struggles against those coverages. A viable WR3. _**[Stream Him]**_
**KC Concepcion** (Cle) — As mentioned above, Concepcion struggles against the Titans’ base defensive principles compared to Boston. If your hand gets forced by injuries or byes, however, you at least have the comfort of knowing that the Browns actively try to get the ball in Concepcion’s hands — his 9 designed targets remain the highest in the entire NFL among WRs and TEs, ahead of even **Puka Nacua**. _**[Stash Him]**_

That stopped a streak of three consecutive 10+-FP games. Obviously, Robinson has enough of a role here to be rostered, but the question is if he (read: **Cam Ward**) can’t be trusted in a pristine matchup, when are you going to play him? I wouldn’t blame you if you wanted to drop him for someone with more upside. _**[Sit Him]**_

**Harold Fannin** (Cle) — Has had 3 targets with a single catch in back-to-back games — the only 2 targets over that span that we have deemed catchable. Fannin separates and wins at an elite level, but **Deshaun Watson** does not appear to care. He’d rather scramble, take a sack, or chuck the ball deep to **Denzel Boston**. The Titans also allow the 4th-fewest schedule-adjusted FPG to TEs over the last five weeks. Fannin is a low-end start who is more of a hold in the event the Browns make a QB switch… which seems far off. _**[Stash Him]**_

**Courtland Sutton** (Den) — While Waddle makes the cut for the top 36, his counterpart **Courtland Sutton** has yet to produce a single top-36 PPR finish for the season and averages 5.2 FPG. Sutton’s 0.78 YPRR ranks 98th and his catch rate hovers at 50%. Sutton still improbably gets 25% of the red zone looks and 38% of the short-yardage targets, and he’s drawing 36.2% of air yards over the last three games. But buyer beware: his next performance as a WR3 or better will be his first of the season. _**[Sit Him]**_
**Kendrick Bourne** (Ari) — The mercurial **Kendrick Bourne** has been boom-or-bust: he has three outings with seven or more targets, and three with three or fewer targets (including a zero-target, zero-point Week 4 outing). He averages 12.4 FPG when seeing seven or more targets, which would rank just ahead of **Luther Burden** at WR26 on the season. Bourne averages 2.4 FPG in the other three, which would rank just behind teammate **Reggie Virgil** at WR129. Which will we get in Week 7? It all depends on whether Denver’s 39.1% pressure rate prevails, as Bourne averages 0.20 YPRR on pressured looks (WR101). Bourne’s increasing slot usage, red zone targets, and strength in short looks may help, but I’m betting against a double-digit fantasy-point outing. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**Trey McBride** (Ari) — To little fanfare, **Trey McBride** leads all TEs in targets/game (10.3), TPRR, and red zone targets/game. His FPG, XFPG, and first-read marks are all top three, and he has at least seven catches and nine targets in every game. Week 6 was one of two where McBride finished outside the top 12, and his 3.2 aDOT was the primary culprit. Week 7 should see Arizona in more 13 personnel looks, which Denver’s defense ranks 31st in stopping. Jacoby Brissett’s EPA/DB against base looks (+0.33) is a top-10 mark, and Vance Joseph’s defense funnels 24.4% of targets to TEs. With zero end zone looks in his last three games after four in the first three games, McBride could be due for a big game. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Evan Engram** (Den) — Sean Payton’s preference for veterans shows in the increasing usage of J.K. Dobbins and **Evan Engram**. Engram’s 3.7 targets/game and 6.7 XFPG are big increases from his 2.0 target/4.8 XFPG marks to start the season. Production has not followed. Engram’s combined 14.4 PPR fantasy points over the last three games is only a fraction more than his 14.3 fantasy points in Week 1 alone. Although Engram’s 0.32 EPA/target mark ranks 15th among TEs and Arizona’s 19.3 FPG allowed to the position is the fifth-most generous, Engram is a desperation high-end TE3. _**[Sit Him]**_
### **Green Bay at Detroit**

##### Quarterbacks
**Jordan Love** (GB) — Love didn’t have his best statistical day against the Cowboys last week, but both **Matthew Golden** and **Christian Watson** dropped TD passes. The Lions give up the league’s most FPG to opposing QBs, and the Packers are underdogs. **Josh Jacobs** should help their run game, but I still view this as a pass-first team (4th-highest PROE). _**[Start Him]**_
**Jared Goff** (Det) — Look, Goff is at home, and the Lions have an implied team total of 28.25. Sometimes, fantasy football is a simple game. But the Packers have allowed the 5th-fewest FPG to opposing QBs, and they are disguising coverages (like the one below) at the league’s highest clip. It won’t be easy for Goff to put up huge numbers. _**[Start Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**Jahmyr Gibbs** (Det) — I hope you survived your bye week without him. The Packers give up the 4th-most FPG to opposing RBs. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Josh Jacobs** (GB) — Jacobs is expected to play this week as he returns from his “pseudo-suspension,” after spending six games on the commissioner’s exempt list. Jacobs wasn’t particularly efficient last year as he battled through injuries, but his role was, as we’ve come to expect, elite (see below). And now he joins a team that is dead last in rushing success rate (38.4%) and stuff rate (55.9%). **Amon-Ra St. Brown** (Det) — Ranks 3rd among all WRs in routes and targets per game. The Lions have an implied team total of 28.25. _**[Crown Him]**_

### **Los Angeles Rams at Las Vegas**
> Sunday, October 25, 2026, 4:25 PM Eastern
##### Quarterbacks
**Matthew Stafford** (LAR) — After a tough loss to Arizona, **Matthew Stafford** will captain an offense with 26.0 implied points against a sputtering Raiders defense. The Raiders allow 21.5 FPG to opposing QBs (tied for fifth-highest), rate in the bottom three against both short passes and explosive plays, and own one of the league’s worst red zone defenses. Stafford has 138 attempts over his past three games, the second-highest-volume three-game stretch of his career, yet he hasn’t thrown an interception over the last two games. Stafford is a mid-range QB1. _**[Start Him]**_
**Kirk Cousins** (LV) — As the younger of the two 38-year-old QBs in this battle, **Kirk Cousins** is posting some young-gun marks. His 14 passing TDs are tied with **Brock Purdy** and **Trevor Lawrence** atop the league’s leaderboard, and he’s QB11 with 19.0 FPG over his past three games. The Rams allow the 17th-most FPG to opposing QBs and have slipped a bit over the past three games, allowing +0.15 EPA/DB. With 307 pass YPG and 40.3 attempts/game over his last three games and entering Week 7 as a 4.5-point underdog, Cousins is a high-end QB2. _**[Stream Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**Kyren Williams** (LAR) — With a smooth 20.0 FPG, **Kyren Williams** has been outstanding as this year’s early RB6. Williams is doing damage on the ground with the league’s best success rate (60%) and stuff rate, but he’s also cooking teams through the air. Williams has 26 targets and 19 catches over the past three games and six or more receptions in half of his games. He faces a Las Vegas defense that rates among the league’s worst in the red zone, ranks second-worst against short passes, and allows the sixth-most fantasy points to backs (24.1 FPG). The Rams are 4.5-point favorites, and Williams is a mid-range RB1. _**[Start Him]**_
**Ashton Jeanty** (LV) — While the Las Vegas passing game posts a top-10 EPA/DB mark, **Ashton Jeanty** and the Raiders’ run game are in the bottom three. Jeanty is a premier receiving back, but the run game is broken. Jeanty’s 16.3% target share and 6.0 targets/game are each top-three marks among backs, and no back posts a better play-action YPRR (4.07). Despite the run game’s struggles, Jeanty owns 74.5% of the backfield’s expected fantasy points. Jeanty is also third in weighted opportunity/game behind only **Jahmyr Gibbs** and **Kenneth Walker**. Jeanty is a low-end RB1 in a down RB week. _**[Start Him]**_
**Blake Corum** (LAR) — Although he operates in a smaller role, **Blake Corum** is quietly posting top-10 marks in a handful of important categories. His 15.2% explosive-run rate and 2.85 yards before contact per carry are top-10 rushing marks, and his 2.17 YPRR and 0.29 targets per route marks are each top-six among backs through the air. His 11.1 FPG over the last three games ranks RB28, ahead of **Travis Etienne** and **Jeremiyah Love** , and his +3.48 fantasy points above expected over the span is RB9. Corum is a low-end RB2. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Mike Washington** (LV) — The leading rusher for Las Vegas in Week 6 was **Mike Washington**. Over the past three contests, Washington posts a higher YPC than Jeanty (3.47 vs. 3.06), a higher explosive run rate (16.7% vs. 3.1%), more MTF/A (0.21 vs. 0.09), and a better RYOE/attempt mark (-0.84 vs. -1.16). Let’s not get too excited: Washington averages 3.6 FPG over the last three games, and the “sit Jeanty” chatter is noise. But if Jeanty were to miss time, Washington appears to be more than ready for a bigger role. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Wide Receivers
**Puka Nacua** (LAR) — With 13.0 targets/game and 0.35 TPRR, **Puka Nacua** is the most-targeted player in the NFL. Puka’s 53 YAC in Week 6 was a small sign of what may be in store against Las Vegas, the league’s second-worst defense against short passes. Puka’s 39 first-read targets and three end zone targets over the past three games are career bests, and the Raiders allow 32.8 FPG to opposing WRs. Puka is Week 7’s consensus overall WR1. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Tre Tucker** (LV) — Last week was one to remember for **Tre Tucker** , who finished as the overall WR2 with 26.7 PPR fantasy points. It was his second top-seven finish this season, and Tucker’s 13.1 FPG places him at WR25 on the year. Although Tucker’s profile is boom-bust, the busts are survivable: his 7.2 FPG outside those two 20-point games would still rate ahead of **Rashid Shaheed** and **KC Concepcion** on the season. The Rams are among the bottom 10 defenses against intermediate throws, and Tucker lives in that range with an aDOT of 14.7. Tucker’s 298 air yards and four red zone targets over his last three games are a career-best three-game stretch, and he’s a solid WR3 option. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Konata Mumpfield** (LAR) — After the unfortunate injury to **Davante Adams** , the Rams’ new de facto WR2 is **Konata Mumpfield**. Mumpfield has [explosive potential](https://www.fantasypoints.com/nfl/articles/2026/dynasty-fantasy-football-market-report-2026-nfl-week-4) and cooks man coverage to the tune of 2.69 YPRR, a WR15 mark. Las Vegas plays man 23.6% of the time (13th-highest) and allows opposing offenses an extra +0.36 EPA/DB on these plays (sixth-worst). The news that Rams TE **Terrance Ferguson** is out enhances the opportunity. Analytics wizard Chris Wecht has [noted](https://x.com/ChrisWechtFF/status/2104996533603328377?s=20) that the Rams’ usage of 11 personnel and Mumpfield’s route share go hand in hand. But opportunity does not always immediately bring production, and Mumpfield projects for 6.3 PPR points in our model. _**[Sit Him]**_

**Brock Bowers** (LV) — The list of TE1 marks for **Brock Bowers** reads like a CVS receipt — he leads all TEs in routes, YPRR, aDOT, air yards share, first-read share, end zone targets, and FPG (20.8). His 333 air yards over the last three contests is the best three-game stretch of his career. Last week’s TE13 finish was the outlier: Bowers has finished TE1, TE3, TE2, and TE13 since his return. _**[Crown Him]**_

Higbee’s 72.3% snap share and 33.7 routes per game over his last three outings have produced 57.3 yards/game inside a high-powered offense. The Raiders allow the sixth-most FPG to opposing TEs (17.9) and funnel 26.9% of targets to TEs. Higbee remains a low-end TE1 over a decade after he was drafted. _**[Start Him]**_
**Michael Mayer** (LV) — The top TE for the Raiders in Week 6 was **Michael Mayer** , who finished as the TE6 and set a career high with 95 receiving yards. Mayer is TE13 on the season with 10.1 FPG, only half a point off the TE10 **Pat Freiermuth** (10.6 FPG). He’s posting TE1 marks with 5.5 targets/game (TE10), an 84.8% catch rate (TE4), and 5.82 YAC per catch (TE12). Week 7 may be tough sledding. The Rams allow the second-fewest FPG to opposing TEs and also post top-five marks against short passes, and Mayer is a low-end TE2. _**[Stash Him]**_

**Kenneth Walker** (KC) — In a matchup featuring two Super Bowl-winning QBs and lights-out defenses, the biggest storyline is **Kenneth Walker**. Seattle allows the 14th-most FPG to opposing backs (22.1 FPG), while Walker enters as the RB2 (27.6 FPG). Almost two in every three Walker carries are zone runs (64%), where his 6.44 YPC (RB2) mark will square off against a below-average Seahawks zone-run defense. _**[Crown Him]**_

It is impossible to know if Charbonnet will be on a snap count, and Kansas City is highly effective against the zone runs he relies on. Better weeks lie ahead, but Charbonnet is a TD-dependent, boom-or-bust RB3 in Week 7 facing a Chiefs defense ranking 11th-best in EPA rush/allowed. _**[Stash Him]**_

Unfortunately for Holani managers, Kansas City is the league’s best defense against short passes and ranks in the top 10 for fewest FPG allowed to opposing RBs. Holani returns to a reserve role barring an injury to Wilson or Charbonnet. _**[Sit Him]**_

**Jaxon Smith-Njigba** (Sea) — The league leader among WRs in FPG (27.5) is **Jaxon Smith-Njigba** , who also leads in target share (32.3%), air yards share (50.6%), and first-read share (42.2%). KC is ninth-worst against deep passes, another Smith-Njigba calling card, and JSN has posted 100+ receiving yards and a top-eight WR finish in five of his six games. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Xavier Worthy** (KC) — After **Rashee Rice** missed Week 6, **Xavier Worthy** climbed to WR23 on the back of 14.3 PPR fantasy points. A Seattle defense allowing the third-fewest FPG to opposing WRs (23.6 FPG) is not an ideal spot for Worthy. Seattle lives in zone, dialing up these looks at an 88.7% rate (the league’s highest). Worthy vanishes against zone looks, with 1.04 YPRR (WR101). Even with Rice uncertain, Worthy is a low-end WR3. _**[Sit Him]**_
**Rashid Shaheed** (Sea) — Coming off a season-high eight targets and five catches, **Rashid Shaheed** may see more deep looks against the Chiefs. Shaheed is Seattle’s field stretcher, and the Chiefs rank ninth-worst against deep passes. Shaheed’s three deep targets tied for the second-most among WRs in Week 6. The challenge is facing the league’s fourth-best man-coverage defense, an area where Shaheed struggles with a 0.31 YPRR (WR78). The popular narrative that Seattle chose Shaheed instead of Kenneth Walker is a fascinating backdrop, but Shaheed is outside the top 50 WRs this week. _**[Sit Him]**_
**Rashee Rice** (KC) — Injury Expert Jeff Mueller says there’s a [chance](https://www.fantasypoints.com/nfl/articles/2026/fantasy-football-injury-tracker) **Rashee Rice** returns, but Rice did not practice on [Wednesday](https://x.com/BlairKerkhoff/status/2112977018489012653?s=20). A Chiefs defense that ranks first against short passes is not ideal for Barner, who is a low-end TE2 this week. _**[Stash Him]**_

**George Pickens** (Dal) — In theory, will face off against **Quinyon Mitchell** more than **CeeDee Lamb** will in this game because of how much he aligns outside (a whopping 92.2% of his routes).
