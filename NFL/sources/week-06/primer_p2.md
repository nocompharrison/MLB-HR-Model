**[Daniel Jones (QB)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/daniel-jones.php)**
[Daniel Jones](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/daniel-jones.php) has been a disaster this season as the QB33 in fantasy points per game. Among 37 qualifying passers, Jones has the third-fewest yards per attempt, the fourth-lowest passer rating, the tenth-lowest passing success rate, and the third-lowest CPOE. Jones has ZERO mobility in the pocket or outside of it. Jones has not surpassed ten rushing yards in any game this season. Yes, I know that’s not surprising after the Achilles injury last year, but it’s worth noting. Jones flubbed a nice matchup against the Commanders recently, so while he could take advantage of a porous Tennessee secondary, I’m not overly optimistic that he will. The Titans have allowed the sixth-most yards per attempt, the fifth-highest passing success rate, and the second-highest CPOE.
**[Cam Ward (QB)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/cam-ward.php)**
[Cam Ward](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/cam-ward.php) has looked lost this season as the QB30 in fantasy points per game. Among 37 qualifying passers, Ward has the second-fewest yards per attempt, the eighth-lowest passing success rate, the lowest highly accurate throw rate, and the second-lowest hero throw rate. Ward hasn’t thrown for multiple passing touchdowns this season, and he also hasn’t had more than 225 passing yards in any game. The Colts have not fielded a good pass defense this season, but I have zero faith that Ward will be able to take advantage of the nice matchup. Indy has allowed the fourth-most yards per attempt, the seventh-highest passer rating, and the tenth-most EPA per dropback.

Indy has allowed the fifth-highest rushing success rate, the second-most yards before contact per attempt, and the ninth-highest missed tackle rate.

**[Romeo Doubs (WR)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/romeo-doubs.php)**
Well, last week was frustrating. With Mack Holins out and a perfect schematic matchup for Doubs, I expected him to crush. Then I got to see [Drake Maye](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/drake-maye.php) light up the Raiders’ secondary and Doubs lose his invitation to the passing attack party. Last week, Doubs led the team with a 94.2% route share, but he had only an 8.8% target share and 12.5% first-read share. UGH! Overall, he is the WR37 in fantasy points per game with a 14% target share, 1.59 YPRR, five red zone targets, six deep targets, and a 15.5% first-read share. With [Drake Maye](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/drake-maye.php) getting back to his spread-the-wealth ways of last year, Doubs will continue to be volatile. This is another strong schematic matchup for Doubs, but as we saw last week, we’ll have to collectively hold our breath if it turns into fantasy points. The Jets have the ninth-highest two-high rate (57.2%). Against two high, Doubs has only a 15% target per route run rate, but he also has seen his yards per route run jump from 0.52 (vs. single high) to 2.59. I will say the Raiders did flip their coverage usage on its head last week, as they ran with single high for 55.3% of their defensive snaps last week, so this easily could explain the lack of Doubs’ involvement last week. The Jets are a tougher matchup for perimeter wide receivers, allowing the eighth-fewest yards per target and the 12th-fewest fantasy points per opportunity to the position. If the coverage structure holds, though, Doubs could be a solid flex play.
