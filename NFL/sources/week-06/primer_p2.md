**[Rhamondre Stevenson (RB)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/rhamondre-stevenson.php)**
Rhamondre Stevenson will be the Pats’ lead back this week. I was originally expecting TreVeyon Henderson to miss this week, so we’re recalibrating Stevenson’s outlook for Week 6. In Weeks 3-4, with both backs active and Stevenson not in fumble jail. Stevenson averaged 13 touches and 56 total yards with a 59.4% snap rate, a 32.8% rushing share, and a 50.6% route share (10.3% target share). Stevenson should have a similar, if not slightly elevated, role this week with Henderson at less than 100% health. Stevenson has been an efficient player on a per-touch basis, which is consistent with his career marks. Among 42 qualifying rushers this season, Stevenson is 18th in yards after contact per attempt and 15th in missed tackle rate. Stevenson should rip the Jets’ run defense apart this week. New York has allowed the 13th-highest explosive run rate, the ninth-highest yards before contact per attempt, and the sixth-highest missed tackle rate.
**[Braelon Allen (RB)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/braelon-allen.php)**
[Braelon Allen](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/braelon-allen.php) likely draws another start this week for the injured [Breece Hall](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/breece-hall.php). Over the last two weeks, Allen has finished as the RB33 and RB8 in weekly scoring, averaging 20.5 touches and 91.5 total yards while playing 88.2% of the snaps. Allen has been New York’s bell cow. Among 42 qualifying backs, Allen is 11th in yards after contact per attempt and 14th in missed tackle rate. Allen should have another nice stat line this week against a Patriots run defense that has allowed the 11th-highest rushing success rate, the sixth-most yards after contact per attempt, and has the 12th-lowest rate of hitting backs at or behind the line of scrimmage.

**[TreVeyon Henderson (RB)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/treveyon-henderson.php)**
TreVeyon Henderson sustained an ankle injury last week. He managed limited practices all week and has been listed as questionable. Henderson wasn’t ruled out on Saturday, so I’m guessing he will be active this week and play his usual complementary role alongside Rhamondre Stevenson. In Weeks 3-4, when Rhamondre Stevenson hasn’t fumbled away his job, Henderson has averaged 11.5 touches and 35.5 total yards with a 36.8% snap rate, a 36.1% rushing share, and a 27.8% route share (2.9% target share). Henderson is a middling flex this week with a good matchup. Among 43 qualifying rushers, Henderson is 14th in explosive run rate, but he is also 34th in yards after contact per attempt and missed tackle rate. If Henderson gets a crease, he can pop big plays, but he’s not going to create a ton of yards on his own. New York has allowed the 13th-highest explosive run rate, the ninth-highest yards before contact per attempt, and the sixth-highest missed tackle rate.
**[A.J. Brown (WR)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/aj-brown.php)**
AJ Brown returned to practice this week (ankle). He managed limited practices all week and has been listed as questionable. It looks like Brown will be activated and play this week. He comes with a wide range of outcomes this week. After this week’s game, New England has a short week facing the Bears next week on Thursday. It’s possible Brown is active for this game, but it is also possible that he is a limited-snap player. During his LIMITED Pats tenure, Brown has had a 29% TPRR and 1.86 YPRR. I’m not saying that he can’t get it done for fantasy this week on a limited-snap basis, but it caps his ceiling and lowers his floor. The Jets are also a tougher matchup for perimeter wide receivers, allowing the eighth-fewest yards per target and the 12th-fewest fantasy points per opportunity to the position. Brown could post WR2/3 numbers this week if he gets into the end zone and the Pats make it a point to get him involved, but he also could be a dud.
**[Romeo Doubs (WR)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/romeo-doubs.php)**
Well, last week was frustrating. With Mack Holins out and a perfect schematic matchup for Doubs, I expected him to crush. Then I got to see [Drake Maye](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/drake-maye.php) light up the Raiders’ secondary and Doubs lose his invitation to the passing attack party. Last week, Doubs led the team with a 94.2% route share, but he had only an 8.8% target share and 12.5% first-read share. UGH! Overall, he is the WR37 in fantasy points per game with a 14% target share, 1.59 YPRR, five red zone targets, six deep targets, and a 15.5% first-read share. With [Drake Maye](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/drake-maye.php) getting back to his spread-the-wealth ways of last year, Doubs will continue to be volatile. This is another strong schematic matchup for Doubs, but as we saw last week, we’ll have to collectively hold our breath if it turns into fantasy points. The Jets have the ninth-highest two-high rate (57.2%). Against two high, Doubs has only a 15% target per route run rate, but he also has seen his yards per route run jump from 0.52 (vs. single high) to 2.59. I will say the Raiders did flip their coverage usage on its head last week, as they ran with single high for 55.3% of their defensive snaps last week, so this easily could explain the lack of Doubs’ involvement last week. The Jets are a tougher matchup for perimeter wide receivers, allowing the eighth-fewest yards per target and the 12th-fewest fantasy points per opportunity to the position. If the coverage structure holds, though, Doubs could be a solid flex play.
