# 2026 Week 6 Fantasy Football Start 'Em, Sit 'Em Advice
Joe DolanOCT. 17, 2026 4:25PM EDT
Welcome to Week 6 of Fantasy Football Start ‘Em, Sit ‘Em!
I’m the new author of this column this year, so I’ll introduce a new format! I will break down every game by position and player. After listing the player (and perhaps some interesting stats or news I’ve found in my research), I’ll pop that player into one of several buckets, which are as follows:
  * **Crown Him** — This player is an every-week starter, someone you almost never take out of your lineup _(90-100% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Start Him** — More than likely, this player should be in your lineup because of his role, recent production, or matchup, but you might have to think about it just a little _(70-90% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Stream Him** — This player isn’t a weekly starter (at least not yet), but circumstances could lead to him being in your optimal lineup this week _(50-70% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Stash Him** — This player is worthy of being on your roster, but ideally I want to see more before putting him in a lineup _(25-50% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Sit Him** — This player should not be in your lineup because of lack of recent production, a difficult matchup, or injury _(10-25% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Drop Him** — This player shouldn’t be on your roster _(0-10% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_


For simplicity’s sake, this column will focus on a 12-team PPR league with a base lineup of **1 QB, 2 RB, 3 WR, 1 TE, and 1 W/R/T FLEX.** Obviously, different league sizes and formats will change recommendations… and that’s what our [Fantasy Points Front Office](https://www.fantasypoints.com/fphq/import), with its multiple start/sit tools, is for.

### **Seattle at Denver**
> Thursday, October 15, 2026, 8:15 PM Eastern
##### Quarterbacks
**Bo Nix** (Den) — Obviously, _the_ biggest issue for Nix’s fantasy production is that he isn’t running — he has just 35 yards in five games. But he also ranks 30th of 36 qualified QBs in catchable throw rate (71%) despite ranking _dead last_ among those QBs in aDOT (6.19). The closest QB in aDOT with a lower catchable throw rate is **Daniel Jones** (7.66). The Broncos can’t run their offense this way no matter who the playcaller is. _**[Sit Him]**_
**Sam Darnold** (Sea) — Darnold has to play better if the Seahawks are going to repeat. He is 30th of 36 qualified QBs in EPA/dropback and is _last_ of 36 in turnover-worthy-throw percentage. That’s hard to do when his top receiver is an absolute stud. The Broncos have major injuries in the secondary, which should help, but Darnold struggles under pressure, and the Broncos generate pressure at the NFL’s 9th-highest rate. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**Emanuel Wilson** (Sea) — Ranked as the RB16 in backfield XFP percentage in Week 5. He ran fewer routes than **George Holani** , but Holani fumbled for a 49ers touchdown, which could make his status precarious. Unfortunately for Wilson, **Zach Charbonnet** is nearing a return, and he may be pushed to RB3 in the offense. Use him while you can as an RB2 if Charbonnet isn’t back. The Broncos have allowed the 8th-most FPG to opposing RBs this year. _**[Start Him]**_
**JK Dobbins** (Den) — Well, there is at least (exactly?) one person who was pleased with the Broncos’ switch of playcallers from **Davis Webb** to **Sean Payton** : Dobbins. His 67.0% backfield XFP percentage was the highest of any Broncos RB this season. Of course, the Broncos are underdogs in this game, and Dobbins ran just four routes in Week 5, his _lowest_ of the year. Nonetheless, it clearly appears that Payton believes starting his offense with Dobbins and the run game is the proper approach right now, whether he’s right or not. He’s a low-end RB2. _**[Stream Him]**_
**RJ Harvey** (Den) — Heading into Week 5, Harvey’s target share was higher than any player on the entire Broncos’ roster. And apparently one of the first things **Sean Payton** did when taking the playcalling back from **Davis Webb** was to completely nuke that role. Harvey’s 41.4% route share wasn’t tangibly lower than previous weeks, but he saw just 1 target and didn’t catch a pass. He had just 3 carries for 7 yards, and now has just 15 carries in four games. He is not usable if he’s not catching passes; at least the Broncos are underdogs in this game. The Seahawks allow the 5th-most FPG to RBs as receivers this season. He’s an RB3. _**[Stash Him]**_
**George Holani** (Sea) — Ran 14 routes to Wilson’s 12 in Week 5, but lost a key fumble for a 49ers TD. I wonder if his role will remain the same when **Zach Charbonnet** returns, with Wilson’s ceding more work. That’s the only reason Holani should be on a roster in any format at this point. The Broncos allow the 10th-most receiving FPG to opposing RBs. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Wide Receivers
**Jaxon Smith-Njigba** (Sea) — The Broncos are likely without **Patrick Surtain** (ankle) in this game. **Vance Joseph** might carry rosary beads with him this week. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Jaylen Waddle** (Den) — **Sean Payton** ’s biggest change to the Broncos’ offense in Week 5 was taking the ball out of **Bo Nix** ’s hands — they posted a PROE of -9.6%, their lowest of the year. Waddle caught just 2 balls for 19 yards on 3 targets, and had a terrible drop. He’s now had 35 receiving yards or fewer in four of five games this year. He needs much better QB play to pay off his ADP. And unfortunately, he might get a healthy dose of **Devon Witherspoon** this week. At least the Broncos might have to throw it more as underdogs. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Courtland Sutton** (Den) — Sutton scored his first touchdown of the season in Week 5, and it’s a good thing he did — he had just 2 catches for 18 yards. There was no tangible change in terms of the usage that we saw for Sutton with **Sean Payton** as playcaller, and touchdowns are literally the only way a receiver with his profile can come through for fantasy — his red-zone target share is literally the only thing on his heat map that ranks in even the 60th percentile. This is a bad offense, and it’s a bad matchup for Sutton, who has been stymied even more than usual against zone coverage, which the Seahawks run at the league’s highest rate. He’s a WR4 at best… if you play him, beg for a TD. _**[Sit Him]**_
![](https://www.fantasypoints.com/nfl/articles/2026/week-6-fantasy-football-start-em-sit-em-advice)
**Rashid Shaheed** (Sea) — Fell significantly below **Cooper Kupp** in route share last week (82.9% to 62.9%). Neither guy is making enough plays or seeing enough targets to be fantasy-relevant, but I have to admit I thought Shaheed would be doing more than this. He has only one reception of 20 or more yards this season. _**[Drop Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**AJ Barner** (Sea) — The Broncos’ LBs are not a particularly strong unit, and they’ve allowed the 12th-most FPG to opposing TEs this year. Barner’s also been top-2 on the Seahawks in route share in each of the last three games. Unfortunately, Barner has topped 10 FP in just one of five games this year. You’re basically begging for a tush-push TD if you play him. _**[Sit Him]**_
**Evan Engram** (Den) — Engram paced the Broncos’ pass-catchers with 4 catches and 55 yards in Week 5, despite a season-low 47.4% snap share and a second-lowest 58.6% route share. The 18.8% 1st-read target share he saw was tied for the team lead. But that role is just so feeble in a bad offense to trust him for fantasy. _**[Sit Him]**_
### **Houston vs. Jacksonville**
> Sunday, October 18, 2026, 9:30 AM Eastern, London, England
##### Quarterbacks
**Trevor Lawrence** (Jax) — Tied his season-high with 25 rushing yards last week despite running just 3 times. Lawrence accounted for just 1 TD and 3 turnovers in two games with the Texans last year, but both came before his late-season surge, a surge that has mostly continued into 2026. Lawrence also crushes single-high looks this year, which the Texans run at the NFL’s 9th-highest rate. A viable low-end QB1 in a low-total game (42.5). _**[Stream Him]**_
**CJ Stroud** (Hou) — I don’t think there’s a QB in the league who is playing better with less fanfare than Stroud. If you include only the games in which **Nico Collins** has played, Stroud is 8th in the NFL in EPA/DB, 3rd in CPOE, 8th in YPA, 6th in catchable throw rate, and 1st in turnover-worthy throw rate (meaning he has zero). He’s 10th in FPG. All this is with absolutely zero run game. I don’t know if **Marvin Harrison** is actually going to help, but Stroud is balling. The Jags have given up the 10th-fewest FPG to opposing QBs this year, so he’s more of a low-end fantasy starter this week. _**[Stream Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**Bhayshul Tuten** (Jax) — I’ll let the guys from this week’s [Everything Report](https://www.fantasypoints.com/nfl/articles/2026/the-everything-report-2026-fantasy-football-week-6) break down why Tuten is one of the best buy-lows in fantasy and a potentially hidden league-winner: Over the last two weeks, “Tuten has seen 83% of his backfield’s rush attempts and 79% of the backfield’s XFP; over the full season, both marks would rank 7th-best among all RBs. Tuten’s route participation has also crept up to 42.3% (~RB21) over the past two weeks (from 32.2%). He also leads the Jaguars in designed targets (6), with no other player seeing more than 3. Tuten’s 16.1 XFP/G over his last two games would rank 13th-best. But to date, he averages just 12.7 FPG (RB19) over the full season.” They wrote a whole lot more, so I would recommend checking it out, but he’s a slam-dunk RB2. _**[Start Him]**_
**Woody Marks** (Hou) — Played 98% of the snaps and got every backfield touch after **David Montgomery** left with a groin injury last week. His usage and efficiency profile still mostly sucks, but at least he’s generating some explosive plays and forcing missed tackles. Maybe the Monty injury is what he needs to become closer to a full-time player, though I’d expect **Jawhar Jordan** to be elevated to the gameday roster for this game. This week, he’s a mid-range RB2 — the Jags have given up the 3rd-fewest FPG to opposing RBs. _**[Stream Him]**_

**Chris Rodriguez** (Jax) — Rodriguez’s already small role has quietly shrunk even more, and a near fumble in Week 6 might give the Jags even less impetus to use him. Merely a handcuff and droppable in shallow leagues. _**[Sit Him]**_
**David Montgomery** (Hou) — Expected to miss multiple weeks with a groin injury. This could be the push **Woody Marks** needs to take over the backfield. He can be dropped in shallow formats. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Wide Receivers
**Nico Collins** (Hou) — Disappointing Week 5 with just 5/56 receiving on 6 targets, but the Texans didn't need him to do much in a blowout. A slam-dunk WR1. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Parker Washington** (Jax) — We’ve noted that the Jaguars have been, surprisingly, one of the lowest play-volume teams in the league. That changed in Week 5, when they were in a competitive tilt with the Eagles. Washington production-mogged every other Jags WR with 6/124/2. Hopefully the Texans can keep this one competitive. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Jakobi Meyers** (Jax) — Has two games of 10.0 FP or more, which is more than **Brian Thomas** (0) and **Josh Cameron** (1) combined. If you have another Jag receiver on your roster, it should probably be Meyers, as he’s the only one outside of Washington with a 60% or higher route share in every game. If you’re desperate, Meyers averages 1.89 YPRR when aligned out wide (+0.19 over NFL average), and the Texans have given up the most FPG to out-wide WRs this year (thanks, **CeeDee Lamb**). Worth a hold if the Jags’ play volume goes up, as it did last week. _**[Sit Him]**_
**Marvin Harrison Jr.** (Hou) — Marv has been legitimately one of the worst receivers in football, but the Texans did pay a hefty price for him, relatively speaking, and he’s reuniting with his college QB. Houston clearly has a plan… maybe some more digs and slants? If you held Marv through the first month of the season, I have no idea why you’d drop him now, but you can’t play him yet. _**[Sit Him]**_
**Brian Thomas Jr.** (Jax) — Had the first goose egg of his NFL career last week. He hasn’t been higher than 4th on the team in route share in any game this year, and **Josh Cameron** ’s routes continue to rise (season-high 29.5% last week). You have to wonder if soon he’ll meet the fate of his counterpart in this game, Moped Marv — traded away. _**[Drop Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**Brenton Strange** (Jax) — Believe it or not, Strange has led or tied **Parker Washington** in 1st-read target share in each of the last two games and is putting up top-12 TE numbers by XFP and FPG over the last three. The only two TEs with a pulse the Texans have faced — **Dalton Kincaid** and **Tyler Warren** — combined for 32.5 FP against them. A viable TE1 this week with **Sam LaPorta** on bye. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Dalton Schultz** (Hou) — Schultz had 26.0 PPR FP in Week 2 against the Bengals’ TE-funnel defense. He has 18.4 FP in the other four games combined. Curiously, his route share has fallen in every game, from 73.8% in Week 1 to 44.4% in Week 5 — it is worth noting that Schultz’s last target came on a play in which he had a big collision in the second quarter, but he returned to action. The Jags have given up the 7th-fewest FPG to TEs this year. A mediocre TE2. _**[Sit Him]**_

**Tre Tucker** (LV) — Despite leading all Las Vegas pass catchers with 144 routes, **Tre Tucker** ranks third among Raiders pass catchers with 8.07 FPG in the last three outings. The coverage this week may be favorable. Buffalo plays two-high at the 10th-highest rate. Tucker is +0.50 YPRR vs. league average against two-high, and Buffalo is 22nd in FPG allowed from these looks. A whopping 44.1% of Tucker’s fantasy production came in a Week 2 outing against a Chargers team that plays two-high at an even higher rate than Buffalo. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Keon Coleman** (Buf) — Part of why Khalil Shakir struggles for TD looks is **Keon Coleman** , who has notched a receiving TD in each of the last two weeks. Coleman’s TD look was his only catch in the game in Week 5, but the Las Vegas desert could be kinder. The Raiders love nickel packages, dialing them up at a 91% rate, and Coleman is +0.65 YPRR above average against those looks. Coleman’s 93rd-percentile explosive target rate could create [downfield opportunities](https://www.fantasypoints.com/nfl/articles/2026/dynasty-fantasy-football-market-report-2026-nfl-week-5) against a Raiders team with a 38% pressure rate. Shakir has the steadier floor, but Coleman is a reasonable ceiling play. _**[Stream Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**Brock Bowers** (LV) — Much like Josh Allen, Brock Bowers is establishing an unreal level of dominance at his position. The race between **Jaxon Smith-Njigba** and **Puka Nacua** is separated by exactly 2.0 FPG, while **Jahmyr Gibbs** is +3.4 FPG against RB2 **Kenneth Walker**. Allen and Bowers are each +5.9 FPG vs. their nearest peer competitors. Scott Barrett put it best — this is legitimately insane. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Dalton Kincaid** (Buf) — Director of Analytics Chris Wecht [notes](https://x.com/ChrisWechtFF/status/2110062890736578883?s=20) that **Dalton Kincaid** is now at the lowest TPRR of his career, a mere 21%, and running 7% fewer routes than he did in 2023. Kincaid’s efficiency remains sterling. His 2.74 YPRR is second among TEs, and his 40.0% explosive rate is light years ahead of all other TEs. Like his teammate Coleman, Kincaid cooks nickel looks to the tune of +2.36 YPRR above average, and he’s a bad man if he gets the targets. But he has fewer end zone targets than **Elijah Higgins** and has seen fewer red zone looks than **Gunnar Helm** and **Mike Gesicki**. _**[Start Him]**_
**Michael Mayer** (LV) — As powerful as Bowers is, his counterpart **Michael Mayer** is proving a reasonable second in command. His 9.87 FPG the past three weeks only trails Bowers among Raiders pass catchers. That mark places Mayer just ahead of **Mark Andrews** , **Kenyon Sadiq** , and **Kyle Pitts** over the same span, and the Raiders love to feature their TEs. Mayer is a strong handcuff and steady streaming option. _**[Stream Him]**_
### Los Angeles Chargers at Kansas City
> Sunday, October 18, 2026, 4:25 PM Eastern
##### Quarterbacks
**Patrick Mahomes** (KC) — Mahomes has thrown for multiple TDs in each of his four starts this year, but it looks like he’ll be without **Rashee Rice** (hamstring) this week and **Tyquan Thornton** (ankle) for the season. He’s already significantly outperforming his QB17 XFPG ranking, and the Chiefs are heavy favorites, so they should be able to lean on **Kenneth Walker** this week. Mahomes is more of a low-end QB1. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Justin Herbert** (LAC) — Herbert ranks 35th of 36 QBs in EPA/DB and 33rd of 36 in turnover-worthy throw rate. His team is decimated, and he has no answers for it. At least there’s a chance Herbert gets **Ladd McConkey** (foot) back this week. He’s only rosterable in superflex formats. _**[Drop Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**Kenneth Walker** (KC) — Coming out of his bye, looking to continue his replication of **Saquon Barkley** ’s 2024 season. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Omarion Hampton** (LAC) — Hampton hasn’t led this backfield in XFP share since Week 3, and hasn’t had even 40% of the share since Week 2. He’s a poor fit for **Mike McDaniel** ’s offense, and McDaniel seemingly has no intention of catering to Hampton’s skill set behind a banged-up offensive line. Hampton is a touchdown-dependent FLEX on a bad team that is a two-score underdog. _**[Stash Him]**_
**Keaton Mitchell** (LAC) — I’d like to throw more support Mitchell’s way in this game, considering he’s led this backfield in XFP in two consecutive games and the Chargers are big underdogs, but the problem is that this is now an ugly three-man backfield with the insertion of **Kimani Vidal** into the equation as the Chargers look for more explosive plays. Vidal has actually run more routes than Mitchell in each of the last two games. If I _HAD_ to play a Charger RB, it’d be Mitchell, but I have no confidence in it. _**[Stash Him]**_
##### Wide Receivers
**Xavier Worthy** (KC) — It looks like Worthy will have to operate as the Chiefs’ #1 WR this week with **Tyquan Thornton** on IR and **Rashee Rice** (hamstring) likely out. For the first time all season in Week 4, Worthy was used like he actually has the 4.2 speed he timed with at the Combine — he had a 12.86 aDOT and 90 air yards, catching all 4 of his catchable targets for 70 yards. He’s been a disappointment in the NFL so far, but he’s tied to **Patrick Mahomes** , which is good enough for me this week. He’s on the low-end WR2 radar. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Ladd McConkey** (LAC) — McConkey could return from his foot injury this week, but how healthy is he? He was an “active inactive” in Week 5, and ran just 13 routes in Week 4. It’s possible the week off helped him heal up, but it’s really hard to trust a guy who has had one usable fantasy week on perhaps the league’s most broken offense. A very flimsy WR3. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Quentin Johnston** (LAC) — Could return from his chest injury this week, and he’ll be back in the lineup in the place of **Tre Harris** , who is dealing with a hamstring injury. But I’m not terribly enthused to be using a WR who has just a 41.2% catch rate even though 76.5% of his targets have been catchable. Johnston hasn’t caught more than 3 passes in a game this year. _**[Sit Him]**_
**Rashee Rice** (KC) — Likely out with a hamstring injury. If you’re desperate for a low-end option in Week 6, **Jalen Royals** saw the biggest increase in usage of any Chiefs receiver with Rice and later **Tyquan Thornton** out of the lineup. Royals ran a route on 63.6% of dropbacks, by far a career high. **Cyrus Allen** also ran his first routes since Week 2, but had just 2 total. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**Travis Kelce** (KC) — KillaTrav has just 5 targets over his last two games combined, but it’d be surprising if **Patrick Mahomes** doesn’t lean on him more this week given the injuries the Chiefs have at WR. Kelce has been especially strong against two-high shells this year, averaging 3.02 YPRR, and the Chargers deploy two-high at the NFL’s 8th-highest rate. _**[Start Him]**_
**Charlie Kolar** (LAC) — Not that he’s worth anything for fantasy yet, but it should be noted that Kolar is definitely the Chargers’ top TE — he ran a 65.6% route share in Week 5 and doesn’t come off the field in obvious run situations. _**[Drop Him]**_
### Dallas at Green Bay
> Sunday, October 18, 2026, 8:20 PM Eastern
##### Quarterbacks
**Jordan Love** (GB) — The QB6 finish in a big division win was the headline for **Jordan Love** , but the way Love got there was impressive. Love threaded the needle by leading all QBs in both yards per attempt and turnover-worthy throw rate. Love projects as the QB4 this week in a friendly matchup against the Cowboys defense. Dallas is dead last in EPA/DB allowed in both man and zone. Start Love with confidence. _**[Start Him]**_
**Dak Prescott** (Dal) — The last time **Dak Prescott** faced the Packers, he turned in a 319-yard passing effort with 3 TDs through the air and another on the ground. The 2026 Packers surrender the sixth-fewest FPG to opposing QBs. Prescott has logged 40 or more pass attempts in three straight games but has produced 20+ fantasy points only once this season. Prescott owns a steady floor, and even last week’s season-low 14.6 fantasy points were enough to finish QB17. Prescott is a safe start, but Dallas is more likely to target Green Bay on the ground. _**[Start Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**Javonte Williams** (Dal) — A career-high 19.2 FPG has powered **Javonte Williams** to RB8 on the season. His efficiency hasn’t been impressive, but 6 rushing TDs (tied for third with **Jonathan Taylor**) have done the heavy lifting. The Packers are a run funnel in fantasy — they rank 31st in FPG allowed to RBs (28.4 FPG), but they’re stout against every other skill group. Expect Dallas to lean on their star and start Williams as a mid-level RB1. _**[Start Him]**_
**MarShawn Lloyd** (GB) — Forgive **MarShawn Lloyd** for celebrating: his two rushing TDs in Week 5 were the team’s first two of the year. Lloyd managed to finish RB12 on the week in both PPR fantasy points and EPA/A. Dallas allows the sixth-most fantasy points to RBs, but Lloyd has yet to surpass 37 yards in any game and has been held to 20 or fewer yards in most contests. Lloyd is a low-end RB2 in a down week at the position. _**[Stash Him]**_
##### Wide Receivers
**CeeDee Lamb** (Dal) — Last week’s 2-for-9 outing aside, **CeeDee Lamb** has cooked. Lamb is the WR3 despite his Week 5 dud and averages 101.4 yards/game. Lamb has twice surpassed 35 fantasy points in a week, but he now faces an above-average Packers pass defense. He cooks nickel at +1.45 YPRR above average, with the Packers using nickel at the league’s 6th-highest rate and allowing an extra 0.29 YPRR against them. It is hard to predict a third 30+ point outing, but Lamb is as sure a start as they come. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Christian Watson** (GB) — The reigning WR1 from Week 5 has another excellent matchup. **Christian Watson** led all WRs last week with 5.11 YPRR to go along with a 56.8% air yards share and 20.43 YPT. It was a special game, but it wasn’t an outlier. Watson is now WR4 on the season with 21.9 FPG, and Scott Barrett notes that Watson has the third-highest YPRR mark of any WR over the last [three years](https://x.com/ScottBarrettDFB/status/2110003874224591134?s=20). _**[Crown Him]**_
**Matthew Golden** (GB) — After failing to reach 13.5 fantasy points in a single contest his rookie season, **Matthew Golden** has multiple 20+ point outings this year. Golden is the WR17 with 15.66 FPG, fueled by a 37.1% air yards share and 13.64 aDOT. Golden produces an extra +1.93 in YPRR over the NFL average against man coverage, and Dallas allows 0.49 YPRR above league average in these looks. Golden is a high-end WR2. _**[Start Him]**_
**George Pickens** (Dal) — It all finally clicked for **George Pickens** in last week’s season-high WR5 finish. Pickens shredded Tampa Bay to the tune of 3.61 YPRR on 13 targets and grabbed his first TD of the year. Pickens has now posted double-digit fantasy outings in three of his last four (with the fourth at 9.5), and he delivered an 8/134/2 line against Green Bay in their last matchup. Pickens and Golden are the cream of this week’s WR2 crop. _**[Start Him]**_
**Ryan Flournoy** (Dal) — The second-leading receiver behind Pickens last week, Flournoy grabbed a career-long 48-yard reception. That helped him crack the Top 10 on the week in YPRR, YAC/REC, and explosive percentage. Keep Flournoy on your bench if you can manage it: he produced multiple 100-yard outings last year, and he has breakout potential if Lamb or Pickens ever miss time. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**Tucker Kraft** (GB) — After a slow start, **Tucker Kraft** is the TE9 over the last two outings with 14.05 FPG. Kraft may be on the verge of bigger things. He is Top 4 among TEs in yards after contact, but he has been on the wrong side of TD luck. Dallas ranks in the Top 10 for TDs allowed to TEs, and they allow the sixth-highest YPT mark (8.31) to opposing TEs. Kraft is among the upper half of TE1s this week. _**[Start Him]**_
**Jake Ferguson** (Dal) — Kraft’s bad TD luck finds a counterpart in **Jake Ferguson**. Ferguson averages 15.3 FPG in the two contests where he has reached the end zone, which would make him a Top 5 TE. His 3.9 FPG in the three contests he has not scored would slot in at TE44, behind **Tanner Hudson** and **Nate Adkins**. Ferguson sits behind Flournoy in target share, and he is a TD-dependent TE2 option facing the league’s eighth-best defense in limiting TE fantasy points. _**[Stash Him]**_

**Rachaad White** (Was) — An extra-sneaky FLEX play this week. **Jacory Croskey-Merritt** is dealing with an injury, and even before that, White was doubling up “Bill” in routes run in his return from injury in Week 5. **Austin Ekeler** is also wearing a non-contact jersey in Thursday practice.
