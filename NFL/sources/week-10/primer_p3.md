#### **Strong Starts**
_Here are players that should be in starting consideration for most fantasy teams._
**[DJ Moore (WR)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/dj-moore-wr.php)**
Last week, [DJ Moore](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/dj-moore-wr.php) wasn’t a full-time player for Buffalo. He had a 66.7% route share, a 16.1% target share with 25 receiving yards (1.04 YPRR), and a 22.7% first-read share. The shoulder injury continues to linger despite being on the field now. In his four “full” games this season, Moore has had a 24.2% target share with 2.71 YPRR, four red zone targets, seven deep targets, and a 25% first-read share. He is Buffalo’s unquestioned WR1. He had a horrible matchup last week, but he could bounce back this week against the Jets’ softer secondary. Over their last five games, New York has ranked 15th in yards per target while allowing the sixth-most fantasy points per opportunity to perimeter wide receivers.

Last week, [Dalton Kincaid](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/dalton-kincaid.php) saw his snap rate and route share tumble as the Bills trotted out [Dawson Knox](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/dawson-knox.php) and [Jackson Hawes](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/jackson-hawes.php) more, given their need to protect [Josh Allen](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/josh-allen-qb.php) and run the ball. [Dalton Kincaid](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/dalton-kincaid.php) had only a 50.6% snap rate and 47.2% route share. Kincaid’s route share has dipped below 60% in three of his last five games. His usage is still fickle this season as it depends upon the matchups and Buffalo’s game plan. When he has been on the field, Kincaid has still commanded targets with a 24% TPRR with 2.66 YPRR, six red zone targets, five deep targets, and a 20.3% first-read share. Kincaid’s range of outcomes is wide again this week. The Jets have been bleeding out production to tight ends, but over their last five games they also have the fourth-best pressure rate. Kincaid could be heavily utilized this week or nerfed with a 40-50% route share. Over their last five games, New York has allowed the fifth-most yards per target and the sixth-most fantasy points per opportunity to tight ends.

#### **Strong Starts**
_Here are players that should be in starting consideration for most fantasy teams._
**[Konata Mumpfield (WR)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/konata-mumpfield.php)**
Without [Davante Adams](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/davante-adams.php) in the lineup the last three games, the Rams have utilized 13 personnel (three tight end sets) with 47.2-54.8% of their offensive snaps. It has left [Konata Mumpfield](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/konata-mumpfield.php) a part-time player. Last week, [Konata Mumpfield](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/konata-mumpfield.php) had only a 42.5% snap rate and a 44.4% route share. In Weeks 2-3, with [Davante Adams](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/davante-adams.php) active and Nacua out, Mumpfield had a 71.8% snap rate, a 66.7% route share, an 11.6% target share with 1.69 YPRR, and an 11.7% first-read share. Mumpfield isn’t a high-end target earner. I expect Mumpfield to be the primary receiver with all the tight ends this week as the full-time option, based on his ascension earlier in the season and his ability to help in the run-blocking department. That’s not something the Rams should be asking [Tutu Atwell](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/tutu-atwell.php) to do often. The Cardinals have the third-highest two-high rate in the NFL (65.5%). In Weeks 2-3, against two-high, Mumpfield saw his target-per-route run rate jump from 14% to 20% and his YPRR jump from 0.97 to 2.76. Now, this is only a 62-route sample, but it’s worth noting. Over the last five games, Arizona has allowed the 14th-most yards per target to perimeter wide receivers. Zoom out to the entire season, and they have allowed the third-most yards per target and the fourth-most fantasy points per opportunity to the position. Mumpfield is a decent flex this week with a wonderful matchup. My biggest concerns for Mumpfield are his ability to earn a higher target share and [Matthew Stafford](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/matthew-stafford.php)‘s passing volume.

**[Matthew Stafford (QB)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/matthew-stafford.php)**
[Matthew Stafford](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/matthew-stafford.php) is the QB16 in fantasy points per game, with four games with at least 18.5 fantasy points. Unfortunately for Stafford, his receiving weapons are dropping like flies, with [Terrance Ferguson](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/terrance-ferguson.php), [Davante Adams](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/davante-adams.php), and [Puka Nacua](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/puka-nacua.php) all hurt. It has also come out that Stafford is dealing with elbow discomfort. This explains his erratic play at times this season. Among 31 qualifying passers, Stafford is 19th in yards per attempt, tenth in passing success rate, 18th in highly accurate throw rate, 20th in catchable target rate, and he has the ninth-highest turnover-worthy throw rate. The Rams have been leaning more run-heavy over their last four games and will likely continue to do so considering all the circumstances. Across his last four games, Stafford has surpassed 33 passing attempts only once. He hasn’t surpassed 230 passing yards in any game during that stretch. Despite all of these factors, Stafford could still post decent QB2 numbers this week because Arizona’s pass defense has been buns. Over their last five games, Arizona has allowed the ninth-highest passing touchdown rate, the seventh-highest passer rating, the fourth-highest EPA per dropback, and the fourth-highest fantasy points per dropback.
**[Blake Corum (RB)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/blake-corum.php)**
Since Week 5, [Blake Corum](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/blake-corum.php) has been the RB16 in fantasy points per game, averaging 11.8 touches and 72.8 total yards. He has scored a touchdown in each game, which has helped to prop up the limited volume and hyper-efficiency. Among 45 qualifying backs, Corum is fourth in explosive run rate and 14th in missed tackle rate. Corum could see his hot streak come to an end this week, but the Rams could feed him more volume this week as [Kyren Williams](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/kyren-williams.php) and Corum could be the engines of the offense. Over their last five games, Arizona’s run defense has tightened up, allowing the ninth-lowest explosive run rate, the 12th-lowest rushing success rate, and ranking 15th in yards before contact per attempt.

Higbee is on the streaming radar this week.

Before I get into this game, let’s discuss Beck as a prospect entering the league. I like Beck to an extent as a possible career backup who could give you a few starts in a pinch if needed. Beck is at his best when he’s gripping and ripping passes to the short and intermediate levels. He can play the role of point guard for a passing attack, but I don’t consider him to be a passer that can put on the Superman cape weekly if needed. In his final collegiate season, among all FBS quarterbacks with at least 150 dropbacks (per PFF), he was eighth in adjusted completion rate and 32nd in yards per attempt, but he was also 131st in aDOT and 102nd in big-time throw rate. The Rams are a below-average matchup for Beck. Over their last five games, the Rams have allowed the fifth-lowest passing touchdown rate, the 14th-lowest passer rating, the 14th-lowest fantasy points per dropback, and the tenth-lowest EPA per dropback. Also, add that Beck is flanked by unproven weapons in the receiver room outside of [Trey McBride](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/trey-mcbride.php). His first NFL start could be ugly. Sit Beck.
