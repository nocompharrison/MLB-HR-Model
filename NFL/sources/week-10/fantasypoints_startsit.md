# 2026 Week 10 Fantasy Football Start 'Em, Sit 'Em Advice
Joe DolanNOV. 14, 2026 11:59AM EST
Welcome to Week 10 of Fantasy Football Start ‘Em, Sit ‘Em!
I’m the new author of this column this year, so I’ll introduce a new format! I will break down every game by position and player. After listing the player (and perhaps some interesting stats or news I’ve found in my research), I’ll pop that player into one of several buckets, which are as follows:
  * **Crown Him** — This player is an every-week starter, someone you almost never take out of your lineup _(90-100% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Start Him** — More than likely, this player should be in your lineup because of his role, recent production, or matchup, but you might have to think about it just a little _(70-90% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Stream Him** — This player isn’t a weekly starter (at least not yet), but circumstances could lead to him being in your optimal lineup this week _(50-70% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Stash Him** — This player is worthy of being on your roster, but ideally I want to see more before putting him in a lineup _(25-50% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Sit Him** — This player should not be in your lineup because of lack of recent production, a difficult matchup, or injury _(10-25% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Drop Him** — This player shouldn’t be on your roster _(0-10% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_


For simplicity’s sake, this column will focus on a 12-team PPR league with a base lineup of **1 QB, 2 RB, 3 WR, 1 TE, and 1 W/R/T FLEX.** Obviously, different league sizes and formats will change recommendations… and that’s what our [Fantasy Points Front Office](https://www.fantasypoints.com/fphq/import), with its multiple start/sit tools, is for.

### **Washington at New York Giants**
> Thursday, November 12, 2026, 8:15 PM Eastern
##### Quarterbacks
**Jayden Daniels** (Was) — Daniels has just 4 passing TD in four games since returning from injury, and after rushing for 103 yards and 2 TD against these Giants in Week 5, has just 49 yards on 10 rushes over his last three games. Daniels’ finishes since his return — QB2, QB7, QB16, QB13. Losing his top receiver, **Terry McLaurin,** hasn’t helped matters. The Giants have allowed the 9th-most schedule-adjusted FPG to QBs over the past five weeks, and they’ve allowed the most rushing yards per game to the QB position—of course, Daniels himself accounted for nearly half of that. _**[Start Him]**_
**JJ McCarthy** (NYG) — McCarthy is expected to play through a hip pointer he suffered in Week 9’s win over the Eagles. McCarthy didn’t play _poorly_ against Philly, but it is at least worth noting that **Jameis Winston** led the Giants on their only TD drive — the one drive of the game he played. “Nine” still tends to run into a bunch of sacks, and his 54.4% accurate throw rate is just 44th of 61 QBs who have attempted a pass this year. His 29% pressure-to-sack ratio is 4th among all QBs who have started multiple games. If you’re dying for a streamer, the Commanders have allowed the 4th-most FPG to opposing QBs this year, so it’s not a bad spot. He’s more of a high-end QB2. _**[Stash Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**Cam Skattebo** (NYG) — Skattebo is expected to be a full go against Washington on Thursday night, now more than two weeks removed from his pre-bye hamstring injury. Skattebo was benched in Week 6, then turned in a 19.0-FP performance in Week 7, his best of the season. I expect him to return as the Giants’ lead back in Week 10, given **Devin Singletary** gained 39 yards on 23 opportunities against the Eagles last week. The Commanders are decimated at defensive tackle. Skattebo is a solid RB2. _**[Start Him]**_
**Rachaad White** (Was) — White’s role in Week 9’s loss to the Rams — a game in which Washington was competitive throughout — was pretty disappointing. He received just a 53.9% backfield XFP share, with **Austin Ekeler** getting the other 46.1%. White carried 7 times for 28 yards and posted 2/41 receiving on 3 targets… while running a route on a season-low 28.6% of Commanders’ dropbacks. Both of those numbers were lower than Ekeler’s… though White was significantly more efficient as both a runner and receiver. What are we doing here, Commanders? Maybe they’ll get their heads out of their butts this week, but it’s a bad matchup — the Giants have allowed the 7th-fewest FPG to opposing RBs, and White’s 3.0-FP performance against them in Week 5 was his worst of the year. White’s a low-end RB2. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Austin Ekeler** (Was) — Can someone explain to me what the Commanders were doing with Ekeler last week? He received more carries than **Rachaad White** (8 to 7) and ran a higher route share (40.5% to 28.6%) despite being less efficient in each area. Ekeler is in the _0th_ percentile among RBs in success rate (33.3%) and the 2nd percentile in stuff rate (58.3%). He is totally dust. But Washington is an underdog this week, so maybe they’re still misguided and think he’s a good passing-down back? _**[Sit Him]**_
**Devin Singletary** (NYG) — Singletary is purely a **Cam Skattebo** handcuff right now, and not a particularly good one if Week 9 is any indication — he had just 39 yards on 23 touches against the Eagles. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Wide Receivers
**Malik Nabers** (NYG) — Had just 5/43 against the Eagles last week on 8 targets, but he dropped a touchdown, and it was a tough matchup. The Commanders give up the 8th-most yards per game to opposing outside WRs. Nabers had 11/121/1 against this team in Week 5. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Stefon Diggs** (Was) — Diggs hasn’t led the Commanders in route share since Week 4, despite **Terry McLaurin** missing most of the last three games with his patellar tendon injury. In Week 9, despite **Treylon Burks** missing the game and the Rams being down top CB **Trent McDuffie** , Diggs was 4th on the team in first-read target share, to boot. Since **Jayden Daniels** returned from injury, Diggs’ best receiving-yardage output is 44, and he’s caught just 3 balls in back-to-back games. Three of his four lowest target-share games have come over the last three weeks. He’s really just a WR3 at this stage. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Antonio Williams** (Was) — I had a prop bet on Williams last week, and got a great number on it because I bet it before **Treylon Burks** was ruled out. So of course **Van Jefferson** went nuts despite Williams running a team-high 76.2% of routes among WRs. He has now led or tied Commanders’ WRs in route share in five consecutive games… but has finished inside the top 36 among WRs exactly zero times over that span. He’s more of an interesting stash than anything else. _**[Stash Him]**_
**Treylon Burks** (Was) — Burks missed last week with a foot injury but is expected to play against the Giants. Prior to missing last week’s game, he had finished as a fringe WR3 in four of his previous five games. Shockingly, Burks is 7th among WRs in ASS, 2nd in route win rate, and 16th in explosive play rate. He’s not a consistent fantasy performer, but he’s left the back porch light on for his dynasty managers, and there’s a slight chance he actually starts to outproduce the other WRs here soon. _**[Stash Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**Isaiah Likely** (NYG) — Likely is the TE3 by both XFPG and FPG, and he scored 2 TD against this defense in Week 5. So, duh, you’re playing him. But it’s at least a little weird that his 2 touchdowns since then came with a backup QB who entered the game — **JJ McCarthy** in Week 6, and **Jameis Winston** in Week 9. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Chig Okonkwo** (Was) — Okonkwo has at least 57 yards receiving in three of the four games he’s played since **Jayden Daniels** returned from injury, and he’s operated as a full-time player in each of the last three, with a route share of 78.6% or better in those three games. He’s yet to score a touchdown this year, but it feels like it’s coming given his usage. A worthy bench TE. _**[Stash Him]**_

It’s really hard to get a read on how this backfield will split up against the Lions in Week 10, but the Lions have been an extreme pass funnel — they’ve given up the 2nd-fewest FPG to opposing RBs. I’ll consider Kiner a low-end RB2 as I expect Larison (or perhaps **Terrell Jennings**) to run a not-insignificant route share. _**[Stream Him]**_

If Stevenson goes, he’s a mid-range RB2, with Kiner a desperation flex.
##### Wide Receivers
**Amon-Ra St. Brown** (Det) — You can’t sit the Sun God, but he’s ranked as a top-12 weekly WR just once in his last six games. That’s mildly concerning! _**[Crown Him]**_
**DeMario Douglas** (NE) — The Lions give up the 3rd-most FPG to opposing slot WRs this season. That’s fantastic news for Douglas (see below). _**[Stream Him]**_
> **UPDATE** : **AJ Brown** (knee) has a chance to play this week. If he goes, he’ll be a volume-based WR2. I still really like Douglas as a WR3 given his recent history and the matchup, but it would obviously crush **Mack Hollins** ’ upside.
**Jameson Williams** (Det) — The entire Lion offense had a bad week last week, so I’m not going to throw up a panic sign on Jamo posting a season-low 5.0 FP. But if you’re planning to use him this week, it is worth noting that the Patriots run man coverage at the NFL’s 2nd-highest rate, and Jamo averages just 0.79 YPRR against man vs. 2.09 YPRR against zone coverages. I know the Packers ate **Christian Gonzalez** ’s soul last week, but Jamo runs 79% of his routes from the outside — he’ll probably see a lot of Gonzo as he attempts to bounce back. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Mack Hollins** (NE) — Hollins returned to the Patriots’ lineup in Week 9 and immediately became **Drake Maye** ’s preferred perimeter target, running a 69.6% route share and commanding a 16.7% first-read target share, second to only slot receiver **DeMario Douglas** (26.7%). He scored his first touchdown of the year, and has now gone over 50 yards or scored in four of his five games this season. The Lions are a slot-funnel defense, but if I’m playing one Patriots outside WR, it’s Hollins. He nuked **Efton Chism** ’s role (47.8% route share), and **Kyle Williams** has been running wind sprints (4 targets, 1 catch on 72 routes the last three weeks). _**[Stash Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**Sam LaPorta** (Det) — LaPorta has had exactly 5 targets in three of his last four games, but hasn’t scored since Week 5. It’s notable that, on a _very_ small sample, LaPorta has crushed man coverage — 3.33 YPRR against man vs. 1.52 YPRR against zone. The Patriots run man coverage at the NFL’s 2nd-highest rate. _**[Start Him]**_
**Hunter Henry** and **Eli Raridon** (NE) — The Lions are _the_ matchup for TEs, as we’ve well established this year. The question is, of course, “which Patriot TE should we trust this week?” Henry came into Week 9 with a foot injury and ran just a 47.8% route share, his lowest in a full game this year. Raridon, meanwhile, ran a route on 41.3% of dropbacks, roughly in line with where he’s been since the start of October with Henry healthy. The two combined for 6/87 on 9 targets against the Packers last week (6 targets for Raridon, 3 for Henry). I suppose the easiest answer is that Henry will be back closer to a full-time role, but Raridon has been making a definitive splash the last couple of weeks here. I slightly prefer the veteran. _**[Stream Him]**_
### **Buffalo at New York Jets**
> Sunday, November 15, 2026, 1 PM Eastern
##### Quarterbacks
**Josh Allen** (Buf) — Struggled against Minnesota last week… but so does everyone. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Geno Smith** (NYJ) — The Jets are 8-point underdogs, so Geno could be throwing. But this is a mediocre matchup by the numbers, and nothing the Bills do schematically stands out one way or another for Geno here. Geno has finished as a top-12 fantasy QB just twice this season, but his weaponry makes him a viable QB2. _**[Stash Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**James Cook** (Buf) — Has scored a touchdown in seven consecutive games. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Breece Hall** (NYJ) — It was obvious Hall was going to be neutered a bit in his return given how well **Braelon Allen** played in his stead, but it was concerning just _how_ neutered he was. In his first game back from injury, he commanded just a 57.5% backfield XFP share (~RB24). For perspective, he was at 73.3% (~RB9) over the first three games of the season before getting injured. He was treated as the Jets’ early-down back. But he also ran fewer routes than Allen, _and_ got no goal-line work. If that workload continues, it will be difficult for Hall to pay off as anything more than a low-end RB2, given the Jets will be underdogs in most games for the rest of the year. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Braelon Allen** (NYJ) — In **Breece Hall** ’s first game back, Allen received just 8 carries to Hall’s 15, but he siphoned off the most valuable work; Allen ran a 39.5% route share to Hall’s 21.1%, while all three snaps and the backfield’s only carry inside the 10 also went to Allen. If I were to have guessed before the game, I would have thought Allen and Hall’s early-down/passing-down roles would have been reversed. If this usage continues, Allen might well have the more valuable fantasy role, but it’s too early to tell. If you need a FLEX, the Bills do surrender the 8th-most FPG to opposing RBs. _**[Stash Him]**_
##### Wide Receivers
**Garrett Wilson** (NYJ) — It looks like Wilson will return from a concussion this week. The #1 WR in the league by average separation score and route win rate, Wilson’s ability to earn targets belies his ability to actually produce for fantasy (see below). And now he has two _other_ elite separators to compete with in **Omar Cooper** and **AD Mitchell**. The Bills have been a good matchup — they’ve allowed a 9th-most schedule-adjusted FPG to opposing outside WRs over their last five — but it is bizarre how relatively little Wilson produces given where he excels. A WR2. _**[Start Him]**_
**DJ Moore** (Buf) — Moore was quiet last week against the Vikings’ relentless defense, but it was the first time this year in his four fully healthy games that he failed to finish as a top-30 weekly WR. And in two of the previous three, he finished as a top-12 WR. The sample size is perilously small, but Moore has had a weird year. When he’s played, he’s been great. A low-end WR2. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Khalil Shakir** (Buf) — Shakir easily crested his 36.5-yard player prop last week, putting up 58 yards against the Vikings’ blitz-heavy defense. Shakir averages 3.31 yards per route run against the blitz, so he’s **Josh Allen** ’s blitz beater. The good news here? The Jets and HC **Aaron Glenn** blitz at the league’s 3rd-highest rate (41%), though that’s nowhere near the Vikings’ league-leading rate. Regardless, Shakir is a viable WR3 if you need him. _**[Stash Him]**_
**Omar Cooper** (NYJ) — Hilariously, Cooper saw 10 targets in Week 9 against Kansas City, one week after throwing up a goose egg in a great matchup with the Raiders. A whopping _4_ of the targets were designed, so it was a conscious effort to put the ball in his hands. Of course, **Garrett Wilson** missed the game with a concussion, and now the Jets might decide to put the ball into the hands of their _other_ receiver who just put up a goose egg, **AD Mitchell**. Cooper is also dealing with an ankle injury, which doesn’t help matters. He’s a fringy WR3 in a relatively neutral matchup. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Adonai Mitchell** (NYJ) — Had a brutal game against the Chiefs last week. Mitchell saw 7 targets. Only 4 of them were catchable… and he dropped 3 of those. It was the second consecutive week that a prominent Jet WR goose-egged us, after **Omar Cooper** in Week 8. Mitchell is 3rd among WRs in ASS and 5th in win rate… but he’s 111th of 114 WRs in drop rate (17.6%). He can get open… but he can’t catch. Nonetheless, he’s a talented guy, and it wouldn’t be the most shocking thing in the world to see him make a couple plays against a defense that allows the 10th-most FPG to opposing WRs. _**[Stash Him]**_
> **UPDATE** : All of Wilson (concussion), Cooper (ankle), and Mitchell (knee) are questionable this week. All of them practiced in full on Friday, but Mitchell was an addition to the injury report, so he clearly got dinged in Friday’s practice. Reading the tea leaves, it feels like all should go, but Mitchell is probably the shakiest of the three.
**Keon Coleman** (Buf) — Coleman has been far better than anyone could have anticipated this year, but on this deep Bills team, he’s going to fall behind some weeks. He’s had fewer than 20 yards receiving in three of his last four games, though he actually tied **DJ Moore** in 1st-read target share in Week 9 (22.7%). Compared to teammates **Khalil Shakir** and **Dalton Kincaid** , Coleman struggles against the blitz — 1.34 YPRR — and the Jets blitz at the NFL’s 3rd-highest rate. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**Dalton Kincaid** (Buf) — Kincaid’s season-low 50.6% snap rate in Week 9 was not too surprising — though he excels against the blitz (2.42 YPRR), the Vikings blitz at such a staggering rate that the Bills got their better blockers on the field more. **Jackson Hawes** played a season-high 58.2% of the snaps, the first time all year he’s played more snaps than Kincaid. The Jets blitz a lot — the 3rd-most in the NFL — but not _nearly_ as much as Minnesota, which blitzes on over 70% of dropbacks. I think this will allow Kincaid to run a more “normal” route share. And not for nothing, the Jets have allowed the 4th-most receiving yards to TEs this year. _**[Start Him]**_
### **Carolina at New Orleans**
> Sunday, November 15, 2026, 1 PM Eastern
##### Quarterbacks
**Tyler Shough** (NO) — After a two-week downturn, Shough was up to his old tricks in the Saints’ last two games, dropping back 41 and 45 times, respectively. He threw just 1 TD pass in each of those two games, but he also attempted a staggering 10 runs in Week 9’s win over the Browns. Their 25.5-point implied team total suggests another 40-plus dropback game for Shough. _**[Start Him]**_

From this week’s [Everything Report](https://www.fantasypoints.com/nfl/articles/2026/the-everything-report-2026-fantasy-football-week-10): “After opening up the year with a +8.4% pass rate over expectation in their first five games, the Panthers have shifted to a balanced attack (+0.5%) over their last three contests (all losses) after sustaining injuries across their offensive line.” Bryce has had to get the ball out quicker, and maybe he’s getting a little more comfortable doing that. With the Panthers narrow underdogs in a softer matchup than last week, he’s a viable QB1. _**[Start Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**Chuba Hubbard** (Car) — In addition to his already massive carry share, Chuba’s averaged a 16.2% target share over the past three weeks as the Panthers weather offensive-line injuries. **Jonathon Brooks** ’ practice window has opened this week, but I can’t imagine he’ll have a significant role even when he gets healthy. The Saints have allowed the most FPG to opposing RBs. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Alvin Kamara** (NO) — Last week, Kamara played the same snap share as **Audric Estime** (48.6%) and ran double the number of routes (26). He’s caught 10 of 11 targets over the past two weeks, which is where he’ll make his hay for your fantasy team if you’re desperate. The issue is knowing what Kamara’s role will be in the event **Travis Etienne** returns in Week 10. Will he keep his heavy route participation from the last two weeks (53.7% and 57.8%), or will he sink into an ugly three-man committee? If it’s the latter, he’ll be downright droppable. _**[Sit Him]**_
**Travis Etienne** (NO) — We’ll see if Etienne is able to return this week. If not, **Audric Estime** would have a viable path to relevance against a Panthers’ run defense that has struggled all year (and led to the firing of DC **Ejiro Evero**). If Etienne _does_ play, it’ll be hard for me to trust him as anything more than a low-end RB2 even in this glorious matchup, because **Alvin Kamara** will likely continue to work in, while Estime could get some work as well after giving the Saints’ run game a much-needed pulse. _**[Stash Him]**_
> **UPDATE** : Etienne HAS BEEN ACTIVATED off IR and is expected to play in Week 10.

Coker does excel against single-high, which the Saints run at the NFL’s 2nd-highest rate, but his usage in their “new” offense concerns me. Coker hasn’t finished as a top-36 fantasy WR since Week 6, but this is a better matchup than some of his recent tilts. _**[Stream Him]**_

The big question is whether Vele’s route share will shrink as Tyson ramps up, or whether Tyson will absorb all of **Bryce Lance** and **Barion Brown** ’s routes. And even if Vele’s route share remains the same, will he become a distant third (or fourth) on the target chain? As for the matchup, Vele averages just 1.09 YPRR against zone coverages, which the Panthers run at the NFL’s 5th-highest rate. We still have him projected ahead of Tyson, but that feels precarious. _**[Stash Him]**_
**Jordyn Tyson** (NO) — Tyson ran just 15 routes in his NFL debut, but was targeted 4 times, catching all 4 for 30 yards, though his most impactful play was unfortunate — he fumbled at the goal line, just inches away from scoring his first NFL TD (on his first catch). It’s a small sample, but we rated him with fantastic separation metrics, so we’re excited to see him as his role grows. But while I view him as a great roster hold, I want to see his role grow before feeling good about putting him into a lineup. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**Juwan Johnson** (NO) — Johnson scored late in Week 9, but he’s run three of his last four lowest route shares of the season in the last four games, and he’s caught a grand total 7 passes for 57 yards in those four games as well. With **Jordyn Tyson** back, Johnson could sink to a distant 4th on the food chain in New Orleans. A mid-range TE2. _**[Stash Him]**_
**Darren Waller** (Car) — I have no idea how we were to project Waller’s 10-target, 8-catch, 90-yard, 1-TD game against the Broncos last week, on a season-high 67.4% route share. In his four games leading up to that contest, he _totaled_ 8 targets, 5 catches, 97 yards, and a score. I’m not trusting it, but if you’re dying, the Saints do allow the 7th-most FPG to opposing TEs. _**[Stash Him]**_

Higbee’s run the highest route shares here, consistently above 60%, but hasn’t ranked inside the top 24 since a three-week run as a top-12 TE from Weeks 3 to 5. I suppose I’d prefer Higbee of these three, but he’s done nothing of note in over a month. _**[Stream Him]**_
> **UPDATE** : Allen (oblique) is questionable. If he doesn’t play, this at least thins out the competition for Higbee and Parkinson.
### **Seattle at Las Vegas**
> Sunday, November 15, 2026, 4:05 PM Eastern
##### Quarterbacks
**Drew Lock** (Sea) — Take these stats with a grain of salt because Lock was dealing with both an oblique and elbow injury on his throwing arm in Week 9. This likely factored into his pedestrian performance: 175 passing yards on 28 attempts, 1 passing touchdown, 0 attempts 20+ yards downfield, and -9.9% CPOE. He was limited in practice Wednesday, which suggests these injuries are lingering. _**[Sit Him]**_
**Fernando Mendoza** (LV) — Mendoza’s first NFL start will come against the Seattle Seahawks, a defense allowing the 2nd-fewest FPG (11.4) and 3rd-lowest passer rating (77.6) to opposing QBs this season. Talk about being thrown to the wolves. All we really want to see from Mendoza here is for him to protect the ball and make smart decisions. _**[Stash Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**Zach Charbonnet** (Sea) — We saw a better look at Charbonnet in Week 9, when he saw a 49% backfield XFP share, 9.9 XFP, and produced 9.6 fantasy points. He commanded 64% of the backfield rush attempts, totaling 77 yards (4.81). He looked good too, churning out 3.69 YACO/Att and forcing 5 missed tackles, but Charbonnet’s upside will depend entirely on goal-line usage. The Raiders have allowed 5 rushing TDs over the last four weeks and 30.7 FPG to opposing RBs (2nd-most). _**[Start Him]**_
**Ashton Jeanty** (LV) — After being benched in Week 8, Jeanty bounced back with a 76.3% backfield XFP share in Week 9 (9.9 XFP). The bigger concern is losing **Kirk Cousins** , whose 10.8% checkdown rate ranked 9th in the league. The whole offense takes a hit, though Jeanty wasn’t scoring on the ground much before this anyway. The Seattle defense has been stout recently, and I don’t have high hopes for **Fernando Mendoza** ’s first NFL start. _**[Stash Him]**_
**Jadarian Price** (Sea) — Price led the Seahawks backfield in XFP share last week (51.2%), totaling 11.8 fantasy points on 10.4 XFP. That was largely due to 4 targets on 8 routes, for 54 receiving yards (83.1% of his total fantasy production). With Charbonnet as the leading rusher, Price’s weekly upside is tied to that pass-catching usage and explosive upside. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Mike Washington** (LV) — Over the last two weeks, Mike Washington has seen 31 carries, totaling just 80 yards (2.58 YPC). That includes an abysmal 4.2% explosive run rate, 35.5% success rate, and 0 forced missed tackles. **Klint Kubiak** gave him a chance to earn the RB1 role, and he completely fumbled it. Does he deserve more than 5 carries a game? No. Will Kubiak still force-feed them to Washington? Unfortunately, probably, yes. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Wide Receivers
**Jaxon Smith-Njigba** (Sea) — Most of Smith-Njigba’s volume-based metrics remained solid with **Drew Lock** at QB in Week 9: 35.7% target share, 47.4% first-read target share, and a 30.0% designed target share, but he was just inefficient for the first time all season with that volume: 1.89 YPRR, 0.074 1D/RR, and 0.45 FP/RR. The game script was weird: both teams combined for 20 total points, and **Jadarian Price** led the Seahawks in receiving yards (54). Smith-Njigba will have a great chance to bounce back against the Raiders in Week 13, who allow the 8th-most schedule-adjusted FPG to opposing WRs (+4.0). _**[Crown Him]**_
**Tre Tucker** (LV) — I’m going to keep this really simple. There is no reason to start a Raiders WR against the Seattle defense with a rookie QB starting his first NFL game, unless you have literally no other options (please check your waiver wire after reading Ryan Heath’s advanced matchup article on Friday). _**[Stash Him]**_
**Cooper Kupp** (Sea) — With Shaheed out in Week 9, Kupp played 81.8% of his snaps out wide. That did not help his production. He totaled 6.2 fantasy points on 7.0 XFP, but his 1.45 YPRR was his highest since Week 3. There’s still just no upside here. _**[Drop Him]**_
**Jalen Nailor** (LV) — Nuh, uh. If we’re not starting **Tre Tucker** , we’re certainly not considering Nailor either (see above for reasoning). _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**Brock Bowers** (LV) — Relying on a rookie QB in his first career NFL start against a top-2 defense isn't an ideal situation. Luckily, Mendoza has one of the league's most talented TEs at his disposal. The Seattle defense runs Cover 3 more often than any other shell (33.0%), and Bowers sees a 42.4% boost in YPRR (3.86) and an 18.5% boost in TPRR (0.32) against it. _**[Crown Him]**_
**AJ Barner** (Sea) — Barner only ran 19 routes in Week 9, but had his best fantasy outing of the season (15.0 points). This was entirely due to his goal-line rush attempt conversion, which I don’t think we should count on week in and week out. However, the Raiders are allowing the 5th-most FPG to opposing TEs this season (17.4). _**[Stream Him]**_
**Michael Mayer** (LV) — Mayer has posted a 63.0%+ route share in each of his last four games, with 5.4 targets per game, 10.2 XFP/G, and 9.7 FPG. Solid production, but Seattle allows only 11.0 FPG to opposing TEs this season (24th), and I expect a measurable volume decrease when going from **Kirk Cousins** to **Fernando Mendoza** at QB. _**[Stash Him]**_
### **San Francisco at Dallas**
> Sunday, November 15, 2026, 4:25 PM Eastern
##### Quarterbacks
**Brock Purdy** (SF) — Purdy ranks 26th among QBs in dropbacks per game, but is the QB3 by FPG. The 49ers added WR **Keenan Allen** at the deadline and could be getting **Deebo Samuel** back this week (it sounds less likely for **Mike Evans**). The Cowboys give up the 6th-most FPG to opposing QBs, and top CB **Joey Porter Jr.** is questionable. The 49ers have a near 30-point implied total. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Dak Prescott** (Dal) — The Cowboys are 4-point underdogs, but in a game with an implied 55.5-point total. This Niners defense is nasty, allowing the 9th-fewest FPG to opposing QBs, but Dak should be dropping back enough to put up numbers in this game… in theory. It’s kind of surprising that he’s merely the QB12 by FPG despite the fact that their defense has been hemorrhaging points. Nonetheless, a solid QB1. _**[Start Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**Christian McCaffrey** (SF) — McCaffrey has seen just 63% of snaps when the 49ers lead by multiple scores this year, compared to 71% (~RB9) otherwise. Fortunately, this game should be competitive enough for CMC to have an absolutely massive role. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Javonte Williams** (Dal) — **Tyler Goodson** had chipping away a little bit at Williams’ role since Week 7 — 25.2% of the backfield XFP. Goodson is now expected to miss multiple weeks with a knee injury. There are no other RBs on the Cowboys’ 53. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Kaelon Black** (SF) — Has double-digit carries in back-to-back games, but **Christian McCaffrey** was playing through a hamstring injury in Week 7, and then the Niners blew out the Raiders in Week 9. I am predicting the Niners to win this game, but not by a big enough margin that Black — still a premier handcuff — is in FLEX consideration. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Wide Receivers
**CeeDee Lamb** (Dal) — From Jake Tribbey: “Since Week 4, Lamb has averaged 13.3 targets per game (a mark that would rank as the most in NFL history over a full season) and a 46% first-read target share (a Fantasy Points Data record over a full season).” _**[Crown Him]**_
**Deebo Samuel** (SF) — Deebo is expected to return this week after a four-week absence with a hamstring injury. We know the Cowboys are a good matchup for just about any player, but especially so Deebo — he’s 25th among 114 qualified WRs in YAC/R (5.63), and the Cowboys have allowed the 5th-most YAC/R to opposing WRs this season (5.24). Deebo’s health is always in question, but this matchup is unimpeachable. A WR3. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Ryan Flournoy** (Dal) — Believe it or not, Flournoy has finished as a top-24 WR as many times as Pickens, but was just the WR47 last week in a game that Pickens missed. He’s a WR4 this week unless Pickens misses again, which would be a bit surprising at this point. _**[Stash Him]**_
**George Pickens** (Dal) — Pickens looks poised to return from a hamstring injury this week. He’s finished as a top-24 WR just three times in eight games played this season, including just one finish as a top-12 WR. He’s received more 1st-read targets than **CeeDee Lamb** just twice all season. It’s a phenomenal scoring environment (55.5-point implied total), but Pickens may be less than 100%, and the Niners allow the 5th-fewest FPG to opposing WRs. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Keenan Allen** (SF) — I don’t know how much Allen will play in his first game with the 49ers, but the fact of the matter is that he remains an elite target-earner. He was consistently target-mogging **Josh Downs** in Indy despite Downs’ far superior separation metrics. Separation won’t be an issue with Allen in San Francisco, given he himself said that **Kyle Shanahan** gets everyone open with his scheme. I expect **Brock Purdy** will fall as much in love with Allen as **Daniel Jones** did… but far more competently. His value in this great matchup with Dallas will be entirely determined by volume. He’s a WR4 but should be 100% rostered. _**[Stash Him]**_

**George Kittle** (SF) — Kittle is earning insane volume (31% target share and 0.62 XFP/RR) when **Mike Evans** is off the field this season. That’s the same exact XFP/RR as **Brock Bowers**. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Brevyn Spann-Ford** (Dal) — In theory, a viable streamer this week with **Jake Ferguson** out. He’s caught at least 2 passes in four consecutive games, but he hasn’t scored a touchdown all year and has topped out at 35 yards. _**[Stash Him]**_
### **Pittsburgh at Cincinnati**
> Sunday, November 15, 2026, 8:20 PM Eastern
##### Quarterbacks
**Joe Burrow** (Cin) — Oh baby, Burrow has been on fire the last four games, throwing for 18 touchdowns in that span. He currently ranks 1st in passing YPG (298.3), 1st in passer rating (114.8), 3rd in CPOE (7.1%), and 2nd in FPG (25.8). The Bengals are also touting a league-leading +15.0% pass rate over expectations since Week 7, and I don’t see them taking their foot off the gas now. I can smell the MVP aspirations. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Aaron Rodgers** (Pit) — Want to hear a fun fact? Rodgers has more fantasy performances of 19.0+ points since Week 3 (3) than **Josh Allen**. It’s less fun when you add in that Rodgers hasn’t had that production since Week 5 and averages 12.0 FPG over his last three games. The good news? Week 10 should force a pass-heavy game script against a Bengals team that's had an unstoppable offense lately. _**[Stream Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**Jaylen Warren** (Pit) — Coming out of his bye week, Warren is the league leader in YPC (5.92) and RYOE/Att (1.54). He saw a 65% backfield XFP share in Week 8 (15.3 XFP), totaling 7.94 yards from scrimmage per touch (compared to **Rico Dowdle** ’s 3.73). I just don’t think Dowdle can do anything better than Warren. They’ll both face a Bengals defense in Week 10 that just allowed 26.7 fantasy points to **Bijan Robinson** in Week 9 and 36.5 fantasy points to **Tony Pollard** in Week 8. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Chase Brown** (Cin) — Where do I even begin? Owning Brown this season has been an exercise in insanity: starting him week after week and expecting different results. Brown saw a 69% backfield XFP share (12.2) in Week 9, but it amounted to only 10.5 fantasy points. Oh, and he was stopped at the one-yard line and failed to punch in his lone rush attempt inside the 5-yard line (poor effort on his part). But this offense is too good to even consider sitting Brown, so you’re stuck with him. _**[Start Him]**_
**Rico Dowdle** (Pit) — Since Dowdle returned from injury in Week 6, he’s averaging 4.42 YPC, a 54.8% success rate, 2.97 YACO/Att, and 0.23 MTF/Att. He’s been very solid on the ground, with a 40.8% backfield XFP share and 9.6 XFP/G. This game has some shoot-out potential, making Dowdle a decent low-end RB2 option. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Samaje Perine** (Cin) — Perine saw 7 of his 11 backfield rush attempts in Week 9 in the 4th quarter, as the Bengals tried to run the clock out on a two-score lead. He’s seen only 1 target over the last three weeks and 1 rush attempt inside the 5-yard line (scoring on that lone attempt). _**[Stash Him]**_
##### Wide Receivers
**Ja’Marr Chase** (Cin) — Chase is back to his game-breaking, league-winning form, with several season highs in Week 9: 41.4% target share, 5.00 YPRR, 0.258 1D/RR, 146.5 passer rating, 1.02 FP/RR, and 31.5 fantasy points. He was fed insane volume and still remained incredibly efficient. Chase has now scored over 30.0 fantasy points in back-to-back weeks, and **Joe Burrow** is playing at an MVP level. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Tee Higgins** (Cin) — I am in awe of the trust and connection that **Joe Burrow** has with Higgins this season. It appears to be at a level we’ve never seen before. If we look at routes where Higgins was “tightly covered”, here is how he ranks: 0.54 TPRR (WR7), 47.4% catch rate (WR3), 0.257 1D/RR (WR3), 73.7% catchable target rate (WR2), 8 contested catches (WR2), and 1.18 FP/RR (WR3). I don’t see their connection weakening any time soon. _**[Crown Him]**_
**DK Metcalf** (Pit) — Metcalf has scored fewer than 8.0 fantasy points in 3 of his last 4 games. He’s tied for the 2nd-most drops in the league this season, with 6 total. The Steelers' pass rate over expectation in Weeks 1-6? +4.6% (6th). In Weeks 7-9? -2.9% (19th). These are extremely downward trends for Metcalf if I’ve ever seen them. Over their last five games, the Bengals have allowed only 13.6 FPG to opposing WRs lined up out wide. _**[Stash Him]**_
**Germie Bernard** (Pit) — That negative pass rate over their last two games has killed Bernard's production: 5.0 targets per game, 28.5 receiving YPG, and 6.8 fantasy points on 6.1 XFP. However, the Steelers are still trying to get him involved as much as possible — his 50.0% designed target rate leads the team by a large margin. The Bengals have allowed the 2nd-most FPG to opposing slot WRs over their last five games (16.5). _**[Start Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**Pat Freiermuth** (Pit) — Freiermuth saw season lows in volume in Week 8: 42.3% route share and 8.0% target share. But he made the most of that limited volume, averaging 3.18 YPRR and scoring 11.6 fantasy points on a one-yard touchdown run at the goal line. The Bengals allow the 8th-most FPG to opposing TEs (16.0), making Freiermuth an intriguing Week 10 play. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Mike Gesicki** (Cin) — Gesicki has 4 performances of 12.0+ fantasy points this season, but three of them have come in Weeks 1-4. And he’s now scored fewer than 8.0 fantasy points in 3 of his last 4 games. The only game in that span he wasn’t a bust was when he ran 38 routes with **Joe Burrow** dropping back 53 times. I worry this is another potential blowout game for the Bengals, but they’re only favored by 3.5 points and the over/under is 49.5. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Darnell Washington** (Pit) — The Steelers have leaned extremely run-heavy the last two weeks, with season highs in 21 personnel (2 RBs), which is probably why Washington has only 15 routes over his last two games. He still commanded 5 targets on 10 routes in Week 8 and scored 12.4 fantasy points on 11.4 XFP. I think the Steelers will be forced into a more pass-heavy script in Week 10. _**[Stream Him]**_

**Justin Herbert** (LAC) — Nobody has disappointed more this season than Justin Herbert. Finding a positive outlook for this offense is futile. Things should be a little easier in Baltimore, considering the Ravens' starting WRs get open at one of the highest rates in the league. _**[Stash Him]**_

**Derrick Henry** (Bal) — Henry went from averaging 22.7 FPG with **Lamar Jackson** at QB in Weeks 1-4 to 10.5 FPG in Weeks 5-9 with **Tyler Huntley** and **Joe Fagnano**. Can **Jacoby Brissett** be his savior? I’m not convinced, but a gunslinger (as inefficient as he’s been this season) may open up the run game. The Chargers are allowing the 12th-most FPG to opposing RBs this season (22.7). _**[Start Him]**_
**Omarion Hampton** (LAC) — So… about the Chargers consolidating the backfield touches going forward. That was a lie. Hampton saw 41.9% of the backfield touches (13) and a pathetic 28% backfield XFP share (7.34 XFP). Hampton hasn’t seen a backfield XFP share over 50% since Week 2. The Ravens have allowed only 21.3 FPG to opposing RBs over the last four weeks (19th). _**[Stash Him]**_
**Kimani Vidal** (LAC) — Vidal ranked 2nd in backfield XFP share in Week (31.4%), but totaled the fewest yards from scrimmage (37) and fantasy points (4.6). I’m tired of this RBBC boss. _**[Sit Him]**_
**Justice Hill** (Bal) — Don’t be fooled by Hill’s impressive fantasy production in Week 9. 12.5 of his 18.2 fantasy points (68.7%) came on a 65-yard rushing TD in garbage time. He has seen 3+ targets in every game since Week 4, and **Jacoby Brissett** ranks 3rd in checkdown rate since 2025 (11.8%). This would be a purely desperation hail-mary start. _**[Sit Him]**_
**Keaton Mitchell** (LAC) — Mitchell led the Chargers backfield with a 40% backfield XFP share in Week 9. He also led the backfield in yards from scrimmage per touch (6.70) and rushing success rate (57.1%). This might convince **Mike McDaniel** to give him a whopping 13 touches instead of Hampton in Week 10. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Wide Receivers
**Zay Flowers** (Bal) — Let’s not dwell on Flowers lack of production the last two weeks. Better times should be ahead with **Jacoby Brissett** at QB. Brissett hasn’t been great this season: QB30 in ANY/A, but he knows how to force-feed his #1 target, and that’s all we need. The Chargers are allowing the 5th-highest FPG to opposing WRs this season (36.9). _**[Crown Him]**_
**Ladd McConkey** (LAC) — I don’t think any player has cost you more wins this season than McConkey. Not only has he been a massive bust, but there are two instances where you may have started him, and he scored zero points. Oh, and another instance where he only scored 2.7. If you haven’t hate-dropped him already, kudos. _**[Stash Him]**_
> **UPDATE** : McConkey has missed practice on both Thursday and Friday with his back spasms. I suppose they could clear up by Monday night, but at this stage, you’d have to have a pain fetish to start him.
**Quentin Johnston** (LAC) — With McConkey sitting out in Week 9, Johnston led the team in route share (79.5%), but only had one reception to show for it (albeit a 58-yard TD). The Ravens allow the 11th-most FPG to opposing WRs, but this is far too boom-or-bust, week in and week out for my taste. _**[Sit Him]**_
**Rashod Bateman** (Bal) — Since **Lamar Jackson** went down in Week 4, Bateman has only exceeded 6.0 fantasy points once and averages 2.5 FPG over his last four games. Bateman has had no issues getting open this season, ranking 8th in ASS (0.150) but 71st in TPRR (0.16). We need to see how Bateman performs with Brissett before we can confidently start him. _**[Sit Him]**_
**Tre Harris** (LAC) — Harris led the Chargers receiving corps in targets (7), first-read target share (30.0%), and XFP (10.0) in Week 9. That amounted to 3 receptions for 6 yards (womp, womp, womp). _**[Sit Him]**_
**KeAndre Lambert-Smith** (LAC) — Lambert-Smith saw 4 targets on 25 routes as one of the Chargers deep threats in Week 9 (19.5 aDOT), hauling in 1 reception for 50 yards. Too volatile for my liking, even if McConkey misses Week 10. _**[Sit Him]**_

**Mark Andrews** (Bal) — I know **Jacoby Brissett** tended to hyper-target his starting TE in Arizona, but Mark Andrews is not **Trey McBride**. Nowhere even remotely close. But maybe…. Nah, don’t fall for it. _**[Stash Him]**_
