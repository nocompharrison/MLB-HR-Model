# 2026 Week 8 Fantasy Football Start 'Em, Sit 'Em Advice
Joe DolanOCT. 31, 2026 1:31PM EDT
Welcome to Week 8 of Fantasy Football Start ‘Em, Sit ‘Em!
I’m the new author of this column this year, so I’ll introduce a new format! I will break down every game by position and player. After listing the player (and perhaps some interesting stats or news I’ve found in my research), I’ll pop that player into one of several buckets, which are as follows:
  * **Crown Him** — This player is an every-week starter, someone you almost never take out of your lineup _(90-100% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Start Him** — More than likely, this player should be in your lineup because of his role, recent production, or matchup, but you might have to think about it just a little _(70-90% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Stream Him** — This player isn’t a weekly starter (at least not yet), but circumstances could lead to him being in your optimal lineup this week _(50-70% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Stash Him** — This player is worthy of being on your roster, but ideally I want to see more before putting him in a lineup _(25-50% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Sit Him** — This player should not be in your lineup because of lack of recent production, a difficult matchup, or injury _(10-25% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Drop Him** — This player shouldn’t be on your roster _(0-10% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_


For simplicity’s sake, this column will focus on a 12-team PPR league with a base lineup of **1 QB, 2 RB, 3 WR, 1 TE, and 1 W/R/T FLEX.** Obviously, different league sizes and formats will change recommendations… and that’s what our [Fantasy Points Front Office](https://www.fantasypoints.com/fphq/import), with its multiple start/sit tools, is for.

### **Carolina at Green Bay**
> Thursday, October 29, 2026, 8:15 PM Eastern
##### Quarterbacks
**Jordan Love** (GB) — Love threw for 274 yards and 2 TD against the Lions last week, so it’s hard to say he killed your lineup, but you probably wanted a little bit more than that against Detroit’s sieve defense (he also threw a pick). On paper, this is a mediocre matchup, but remember that the Bucs didn’t have to throw at all last week against Carolina. Love’s accounted for multiple TDs in every game this year, so he remains a solid start. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Bryce Young** (Car) — The entire Panthers team no-showed in Week 7 against the Bucs (take it from someone who had two bets on them). And it wasn’t even the blitz that really got to Bryce — **Todd Bowles** sent extra rushers at him on just 27.3% of his dropbacks. When the Bucs _didn’t_ blitz, Bryce was just 10/18 for 79 yards and a pick. The Packers blitz at the NFL’s 10th-highest rate, but what will define this matchup is a strength vs. a strength — Bryce averages 0.59 EPA/DB against Cover 2, which the Packers run at the NFL’s 3rd-highest rate. And they run it that much for good reason — they allow -0.30 EPA/DB when in that coverage. Bryce has had stinkers in the past and has bounced back, but the Packers have allowed the 5th-fewest FPG to opposing QBs, and he’ll have a rookie at RT in **Monroe Freeling** given **Taylor Moton** ’s injury. I’m downgrading him to more of a QB2 this week. _**[Stash Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**Chuba Hubbard** (Car) — Hubbard’s RB29 finish in Week 7 was his first non-RB1 (read: top-12) week of the season as the entire Panthers team no-showed. He was mercy-benched in the fourth quarter for **Trevor Etienne** and **Anthony Tyus III**. It was just a crappy game across the board. The Packers have allowed the 6th-most FPG to opposing RBs this year. Chuba’s back on the RB1 radar. _**[Start Him]**_
**Josh Jacobs** (GB) — Despite a poor 3.28 YPC in his season debut, Jacobs ranked 5th among all RBs in XFP in Week 7 because of his massive route share and his exclusive goal-line role. As expected, Jacobs didn’t immediately solve the Packers’ run-game woes, but that won’t matter if his role is as juicy as it was in Week 7. The Panthers have been shredded on the ground multiple times this season, including by the Bucs last week, and have allowed the 2nd-most FPG to opposing RBs. Jacobs is a solid RB2 with the potential to be more. _**[Start Him]**_
##### Wide Receivers
**Christian Watson** (GB) — Watson has fallen below 11.0 PPR FP in three of his last four games, but that seems more fluky than anything else — he’s had 26 targets over those four games, including 23 over his last three. The Panthers have been a run-funnel defense, but the Packers’ strength — obviously — is throwing the ball. Watson also averages 3.45 YPRR against zone coverage, which the Panthers run at the NFL’s 5th-highest rate, and they are still down their top two CBs. _**[Start Him]**_
**Matthew Golden** (GB) — Golden remains a must-start given how good he’s been this year, but schematically, it is a better matchup for Watson — Golden excels more against man coverage, which the Panthers run at the NFL’s 5th-lowest rate. Still, a WR2. _**[Start Him]**_
**Tetairoa McMillan** (Car) — McMillan averages 2.71 YPRR against two-high shells, which the Packers run at the NFL’s 3rd-highest rate. The Packers have also allowed the 3rd-most FPG to opposing perimeter WRs, where McMillan runs 72% of his routes. Like the entire Panthers team, he no-showed last week, but this is a good bounce-back spot. He’s a high-end WR2. _**[Start Him]**_
**Jalen Coker** (Car) — I had a prop bet on Coker last week, and it was just an awful read — the Panthers completely no-showed, and only 1 of Coker’s 3 targets was deemed catchable (he had just 9 receiving yards total). Coker also picked up a shoulder injury in the game, so he’ll be a little less than 100% on the short week. Schematically, it’s a better matchup for TMac, but I do like the Panthers’ chances of bouncing back against a defense that has allowed the 7th-most FPG to opposing WRs. Coker is a WR2. _**[Stream Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**Tucker Kraft** (GB) — The Packers didn’t have a crisp offensive performance against the Lions in Week 7, at least relative to what you might have expected. Their 0.24 EPA/drive ranked just 16th among all teams in Week 7. So while Kraft came through for fantasy with 4/48/1 receiving, it wasn’t the absolute smash performance the matchup suggested he should have. The Panthers are a mediocre matchup for opposing TEs, so Kraft is more of a mid-tier TE1 this week, but a TE1 nonetheless. _**[Start Him]**_
**Darren Waller** (Car) — Waller had a single target in Week 7. He turned it into a 50-yard TD, on perhaps **Bryce Young** ’s only particularly good throw of the day. But he has just that single catch over the last two games, and has run a route on less than half of Carolina’s dropbacks. He’s not roster-worthy. _**[Drop Him]**_
### **Arizona at Dallas**
> Sunday, November 1, 2026, 1 PM Eastern
##### Quarterbacks
**Dak Prescott** (Dal) — In Week 7, Dak had his first multi-TD game since Week 2 (!) against an Eagles’ defense that looked like it had quit. It was a great time for it, because he now gets to face off against a Cardinals defense that has allowed the 4th-most FPG to opposing QBs, and Dallas’ 30.5 implied team total is the highest of the entire Week 7 slate. He’s a strong QB1. _**[Start Him]**_
**Jacoby Brissett** (Ari) — The Cowboys’ defense is still not good, but they certainly have benefited from the acquisition of CB **Joey Porter Jr.** —- over the last two weeks, games in which Porter has played the majority of Dallas’ snaps, the Cowboys are a perfectly average 16th in EPA per drive defensively. Of course, it also helps that they played **Tanner McKee** and a slew of Eagles’ backups in Week 7. So I need a bigger sample to say this isn’t a good defense to stream QBs against. The Cardinals have a 24-point implied team total in the highest overall total (54.5) on the Week 8 slate. Brissett is a perfectly fine streamer, but I’m worried his poor play will portend a benching soon. _**[Stream Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**Javonte Williams** (Dal) — Coming off his best game of the year with 155 yards and 3 TDs on the ground. He’s the RB7 by XFPG and RB8 by FPG, and he’s had 79.8% of the backfield’s XFP, 4th among all RBs. The Cowboys are touchdown favorites. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Jeremiyah Love** (Ari) — Expected to return to practice this week with the intention of playing in Week 8. The biggest question is how the Cardinals will operate their backfield. In the last two weeks with Love inactive, **Bam Knight** has been so much more efficient than **Tyler Allgeier**. Will Knight win out over Allgeier, or will Arizona shift to a useless three-man committee? The Cowboys allow the 8th-most FPG to opposing RBs, but I’m not confident enough in Love’s health or role to recommend him as anything more than a low-end RB2. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Tyler Allgeier** (Ari) — I’ve been talking smack on Allgeier all season here because he’s been so hilariously bad, and that has never been more evident than the last two weeks. With **Jeremiyah Love** out with an ankle injury, **Bam Knight** has climbed up from irrelevance to efficiency-mog Allgeier in basically every department. I don’t think the Cardinals can justify taking snaps from Love or Knight if their goal is actually to win football games. But I’m not sure that is their plan! Allgeier is a handcuff at best, and he might not even be that right now. _**[Sit Him]**_

##### Wide Receivers
**CeeDee Lamb** (Dal) — Has finished as a top-5 weekly WR in three of seven games and as a top-20 WR in six of seven. He’s also been first on the Cowboys in 1st-read target share in given of seven games. _**[Crown Him]**_
**George Pickens** (Dal) — Three of Pickens’ four best games by total FP scored this season have come over the last three weeks. Now, he is clearly the Cowboys’ #2 WR — he’s ranked behind **CeeDee Lamb** in 1st-read target share in all but two games. And we also saw an interesting usage wrinkle last week against the Eagles. Pickens ran an 89.3% route share — his highest of the season — but played on just _60%_ of the Cowboys’ snaps. That came during a week in which coach **Brian Schottenheimer** insinuated Pickens needs to do a better job of blocking. Whatever the cause, **Javonte Williams** had _by far_ his best game of the season on the ground. We’ll see if that’s a blip on the radar or the beginning of a trend. For now, Pickens remains a rock-solid WR2. _**[Start Him]**_
> **UPDATE** : Pickens is questionable with shoulder and hamstring issues, but is expected to play.
**Michael Wilson** (Ari) — I’ve mentioned in this column that Wilson’s role hasn’t changed much since the **Marvin Harrison** trade, except that the Cardinals have increased their rate of multi-TE sets, which has cut his routes slightly. The biggest issue I see? Over the last two weeks, only 8 of Wilson’s 14 targets have been deemed catchable. He’s caught all 8. The Cowboys have allowed the 10th-most FPG to opposing WRs, and Wilson is excellent against their preferred coverage (Cover 3, which they run the 6th-most in the league), but could they choose to shadow Wilson with **Joey Porter Jr**.? He hasn’t done so in a Cowboys’ uniform, but he has in the past, and he’s allowed just 2 catches for 10 yards on 8 targets since arriving in Dallas. Wilson is more of a WR2 until I see him produce more. _**[Start Him]**_
**Ryan Flournoy** (Dal) — Believe it or not, Flournoy has a higher route share over the last two weeks (86.9%) than **George Pickens** (84.5%). He’s still the Cowboys’ primary slot receiver, and he’s benefited from the Cowboys making **Jake Ferguson** a part-time player and **KaVontae Turpin** being hurt. The Cowboys should continue to run 11 personnel at a top-5 rate in the league, which makes Flournoy a solid bench option who can fill into a lineup in a pinch. The Cardinals have allowed a mediocre 15.6 FPG to opposing slot WRs (15th-most). _**[Stash Him]**_
**Kendrick Bourne** (Ari) — Bourne has 20 targets and 13 catches in his first three games without **Marvin Harrison** , but for 126 scoreless yards. The Cowboys have allowed the 7th-most FPG to opposing slot WRs, but Bourne actually averages fewer YPRR in the slot than outside. I also don’t think **Jacoby Brissett** is playing well enough for me to endorse a Bourne start. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**Trey McBride** (Ari) — His 126 receptions in 2025 are an NFL record for a tight end. He’s on pace for 143 this year. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Jake Ferguson** (Dal) — Ferguson has fallen below a 60% route share in back-to-back weeks as the Cowboys increase the role of **Brevyn Spann-Ford**. He was not targeted against Philly last week. _**[Drop Him]**_
### **Atlanta at Tampa Bay**
> Sunday, November 1, 2026, 1 PM Eastern
##### Quarterbacks
**Baker Mayfield** (TB) — It’s hard to imagine a better outcome for Baker’s first start back from his throwing-hand thumb injury, as he had to drop back just 11 times against Carolina in a big victory. But this is a bad matchup for him, especially if WR **Emeka Egbuka** can’t return — the Falcons generate pressure at the NFL’s 6th-highest rate, and Baker has been 3rd-worst among 37 qualified QBs at -0.63 EPA/DB against pressure. I think you can do better. _**[Stash Him]**_
**Michael Penix** (Atl) — Penix has thrown 3 TD passes in back-to-back games, but he’s been very touchdown-dependent — more than half of his 44.5 FPG over that span has come on TD passes alone. Still, he appears to be a pretty “safe” fantasy QB if all you need is floor. He hasn’t thrown a pick since his first start (kind of fluky, since he’s got the 10th-highest turnover-worthy throw rate). It’s not a great matchup, though — Penix is much better against man coverage, which the Bucs run at the NFL’s 5th-lowest rate. Fortunately, the Bucs generate pressure at the league’s lowest rate, and Penix _really_ struggles against pressure — -0.90 EPA/DB, worst among 37 qualified QBs. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**Bijan Robinson** (Atl) — Obviously, he remains a bell cow (especially with **Brian Robinson** injured), but per the [Everything Report](https://www.fantasypoints.com/nfl/articles/2026/the-everything-report-2026-fantasy-football-week-8): “is averaging just a 51.2% route participation rate (~RB10) and a 10.9% target share (~RB14) in **Michael Penix’s** starts this season, compared to a 74.9% route participation rate (~RB1) on an 18.2% target share (~RB2) in Penix’s starts in 2025.” That’s a meaningful decline. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Bucky Irving** (TB) — Irving has a 15.5% target share in **Baker Mayfield** ’s starts this year, which is roughly the equivalent of RB4. I have to admit this is the area of his game that surprises me the most, considering the Bucs signed **Kenny Gainwell** to, ostensibly, handle just that. But Irving continues to look like a league-winning pick at his 5th-round ADP. The Falcons have given up the 7th-most FPG to opposing RBs. _**[Start Him]**_
**Kenny Gainwell** (TB) — Saw a season-high 14 rush attempts last week as the Panthers couldn’t stop anything, but just 1 target. The latter does continue a disturbing trend, but keep in mind **Baker Mayfield** dropped back just 11 times in total. He’s simply a handcuff or desperation FLEX. _**[Sit Him]**_
**Brian Robinson** (Atl) — Expected to miss a second consecutive game with a hamstring injury. If you have the roster spot, still worth holding as a high-end handcuff. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Wide Receivers
**Drake London** (Atl) — Unfortunately, he hasn’t come close to the overall WR2 finish he had in **Michael Penix** ’s first start in Week 3, but he’s still ranked as a top-24 WR in three of the last four, including as a borderline WR1 in two of the last three. He’s 13th of 113 WRs in route share (85.3). He also absolutely smokes Cover 3 (4.23 YPRR), which the Bucs run at the NFL’s 2nd-highest rate. That 4.23 YPRR is the highest among _any WR or TE_ , without qualification — 317 of them. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Emeka Egbuka** (TB) — Missed last week with a concussion. It’s hard to really get a read on what things will look like now that **Baker Mayfield** is getting healthier; Egbuka was out last week, AND Mayfield dropped back just 11 times. He hasn’t topped 63 receiving yards in a single game this year, but that was at least with Mayfield in Week 1. The Falcons give up the 11th-most FPG to opposing WRs, but Egbuka really struggles against single high, which the Falcons run at the NFL’s 3rd-highest rate. He’s a WR2. _**[Stream Him]**_
> **UPDATE** : Egbuka cleared concussion protocol, but is OUT with a foot injury. That puts **Tez Johnson** and **Ted Hurst** on the “Oh my God, my entire roster is hurt” FLEX radar.
**Chris Godwin** (TB) — Godwin was targeted on 5 of Baker’s 11 pass attempts in Week 7, but in true 2026 Godwin fashion, gained just 27 yards on those targets. He hasn’t topped 46 receiving yards in a single game this year, and hasn’t scored a single touchdown. The only reason to even hold him at this point is to see if things get tangibly better with Baker back. _**[Stash Him]**_
**Jahan Dotson** (Atl) — Had a terrible drop against the 49ers last week. He has an aDOT of 16.84, so in theory, if you are utterly dying at your WR3 spot, he should get an opportunity to make a big play. Whether or not he _catches_ it is the problem. He has been a bit of a safety valve for **Michael Penix** when teams blitz, which the Bucs do at the league’s 5th-highest rate — he averages 3.02 YPRR against blitzes. _**[Drop Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**Kyle Pitts** (Atl) — Pitts is being elevated right now by something he has notoriously been bad at in his NFL career: scoring TDs. He’s scored in three consecutive games. But, per this week’s [Everything Report](https://www.fantasypoints.com/nfl/articles/2026/the-everything-report-2026-fantasy-football-week-8): “In the five games **Michael Penix** has started this season, Pitts has run a route on only 61% of pass plays (TE23), with a 15.6% target share (TE16) and just 9.1 XFP/G (TE16). Atlanta was down a WR in Week 7 without **Olamide Zaccheaus** (shoulder), and Pitts still ran a route on only 60% of pass plays.” He’s gone over 50 receiving yards just once this season. Still a viable start because the Bucs allow the 8th-most FPG to opposing TEs, but the role is precarious. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Cade Otton** (TB) — Had a season-low 54.5% route share last week, but it was a weird game in which the Bucs just ran all over Carolina. He plays a ton (94.5% snap share leads all TEs), but he has just 4 receiving yards over the last two games combined. _**[Sit Him]**_
### **Baltimore at Buffalo**
> Sunday, November 1, 2026, 1 PM Eastern
##### Quarterbacks
**Josh Allen** (Buf) — Since 2023, Allen averages 25.2 XFP/G (QB1) and 30.4 FPG (QB1) in games with a total of 50.0 or higher. The total here currently sits at 54.5. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Lamar Jackson** (Bal) — OK, I think _this_ is the week Lamar returns from his high-ankle injury! We’ve been fooled twice now, but it appears he was close to playing last week and the Ravens just gave him an extra week. They need him now; at 4-3, they’re actually in last place in the AFC North. The Bills have been a smash matchup for opposing QBs, allowing the 3rd-most FPG. Hopefully, the three weeks off Lamar has had means his rushing upside isn’t as nerfed as it has been in the past when he’s coming off injury. If you’ve got him, you play him. _**[Start Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**James Cook** (Buf) — In Week 6 prior to the Bills’ bye, Cook saw his best route participation rate (47.2%), backfield XFP share (74.8%), and XFP total (17.9) out of any game **Ty Johnson** has been active this season. The Ravens allow the 5th-fewest FPG to opposing RBs, but Cook is a lineup lock. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Derrick Henry** (Bal) — Welcome back (we hope), **Lamar Jackson**. From this week’s [Everything Report](https://www.fantasypoints.com/nfl/articles/2026/the-everything-report-2026-fantasy-football-week-8): “Henry’s explosive run rate [fell from](https://x.com/FantasyPtsData/status/2066860340487569655) 2nd in Jackson’s starts to 32nd without him in 2025. This year, we’ve seen a similar decline in Henry’s efficiency metrics across the board without Jackson (below), and Henry has likewise averaged 22.7 FPG (~RB3) on 18.3 XFP/G (~RB6) with Jackson, compared to just 11.0 FPG (~RB24) on 15.5 XFP/G (~RB13) without him.” _**[Crown Him]**_
**Justice Hill** (Bal) — Has just 14 carries and 6 targets in Lamar’s four starts this year. His receiving prowess might be needed in a high-total game with the Ravens underdogs, but that’s more in theory than in reality. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Wide Receivers
**Zay Flowers** (Bal) — Has averaged 6.64 yards per route run and .396 targets per route run with **Lamar Jackson** this year. Yes, that says _6.64_. _**[Crown Him]**_
**DJ Moore** (Buf) — Looking to return from his shoulder injury this week. Curiously, the Bills’ _run_ game has been the most affected by his absence, losing -0.170 EPA/rush compared to when he’s on the field. But Moore has ranked in the 92nd percentile among all WRs in YPRR (2.66) and target per route run (0.28). It’s clear **Josh Allen** loves throwing him the ball. The Ravens give up the 8th-most FPG to opposing WRs. _**[Start Him]**_
**Khalil Shakir** (Buf) — Has averaged 12.2 FPG in games **DJ Moore** missed this year vs. 7.90 in games in which Moore was active. His three highest route shares of the season have come in the last three games, which Moore either missed or left early with injury. The Ravens allow the 6th-most FPG to opposing slot receivers, but Shakir’s role isn’t easy to trust. He’s more of a WR4/FLEX. _**[Stash Him]**_
**Keon Coleman** (Buf) — Coleman has scored in three consecutive games — games in which **DJ Moore** either missed or left early. His ascendance has been one of the more underrated stories of the year, because he has given the Bills a viable perimeter WR even when Moore misses. But I’m not sure his role can be trusted when Moore is active, as he is expected to be this week. He’s a desperation option. _**[Sit Him]**_
**Ja’Kobi Lane** (Bal) — Nearly matched **Rashod Bateman** in route share (65% to 67.5%) while besting him in 1st-read target share (15.4% to 7.7%) in his return from injury, posting 3/25 on 5 targets. He’s not worth a start, but I really like him as a speculative hold in the event this passing game takes off with Lamar back. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**Dalton Kincaid** (Buf) — **DJ Moore** is expected to be back this week. I think that’s a _good_ thing for Kincaid! His TPRR with Moore on the field (0.24) is almost the same as with him off (0.23), while his YPRR (and, relatedly, the general success of the passing offense) nearly doubles with Moore on (from 1.96 to 3.71). His route share has been back up above 60% the last two games after a 45.7% blip in Week 4. _**[Start Him]**_
**Mark Andrews** (Bal) — Andrews’ route share has been consistently above 60%, but the Ravens have been going out of their way to get rookies **Josh Cuevas** and **Matthew Hibner** some run — Cuevas actually has the same 1st-read target share (10.4%) over the last two weeks as Andrews. Andrews is still the TE1 here, and it’s a generally good matchup with **Lamar Jackson** back. But he hasn’t hit 50 receiving yards in any game this year. _**[Stream Him]**_
### **Cleveland at Pittsburgh**
> Sunday, November 1, 2026, 1 PM Eastern
##### Quarterbacks
**Aaron Rodgers** (Pit) — Rodgers threw for just a single touchdown against the Saints last week, his first game without multiple TD passes since all the way back in Week 2 against the Patriots. He’s taken very good care of the ball in general — his turnover-worthy throw rate is 10th-best among 35 qualified QBs — but otherwise, his metrics look like those of a 42-year-old QB, as he ranks bottom-10 in EPA/DB, CPOE, and YPA. On the other hand, while the Browns have given up the 7th-fewest FPG to opposing QBs this year, Rodgers posted a season-high 24.0 FP against them back in Week 4. I think he’s a mediocre streamer. _**[Stash Him]**_
**Deshaun Watson** (Cle) — Watson hasn’t thrown a TD pass since Week 5, and now has just 2 over his last four games total. So it’s a good thing that he converted on his first rushing TD of the season in Week 7’s win over the Titans — despite season-lows in carries and yards (3/19). His previous season-low in yardage was 22, which he “accomplished” twice, including against these Steelers in Week 4. Watson’s receiving group is getting strong play from a pair of rookies, but the Steelers continue to be a tough matchup, allowing the 4th-fewest FPG to opposing QBs. _**[Stash Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**Jaylen Warren** (Pit) — Unfortunately, **Rico Dowdle** is going to continue to be a thing. From this week’s [Everything Report](https://www.fantasypoints.com/nfl/articles/2026/the-everything-report-2026-fantasy-football-week-8): “Warren averages a 73.9% route participation rate (~RB1), a 15.2% target share (~RB4), and 15.1 XFP/G (~RB15) in three games without Rico Dowdle this season. With Dowdle active, those fall to a 41.5% route participation rate (~RB20), a 10.0% target share (~RB18), and 11.5 XFP/G (~RB27).” He’s the lead back here — Warren has a 66.7% designed-carry share in each of the last two games — and he’s a must-start. But he’s not the league-winning bell cow he was when Dowdle was out. _**[Start Him]**_
**Quinshon Judkins** (Cle) — Judkins averaged 5.65 YPC in his big Week 7 game against the Titans, his first time averaging even 4.0 YPC in a single game since Week 5… _of last season_. Judkins scored a 21.3 FP against these Steelers back in Week 4 of _this_ season, but that came largely because of an unusual receiving role — Judkins had 7 targets and 6 catches in that contest. In Week 7, with **Dylan Sampson** back, Judkins’ route share fell to a near season-low 21.1%, and he saw just a single target. That’s bad news for Judkins, because the Steelers have allowed the 5th-fewest FPG to RBs on the ground. He’s a TD-dependent, low-end RB2. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Rico Dowdle** (Pit) — Ran a higher route share (55.9%) than Warren last week, and has now had a higher first-read target share than Warren in back-to-back games since his return. Admittedly, Dowdle played well last week, but it’s wild that he’s out-targeted Warren since he came back. Unfortunately for Warren owners, Dowdle has to be considered a weekly FLEX option unless things change. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Dylan Sampson** (Cle) — Posted an incredibly efficient 6/45 rushing and 3/32 receiving in what was, effectively, his 2026 debut. Sampson played 47% of the offensive snaps and ran a route on 39.5% of **Deshaun Watson** ’s dropbacks, nearly double the route share of **Quinshon Judkins**. If you’re dying for a FLEX this week, the Browns are 3.5-point underdogs, and the Steelers have given up 285 receiving yards to RBs, 3rd-most in the NFL. Sampson should be rostered in all formats. _**[Stash Him]**_
##### Wide Receivers
**DK Metcalf** (Pit) — Metcalf had 5 catches for a season-high 115 yards the last time these two teams met back in Week 4. But I wonder if there will be a tactical change for Cleveland this week. **Kimani Vidal** (LAC) — I don’t trust anyone on this Chargers team, but I think it’s interesting that Vidal got 10 opportunities to **Keaton Mitchell** ’s 2 in Week 6 before the bye — he ran only 1 more route than Mitchell (13 to 12), but saw 3 targets to Mitchell’s 0, and also got 7 carries. He posted a season-high 46.5% backfield XFP share in Week 6, and has now run more routes than Mitchell in three straight games. If I had to play a non-Hampton RB here, it’d be Vidal, but I’d also have to be desperate. _**[Stash Him]**_
##### Wide Receivers
**Puka Nacua** (LAR) — Saw 10 targets on just 26 routes in his first game without **Davante Adams**. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Ladd McConkey** (LAC) — Should return this week from a concussion, but he’s been utterly snakebitten. He has started and completed just one full game this year — Week 3. He posted 5 catches for 66 yards on an 84.2% route share in that contest against the Bills. The Chargers are touchdown underdogs in this game and McConkey is pretty much their only receiver who can get open against man, but I am finding it very difficult to rank him as anything more than a WR3. I wouldn’t be shocked if the Rams chose to selectively follow him around with **Trent McDuffie**. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Quentin Johnston** (LAC) — One of the NFL’s cardio kings. Johnston has just 20 targets on 137 routes run… and just 8 of them have been caught. _**[Drop Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**Tyler Higbee** (LAR) — The Rams had 3 TEs — Higbee, **Colby Parkinson** , and **Davis Allen** — run a 40% or higher route share in Week 7 against the Raiders, which isn’t surprising given their WR group had just one player run a 50% or higher share (**Puka Nacua**). With **Davante Adams** down, the Rams ran 34 plays of 13 personnel, their highest number of the season in that regard. In my opinion, Higbee is the best receiver of the bunch and ran a route on the highest percentage of these TEs (53.3%), but it really just feels like all these guys will vulture each other, as Allen did with 2 TD last week. _**[Stash Him]**_
### **Kansas City at Denver**
> Sunday, November 1, 2026, 4:25 PM Eastern
##### Quarterbacks
**Patrick Mahomes** (KC) — After back-to-back outings without a touchdown, **Patrick Mahomes** has fallen to QB17 with 17.3 FPG. Mahomes produced 22.7 fantasy points in Week 1 against Denver but has only 18.5 points combined over his last two games. Denver DC Vance Joseph relies on heavy doses of single-high and blitz, and Andy Reid’s offense has averaged +0.21 EPA per dropback against both since 2021. Mahomes is a low-end QB1 in a close game. _**[Start Him]**_
**Bo Nix** (Den) — Almost half of the Week 7 fantasy production for **Bo Nix** came on a 62-yard, [19.9-mph](https://x.com/NextGenStats/status/2114470361853222937) scramble TD run — it was the longest QB scramble since 2016, and more than his total output in Week 1 against the Chiefs (7.4 fantasy points). Kansas City allows the third-fewest FPG to QBs (13.4), and the Denver offense ranks fifth-worst when pressured (-0.54 EPA per dropback). Chiefs DC Steve Spagnuolo plays two-high shells 64% of the time (second-most): Denver manages 0.00 EPA per dropback against Cover 2. Nix is a low-end QB2. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**Kenneth Walker** (KC) — It was a tough outing for **Kenneth Walker** against his former team in Seattle last week, but Week 8 could be Christmas in October. Denver’s otherwise strong defense ranks fifth-worst in EPA per rush allowed: the Chiefs are first in EPA per rush gained. If Week 8 looks anything like Walker’s 23/173/1 effort in Week 1, where his 34.1 PPR fantasy points were RB3 on the week, Walker managers will be thanking Santa. _**[Crown Him]**_
![](https://www.fantasypoints.com/nfl/articles/2026/week-8-fantasy-football-start-em-sit-em-advice)
**Jonah Coleman** (Den) — It has been a small sample, but Jonah Coleman appears to be the clear early winner in the Denver RB competition. Coleman posts 4.89 YPC, a 52.6% rushing success rate, and +0.83 RYOE per attempt (RB7). The Week 8 matchup with a Kansas City defense ranking in the top 10 for EPA per rush allowed and FPG allowed (19.4) is not ideal, but Coleman looks like a potential fantasy starter in the near future. He’s a mid-range RB3 this week. _**[Stash Him]**_
**RJ Harvey** (Den) — Like Nix, **RJ Harvey** did most of his work on one explosive play: a 52-yard TD catch powered most of his 14.0 PPR fantasy points. While Denver HC Sean Payton sends 24% of his targets to running backs and Harvey is the clear receiving back, Week 8 is unlikely to bring fireworks. Harvey averages four carries a game and is a sit against KC. _**[Sit Him]**_
> **UPDATE** : Harvey is OUT with a knee injury. That upgrades Coleman to an RB2 and Dobbins to a usable FLEX.
**J.K. Dobbins** (Den) — With a 21.4% snap share and 4.0 PPR fantasy points, **J.K. Dobbins** was the odd man out for Denver in Week 7. While Dobbins saw double the carries of **RJ Harvey** (6 vs. 3), **Jonah Coleman** was Denver’s clear lead back over Dobbins on rushing plays (9 vs. 6). Coleman’s snap share was twice that of Dobbins. If all three Denver backs are healthy, Dobbins is an easy sit until further notice. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Wide Receivers
**Xavier Worthy** (KC) — It took me [aback](https://x.com/SideHustleFF/status/2114543877554073862) when Cris Collinsworth said that **Xavier Worthy** could only be contained by one player in Seattle’s **Nick Emmanwori** : Emmanwori is all-world, but almost any NFL defender can contain Worthy. Worthy once again got the looks but failed to produce in Week 7 against Seattle, including a costly fourth-quarter drop. Worthy is WR56 in FPG (8.6) and would likely draw **Patrick Surtain** in outside looks if Surtain returns. _**[Sit Him]**_
**Courtland Sutton** (Den) — With five end zone targets (26% of Denver’s total), **Courtland Sutton** might appear tempting to those reaching into the Halloween bucket. But with 5.5 FPG and 0.94 YPRR (WR93), Sutton is more like the half-eaten candy that you re-wrap and leave for a younger sibling. _**[Sit Him]**_
**Rashee Rice** (KC) — After playing in only four games, **Rashee Rice** could return in Week 8 against Denver. Rice is easily the best short-area target for Mahomes. His 2.32 YPRR ranks in the top 25 (WR22), and Kansas City’s short-passing game is the league’s fourth-best (+0.41 EPA per dropback). Rice may be marked safe from **Patrick Surtain** in his slot role, but Denver allows the sixth-fewest FPG to opposing WRs. Rice is a mid-level WR3. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Jaylen Waddle** (Den) — Although **Jaylen Waddle** was highly efficient on a per-snap basis in Week 7, 24 routes only go so far — he left with a chest injury. Glass-half-full, Waddle produced his third-best fantasy outing on the season; glass-half-empty, that third-best finish netted Waddle 8.8 PPR fantasy points. That low output came despite Waddle operating as the clear leader in targets (7) and air yards (95). The Chiefs allow the third-fewest FPG to opposing WRs, and Waddle is a WR4. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**Travis Kelce** (KC) — Just like old times, **Travis Kelce** may be Kansas City’s best answer in Week 8 against Denver. Kelce is TE8 in FPG (11.7), and Denver allows the 10th-most points of any defense to opposing TEs (16.0 FPG). Kelce drew eight targets last week, and Denver DC Vance Joseph funnels 24.7% of targets to TEs. Kelce is a low-end TE1. _**[Start Him]**_
**Evan Engram** (Den) — The déjà vu effect isn’t as kind to **Evan Engram** , whose Week 7 1/2/0 line reads like malformed computer code or an alt-J song title. Kansas City allows the fifth-most FPG to opposing TEs, but Engram’s 4.7 FPG and 2.7 targets/game provide little floor protection. _**[Sit Him]**_
### **New England at Miami**
> Sunday, November 1, 2026, 4:25 PM Eastern
##### Quarterbacks
**Drake Maye** (NE) — **Drake Maye** and the New England passing offense have posted the league’s best EPA per dropback mark (+0.46) over the past three games, light years ahead of second-place Buffalo (+0.33). The Patriots rank sixth in EPA per dropback on the season and are the league’s best offense on short throws: the Dolphins’ pass defense ranks last in EPA per dropback and third-worst on short throws, and they struggle mightily against opposing QBs and TEs. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Malik Willis** (Mia) — Miami’s first win of the season was driven by +0.65 EPA per dropback by QB **Malik Willis** , who will try to post his third straight outing of 20+ fantasy points against New England. Willis has over 100 rushing yards combined in the last two outings, but he now faces a top-10 fantasy defense against opposing QBs in the Patriots. Willis remains a low-end QB2. _**[Stash Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**Ollie Gordon** (Mia) — Give **Ollie Gordon** his flowers: the Miami Dolphins are posting a positive EPA per play as an offense over the past three games, and Ollie Gordon is a [big reason](https://x.com/SideHustleFF/status/2115227653250723953) why. The Dolphins have the league’s sixth-best EPA per rush in the last three games, ahead of the mighty Los Angeles Rams, and Gordon is posting 4.71 YPC (RB11). New England is a top-12 defense against the run, but Gordon remains a solid low-end RB2 option. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Rhamondre Stevenson** (NE) — After averaging 11.1 FPG over his first three games, **Rhamondre Stevenson** has produced 14.5 FPG over the last three contests and faces the league’s fourth-most generous defense to opposing RBs in Miami. His snap share has risen more than five percentage points over the same period (from 59.6% to 65.0%), and his target share, snap share, and FPG marks on the season are all top 20 among backs. Stevenson is a mid-range RB2. _**[Start Him]**_
**TreVeyon Henderson** (NE) — The extra 3.4 FPG earned by Stevenson over the past three weeks has come at the expense of **TreVeyon Henderson**. Henderson is averaging 4.4 FPG over his last three games (7.2 before), and his snap share over the past three weeks has fallen to an anemic 21.9%. Henderson has produced negative RYOE marks and trailed his expected fantasy output in four of six games. He’s flirting with being an RB4 in his recent run. _**[Sit Him]**_
> **UPDATE** : Henderson is OUT with an illness. He wasn’t someone worth playing anyway, but it solidifies Stevenson as an RB2 and makes **Corey Kiner** a desperation FLEX who might close out a big Patriots win in the fourth quarter.
**Corey Kiner** (NE) — Week 7 was the third straight week where **Corey Kiner** produced more rushing yards than TreVeyon Henderson. That amounted to only 13 rushing yards last week, but Kiner produced +15.7 and +27.6 RYOE in the weeks prior. The former UDFA has finally found some traction on his fourth NFL team in his first two seasons. Keep him on your dynasty radar — Kiner may be the true handcuff to Stevenson. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Wide Receivers
**AJ Brown** (NE) — Although **AJ Brown** has only 13.5 fantasy points combined over the last two games, there is reason to believe a turnaround could come soon. He’s posted 33.4 expected fantasy points over the same period to go with 15 targets (including three red zone targets) and 253 air yards. The challenge for Brown is that Miami is the league’s second-toughest defense against opposing WRs and one of the league’s biggest run-funnel defenses. Brown is a mid-range WR2. _**[Start Him]**_
> **UPDATE** : Hoooooo boy. Brown is now OUT with a knee issue, which is reportedly the same degenerative issue that the Rams flagged when pulling out of a potential Brown trade. It’s turning rapidly into a lost season for Brown. **DeMario Douglas** would be my preferred WR3 pivot here, though **Efton Chism** and **Kyle Williams** will get some looks for those desperate.
**Malik Washington** (Mia) — A top-10 target share (25.8%, or WR9) has yet to deliver fantasy glory for Malik Washington. Those seeking low-ceiling stability may get it in Washington, who has posted 10.2 FPG over the last three games with an 80%+ route share. But 0.33 red zone targets per game, a -0.78 YPTOE mark, and a 10.4-yard aDOT are all outside the top 70 for WRs, and Washington is a low-end WR3. _**[Stash Him]**_
**DeMario Douglas** (NE) — The quiet winner of New England’s competition for a new WR2 has been **DeMario Douglas** , who has stepped up admirably in the absence of **Romeo Doubs**. Although the 5.3 fantasy points he put up in Week 7 were disappointing, Douglas averaged 21.1 FPG in the two games prior. The talent is there, but Douglas is a low-end WR3 this week with a tough matchup. _**[Sit Him]**_
**Caleb Douglas** (Mia) — Douglas is the only other Dolphin player besides Washington with a route share above 66% in all of his healthy games. He had only 3 targets in his return to action in Week 7, but he turned them into 2 touchdowns. I want to see more before trusting Douglas in a lineup, but he deserves to be rostered in most formats. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**Hunter Henry** (NE) — Week 7 was the third straight game in which **Hunter Henry** produced 13.4 or more fantasy points. Henry has run 21 or more routes in every game this season, and his 22.6% target share and 71 receiving yards in Week 7 were both season highs. Henry is a low-end TE1 against the league’s third-friendliest fantasy defense to opposing TEs. _**[Start Him]**_
**Greg Dulcich** (Mia) — A season-high 86 receiving yards powered Greg Dulcich to 13.6 fantasy points in Week 7. Dulcich has at least one red zone target in four of his last five games, and he’s posted a 60% or higher route share in each of the last two. His 23.8% target share in Week 7 was also a season high, and New England is league average against opposing TEs. But 6.9 FPG (TE29) and 0.35 FPRR (TE28) on the season are tough to swallow, and Dulcich is a low-end TE2 in a game where Miami’s offense has an implied total of 18.8 points. _**[Stash Him]**_
**Eli Raridon** (NE) — The breakout game for Eli Raridon finally came in Week 7, with the rookie finishing as the TE7 with 14.6 PPR fantasy points. After posting only one target per game over his first three games, Raridon is posting four targets per game over his last three. His receiving yards/game, YPRR, and FPG have all more than [doubled](https://x.com/SideHustleFF/status/2114889280279511375) over the second half of the season. He’s outside the top 25 for now, but stay tuned. _**[Sit Him]**_
### **Philadelphia at Washington**
> Sunday, November 1, 2026, 8:20 PM Eastern
##### Quarterbacks
**Jayden Daniels** (Was) — The rushing upside is obviously elite with Daniels – he’s already scored 33.3 FP on the ground (QB2) across just 14 quarters of game action. He’s going to have to continue to lean on his legs with WR **Terry McLaurin** now out for the season. As much as the Eagles’ offense can’t help its woes given all its injuries, it’s their _defense_ that has been more disappointing, because they are generally healthy. Philly has allowed the 8th-most FPG to opposing QBs this year, including 17.7 to Daniels in Week 1. _**[Start Him]**_
**Tanner McKee** (Phi) — Look, I don’t think McKee is good or anything, but I’m not sure how the guy is supposed to produce with a dinged-up offensive line, his top two receivers out, and potentially his top _four_ RBs out if **Saquon Barkley** can’t play in Week 8. The Commanders' defense stinks, but McKee is playing almost literally with a practice squad of skill players. _**[Drop Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**Rachaad White** (Was) — With **Jacory Croskey-Merritt** on IR, White handled 61.9% of the Commanders’ backfield XFP in Week 6 before their bye. That was the highest share of backfield XFP from any Commander RB all season. It is worth noting that perhaps **Jeremy McNichols** will have a higher share of carries if the Commanders are in a positive gamescript in Week 8, but White is really the only Washington RB to consider given his actual usage. He’s a back-end RB2. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Saquon Barkley** (Phi) — Questionable at best to play with hamstring and back injuries — Barkley said the back injury was a compensation for his hamstring strain, and he clearly wasn’t 100% even on his extreme load limit in Week 7, playing just 9 snaps. If he doesn’t play, **Elijah Mitchell** would presumably be the next man up for the Eagles, though they did sign **Jaydon Blue** to their 53-man roster. What a disaster. _**[Sit Him]**_
> **UPDATE** : Barkley doesn’t even have an injury designation for this game. I’m skeptical he’ll be anything close to 100%, but he’s on the RB2 radar because Philly has nothing else. The Commanders are down DTs **Javon Kinlaw** and **Daron Payne**.
##### Wide Receivers
**Stefon Diggs** (Was) — Caught 6 passes for 44 yards and a TD in Week 6 against the 49ers, his first game back from a hamstring injury. Diggs has now had an extra week to heal up with the Commanders coming off a bye, and he’ll ostensibly be their top receiver with **Terry McLaurin** out for the year. Diggs has been getting open at a very high rate and has the 9th-highest targets per route run of any WR in football. He scored against this disappointing Eagles’ defense in Week 1. I think he’s a solid WR2. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Makai Lemon** (Phi) — It’s been a nightmare season for Eagles fans, but at least they got to see their first-round pick step up in Week 7. In what was, of course, a very good matchup against Dallas, Lemon posted 7/67/1 on 8 targets with **DeVonta Smith** and **Dontayvion Wicks** out. It looks like Smith and Wicks are trending toward missing again, which makes Lemon a perfectly fine WR3 — Washington has allowed the 6th-most FPG to opposing WRs this year. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Antonio Williams** (Was) — After **Terry McLaurin** went down with an injury in Week 6, Washington ran a four-way WR rotation. Williams led the way with a 74% route share, followed by **Stefon Diggs** (64%), **Treylon Burks** (57%), and **Dyami Brown** (50%). Diggs has been awesome this year, but he’s also dealt with soft-tissue issues, and this is now the second consecutive year Diggs’ team has determined he’s better in a part-time role. Williams scored against these Eagles in Week 1 and has a legitimate shot to lead the Commanders in routes run for the rest of the season. _**[Stash Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**Dallas Goedert** (Phi) — Goedert has 11 catches for 109 yards on 14 targets in **Tanner McKee** ’s two starts. It appears highly unlikely that the Eagles will have **DeVonta Smith** or **Dontayvion Wicks** this week, which should also help matters. My biggest question now is if Goedert is playing one of his final games in an Eagles’ uniform as their season officially looks beyond saving. The Commanders have allowed the 2nd-most FPG to opposing TEs. _**[Start Him]**_
**Chig Okonkwo** (Was) — Okonkwo could be one of the biggest beneficiaries of the **Terry McLaurin** injury. He ran a route on 80.9% of **Jayden Daniels** ’ dropbacks in Week 6, which was easily a season-high, catching all 7 of his targets for 69 yards. The Eagles have given up the 7th-fewest FPG to opposing TEs, but Okonkwo might get enough volume to make up for a lack of efficiency. He’s an interesting waiver add. _**[Stash Him]**_
### **Chicago at Seattle**
> Monday, November 2, 2026, 8:15 PM Eastern
##### Quarterbacks
**Caleb Williams** (Chi) — The unstoppable force of **Caleb Williams** and the Chicago Bears offense meets the immovable object of Seattle’s defense. In a similar physics battle last week, the Seahawks brought **Patrick Mahomes** and the Chiefs down to earth. Williams is QB3 in EPA per dropback (+0.32) and QB2 in accurate throw rate (66.1%) in his four games. But Seattle allows the second-fewest FPG to opposing QBs (11.7), and Williams is a low-end QB1. _**[Start Him]**_
**Sam Darnold** (Sea) — In a strange game of opposites, the stoppable force of **Sam Darnold** meets the movable object that is Chicago’s pass defense. Darnold has a passer rating of 126.4 on passes of 20+ yards, and Chicago is second-worst at stopping deep passes. But Darnold’s 6.8% turnover-worthy throw rate leads the league, and he’s a mid-range QB2 despite a promising matchup. _**[Stash Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**D’Andre Swift** (Chi) — None of the Bears have it easy against Seattle, but **D’Andre Swift** has it better than most. The Seahawks yield the 18th-most FPG to opposing RBs, and Swift is RB10 in FPG (17.8). Swift played a season-high 86.4% of snaps with **Kyle Monangai** out in Week 7, and he’s RB8 in weighted opportunities on the season. Swift’s YPRR against two-high coverage (2.29) is second among backs, and Seattle plays two-high 60.2% of the time. Swift is a high-end RB2 if Monangai sits and a low-end RB2 if he plays. _**[Start Him]**_
**Zach Charbonnet** (Sea) — The situation with **Zach Charbonnet** and **Jadarian Price** remains in flux. Asked to assess whether both backs might play in Week 8, Seattle HC Mike Macdonald [said](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/seahawks-drop-intriguing-injury-rb-120908595.html); "Could both play? Yes. Could either play? Yes. Could either one play? Yes." Charbonnet was the overall RB5 in FPG (22.5) in the final two weeks in 2025, and his return would be a big lift. But it’s worth waiting to slot him into your lineups. _**[Stash Him]**_
**Emanuel Wilson** (Sea) — The looming return of Charbonnet and Price casts a shadow over **Emanuel Wilson**. Wilson posted 24/100/1 in Week 7, and he owns 54.5% of rushes inside the 10. But Chicago allows the sixth-fewest FPG to opposing RBs (18.7), and Wilson is an RB3 this week. _**[Sit Him]**_
**George Holani** (Sea) — Even with only Wilson as competition, **George Holani** never really broke out. He averages 5.1 carries per game, and his -1.27 RYOE per attempt and 3.2% explosive rate offer very little to hang your hat on. Holani is a deep-league handcuff at best. _**[Sit Him]**_
**Kyle Monangai** (Chi) — After he missed three straight games, it is unclear when **Kyle Monangai** will return. When he does, Chicago’s offense will get a big boost. Monangai was very efficient when healthy: he was RB2 in RYOE per attempt (+1.55) and RB1 in yards after contact per attempt (3.25). Monangai is a flex play if he goes. _**[Stash Him]**_
> **UPDATE** : Monangai is OUT. **Roschon Johnson** is a poor FLEX fill-in against this Seattle defense.
##### Wide Receivers
**Jaxon Smith-Njigba** (Sea) — With 28.0 FPG, a 33.2% target share, and a 43.1% first-read share (all first-place marks among wideouts), the undisputed king of the WR position so far is **Jaxon Smith-Njigba**. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Luther Burden** (Chi) — Few WRs have passed the test against the Seattle Seahawks, who own the league’s best defensive mark against WRs (21.6 FPG), but **Luther Burden** may find a path. Seattle lives in zone and two-high, and Burden is seventh in YPRR against both. Seattle bends a bit on short passes, and Burden is top 10 among all WRs in YAC per reception. After averaging 16.7 FPG over his last three games, Burden is the one Bears receiver to profile as a plus this week in a tough matchup. _**[Start Him]**_
**Rome Odunze** (Chi) — The Seattle matchup is less kind to **Rome Odunze**. Odunze is WR32 in YPRR against zone (2.16), and Seattle is in zone 90% of the time. With a 13.6-yard aDOT and eight deep targets, he thrives in the exact areas that Seattle takes away: the Seahawks are first against intermediate throws and third against deep looks. Odunze is a low-end WR3. _**[Stash Him]**_
**Kalif Raymond** (Chi) — When Chicago needs a man-beater, it turns to **Kalif Raymond** , who is ninth in YPRR against man (2.81). Chicago likely won’t call on Raymond against a Seattle defense that lives in zone looks, where Raymond is WR77 (1.17). Raymond has averaged 2.4 FPG in his last three games, including a zero in Week 7. _**[Sit Him]**_
**Rashid Shaheed** (Sea) — We could see a big play this week from **Rashid Shaheed** , who meets the league’s second-worst defense against deep balls in Chicago. Shaheed’s 2.66 YPRR on deep balls is a top-25 mark among WRs, but the challenge is volume. Shaheed has had only five deep targets all season, and he’s merely a desperate dice roll this week. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**Colston Loveland** (Chi) — Although Seattle allows the third-fewest FPG to TEs (9.9), **Colston Loveland** has some strengths to lean on after averaging 14.1 FPG over his last three games. Loveland is fourth in TPRR from the slot and faces a Seattle defense that funnels 31.4% of targets to the slot. Loveland is also third in red-zone targets and fifth in first-read share (22.3%). The usage keeps Loveland in the TE1 ranks, albeit at the low end. _**[Start Him]**_
**AJ Barner** (Sea) — Although **AJ Barner** has run routes on 80.7% of dropbacks over the last three games, he has averaged only 6.2 FPG over that span. Much of that owes to a 2.0-yard aDOT (third-lowest among qualifying TEs) and a 14.8% drop rate, which is second-highest among TEs. But if Seattle leans on 12 personnel, Barner could see some looks: Chicago is among the league’s five worst defenses against 12 personnel. Barner is a low-end TE2 this week. _**[Sit Him]**_
