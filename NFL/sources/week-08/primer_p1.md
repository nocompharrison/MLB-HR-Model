**Strong Starts**
_Here are players that should be in starting consideration for most fantasy teams._
**[Jordan Love (QB)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/jordan-love.php)**
[Jordan Love](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/jordan-love.php) is the QB12 in fantasy points per game. It was nice to see that in [Josh Jacobs](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/josh-jacobs.php)‘ first game back, Green Bay didn’t dial back their passing rate. In Weeks 1-6, Green Bay had a 65.9% pass rate, and last week they actually bumped it a tad higher to 70.8%. Overall, they have the second-best passing rate in the NFL. Among 34 qualifying passers, Love has still struggled with accuracy, ranking 22nd in passing success rate and 28th in catchable target rate, but he is also tenth in hero throw rate and 14th in passing touchdown rate. This week, Love will have a tougher matchup. Since Week 4, Carolina has continued to field a strong pass defense, allowing the 11th-fewest yards per attempt, the eighth-lowest passing touchdown rate, and the 13th-lowest passing success rate. Love has the volume, the talent, and the skilled receivers to overcome a strong pass defense.
**[Josh Jacobs (RB)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/josh-jacobs.php)**
Last week, in his first game back, [Josh Jacobs](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/josh-jacobs.php) was the RB7 in fantasy scoring. He played 53.8% of the snaps with 57.9% of the rushing workload, a 43.5% route share, and a 15.6% target share. Jacobs had an 11.1% explosive run rate, 1.45 yards after contact per attempt, and a 9% missed tackle rate. Those aren’t amazing numbers (outside of the explosive run rate), but it’s a one-game sample, so I’m not freaking out. Jacobs finished with 16 touches and 91 total yards. It was a more substantial workload than I thought he’d get in his first game back, but five of those touches were receptions. His 11 carries were about what I thought he would see. Jacobs should have another strong game this week. Since Week 3, Carolina has allowed the tenth-highest explosive run rate, the eighth-highest rushing success rate, and the seventh-most yards after contact per attempt.
**[Tetairoa McMillan (WR)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/tetairoa-mcmillan.php)**
[Tetairoa McMillan](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/tetairoa-mcmillan.php) is the WR13 in fantasy points per game. In the four games he has played with [Jalen Coker](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/jalen-coker.php) as a full-time player, McMillan has had a 21.5% target share with 2.19 YPRR, four red zone targets, six deep targets, and a 27.9% first-read share. This week McMillan faces a Green Bay defense that has the third-highest two-high rate in the NFL (66.3%). In those four games with Coker as a full-go, McMillan has had a 21% TPRR and 2.67 YPRR against two high. McMillan has a nice shot at a bounce-back game this week against a Green Bay secondary that (since Week 3) has allowed the fifth-most receiving fantasy points per game while ranking 15th in yards per target allowed to perimeter wide receivers.
**[Jalen Coker (WR)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/jalen-coker.php)**
[Jalen Coker](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/jalen-coker.php) didn’t have the massive game last week that I was projecting, as Carolina’s passing offense cratered as [Bryce Young](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/bryce-young.php) fell back to Earth. Coker could have a bounce-back outing this week. In his four full games this season, Coker has had a 22.2% target share with 2.40 YPRR, five red zone targets, eight deep targets, and a 30.2% first-read share. Overall, he is the WR22 in fantasy points per game. This week, Coker faces a Green Bay defense that has the third-highest two-high rate in the NFL (66.3%). In those four full games, against two-high, Coker has had a 23% TPRR with 1.85 YPRR. [Bryce Young](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/bryce-young.php) will have to look like his early-season form for this passing attack to have bite again, but it’s possible. Since Week 3, Green Bay has allowed the fifth-most receiving fantasy points per game while ranking 15th in yards per target allowed to perimeter wide receivers.
**Fantasy Football Flex & Deep Plays to Consider**
_Let’s fill out those flex spots._
**[Bryce Young (QB)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/bryce-young.php)**
Even after last week’s stinker, [Bryce Young](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/bryce-young.php) is the QB8 in fantasy points per game. Last week, Young completed only 51.8% of his passes with 163 passing yards (6.4 yards per attempt), a 69.6% catchable throw rate, and two picks. Overall, Young (among 34 qualifying passers) is 13th in yards per attempt, 19th in passing success rate, 26th in catchable throw rate, and 12th in hero throw rate. If Young is going to bounce back this week, he’ll have to do so against a solid Green Bay pass defense. Since Week 3, Green Bay has allowed the 12th-fewest yards per attempt, the fourth-lowest passing touchdown rate, and the seventh-lowest CPOE. Green Bay during that stretch also has had the 12th-lowest pressure rate, so Young should have clean pockets this week. It’s a good litmus test matchup to see if Young has turned back into a pumpkin prior to Halloween.

**Fantasy Football Flex & Deep Plays to Consider**
_Let’s fill out those flex spots._
**[Ja’Kobi Lane (WR)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/jakobi-lane.php)**
Last week, [Ja’Kobi Lane](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/jakobi-lane.php) was second on the team with a 14.7% target share while having a 65% route share. Lane finished with 25 receiving yards (0.96 YPRR), two red zone targets, and a 15.4% first-read share (second on the team). Lane is a viable flex option this week. Over the last five weeks, Buffalo has allowed the second-most receiving fantasy points per game and the fourth-most fantasy points per opportunity to perimeter wide receivers.
**[Dalton Kincaid (TE)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/dalton-kincaid.php)**
Before Buffalo’s bye, [Dalton Kincaid](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/dalton-kincaid.php) bounced back (some) with his first game with at least double-digit PPR points since Week 2. Kincaid is the TE11 in fantasy points per game. Really, it’s off the strength of those opening two games of the season. Kincaid has a 61.5% route share, a 17% target share, 2.56 YPRR, five red zone targets, three deep targets, and a 21.2% first-read share. It’s possible that Kincaid has a solid game this week, but the coverage structure isn’t in his favor. Baltimore has the fifth-highest rate of two-high (62.4%). Against two-high, Kincaid’s TPRR has fallen to 19%, and his YPRR sits at 1.23. Across their last five games, Baltimore is 18th in yards per target allowed to tight ends while surrendering the fourth-most fantasy points per opportunity to the position. Kincaid has a wide range of outcomes this week.
**[Mark Andrews (TE)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/mark-andrews.php)**
[Mark Andrews](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/mark-andrews.php) is the TE28 in fantasy points per game and hasn’t had at least double-digit PPR points since Week 4. Andrews has a 66.1% route share, an 18.3% target share with 1.29 YPRR, five red zone targets, two deep targets, and a 20.5% first-read share. I have almost no confidence in Andrews at this point, but if you’re desperate, he could have a mild bounce-back game this week. Across their last five games, Buffalo has allowed the fourth-most yards per target and the 13th-most fantasy points per opportunity to tight ends. If he has a decent game, I won’t be surprised, but I also won’t be shocked if Andrews disappoints yet again against Buffalo.
