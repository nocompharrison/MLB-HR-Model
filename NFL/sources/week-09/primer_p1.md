**Strong Starts**
_Here are players that should be in starting consideration for most fantasy teams._
**[Parker Washington (WR)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/parker-washington.php)**
Real talk time. [Parker Washington](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/parker-washington.php) is damn good and has had a bad run, with the Jags blowing out teams. I’m not panicking at all on Washington. Last week, they crushed the Jags while [Trevor Lawrence](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/trevor-lawrence.php) had to throw the ball only 14 times. Against Houston, Washington earned 11 targets and dropped a touchdown. Even going back to the game against Cincy, Washington had to deal with [DJ Turner](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/dj-turner.php) for most of the game, so the disappearing act is understandable. Ok. Rant over. Washington is the WR22 in fantasy points per game with a 22.9% target share (leads the team), 2.47 YPRR, seven red zone targets (leads the team), nine deep targets (leads the team), and a 26.6% first-read share (leads the team). So, just to recap, Washington leads the team in every important metric for pass catchers. This week, Washington gets to face a Baltimore secondary that, over their last five games, has allowed the most yards per target and the ninth-most fantasy points per opportunity to perimeter wide receivers.
**[Brenton Strange (TE)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/brenton-strange.php)**
After last week’s limited volume performance, [Brenton Strange](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/brenton-strange.php) has fallen to TE15 in fantasy points per game. Strange has a 17.4% target share with 2.26 YPRR, three red zone targets, one deep target, and an 18.9% first-read share. Strange could easily bounce back this week as a strong TE1. Over their last five games, Baltimore has allowed the 14th-most yards per target and the fourth-most fantasy points per opportunity to tight ends.
**Fantasy Football Flex & Deep Plays to Consider**
_Let’s fill out those flex spots._
**[Chris Rodriguez (RB)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/chris-rodriguez-jr.php)**
[Chris Rodriguez](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/chris-rodriguez-jr.php) could draw the start for the Jags this week if [Bhayshul Tuten](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/bhayshul-tuten.php) (ankle/foot) is unable to play. If Tuten is active, Rodriguez could still see an elevated role. Rodriguez has had a 24.3% snap rate while averaging 6.7 touches and 33.2 total yards. Among 45 qualifying rushers, Rodriguez is 20th in yards after contact per attempt and 15th in missed tackle rate. If he draws the start, he’s a volume-based RB2. If Tuten plays, Rodriguez is a touchdown-dependent flex. Baltimore hasn’t been an easy team to run on. Over their last five games, Baltimore has allowed the second-lowest explosive run rate, the seventh-lowest rushing success rate, and the sixth-lowest yards after contact per attempt, but there is a sliver of hope here. Over the same time span, this run defense has also had the fourth-lowest rate of hitting backs at or behind the line of scrimmage, and it has allowed the 11th-highest gap rushing success rate (Rodriguez 53.3% gap).
**[Jakobi Meyers (WR)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/jakobi-meyers.php)**
[Jakobi Meyers](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/jakobi-meyers.php) is the WR51 in fantasy points per game with three outings this season with 12.2 or more PPR points. Meyers has a 15.4% target share with 1.85 YPRR, seven red zone targets (tied for the team lead), three deep targets, and a 17.5% first-read share. Meyers is a solid flex again this week. Over their last five games, Baltimore has allowed the most yards per target and the ninth-most fantasy points per opportunity to perimeter wide receivers.
**Concerning Starts & Players to Fade**
_Players in consideration for lineups, but temper your expectations this week._
**[Joe Fagnano (QB)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/joe-fagnano.php)**
Baltimore’s third-string quarterback [Joe Fagnano](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/joe-fagnano.php) will be starting this week for the Ravens. Last week, with injuries to [Tyler Huntley](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/tyler-huntley.php) and [Lamar Jackson](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/lamar-jackson.php), he was pressed into duty. The team just traded for [Jacoby Brissett](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/jacoby-brissett.php), but he won’t have enough time to take the starting job this week. He finished with 21 passing attempts, a 47.6% completion rate, 91 passing yards (one pick), and 4.3 yards per attempt. This preseason, he had some success. Among 62 qualifying passers, he was 22nd in yards per attempt and ninth in adjusted completion rate with a 3:1 passing touchdown-to-interception ratio. Fagnano also had the tenth-lowest aDOT. If he can even be a league-average game manager this week, it should be considered a win for Baltimore. Adding to all of this, he is playing through an ankle injury. He’s a low-end QB2 with a rough matchup. Over their last five games, the Jags have allowed the eighth-lowest passing touchdown rate, the 12th-lowest passing success rate, and the seventh-lowest EPA per dropback.
**[Derrick Henry (RB)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/derrick-henry.php)**
[Derrick Henry](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/derrick-henry.php) might be the RB13 in fantasy points per game overall, but that doesn’t tell the entire story. Since Week 4, without [Lamar Jackson](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/lamar-jackson.php), he has been the RB28 in fantasy points per game, averaging 19.8 touches and 73.6 total yards. Among 42 qualifying backs, he is still performing well, ranking 18th in explosive run rate and 17th in yards after contact per attempt. Henry is now a volume-based RB2 with a horrible matchup this week. Yes, Jacksonville just lost [DaVon Hamilton](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/davon-hamilton.php), but they should still field a solid, if not strong, run defense. Over their last five games, the Jags have allowed the third-lowest explosive run rate, the lowest rushing success rate, and the third-lowest missed tackle rate.
**[Zay Flowers (WR)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/zay-flowers.php)**
In Weeks 4-8, without [Lamar Jackson](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/lamar-jackson.php) under center for the majority of games, [Zay Flowers](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/zay-flowers.php) has been the WR14 in fantasy points per game with two 100-yard receiving days. In those games, Flowers has had a 28.3% target share with 2.31 YPRR, four red zone targets, four deep targets, and a 35.5% first-read share. There are obvious quarterback play concerns with Baltimore now that could persist for the rest of the season. Flowers can still rely on volume weekly, but his range of outcomes is much wider. This week, he’ll need all the volume possible. Over their last five games, the Jags have allowed the seventh-fewest yards per target and the 12th-fewest fantasy points per opportunity to slot receivers.
**[Brian Thomas Jr. (WR)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/brian-thomas-jr.php)**
Last week, [Brian Thomas Jr.](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/brian-thomas-jr.php) remained a limited snap player with a 45.5% route share, an 11.8% target share, and a 20% first-read share. Yes, he caught a touchdown, but I’m not chasing that as a reason to plug him into a lineup this week. Since Week 3, Thomas has surpassed a 50% route share only once, and last week was the first time since Week 2 that he had even sniffed a target share in the double digits. Sit Thomas until the usage picks up.
**[Mark Andrews (TE)](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/mark-andrews.php)**
In Weeks 4-8, [Mark Andrews](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/players/mark-andrews.php) has been the TE34 in fantasy points per game. He had eclipsed six PPR points only once during that span. Since Week 4, he has had a 15.1% target share with 0.81 YPRR, six red zone targets, two deep targets, and a 16.8% first-read share. Volume and red zone equity haven’t been the issue. Andrews just isn’t doing anything with the opportunities. I think this week is more of the same. Sit Andrews. Over their last five games, the Jags have allowed the fewest yards per target and the third-fewest fantasy points per opportunity to tight ends.

Overall, among 30 qualifying passers, Penix is tenth in yards per attempt, 15th in passing success rate, 27th in highly accurate throw rate, and he has the highest turnover-worthy throw rate.
