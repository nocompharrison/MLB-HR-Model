# 2026 Week 9 Fantasy Football Start 'Em, Sit 'Em Advice
Joe DolanNOV. 6, 2026 8:35AM EST
Welcome to Week 9 of Fantasy Football Start ‘Em, Sit ‘Em!
I’m the new author of this column this year, so I’ll introduce a new format! I will break down every game by position and player. After listing the player (and perhaps some interesting stats or news I’ve found in my research), I’ll pop that player into one of several buckets, which are as follows:
  * **Crown Him** — This player is an every-week starter, someone you almost never take out of your lineup _(90-100% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Start Him** — More than likely, this player should be in your lineup because of his role, recent production, or matchup, but you might have to think about it just a little _(70-90% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Stream Him** — This player isn’t a weekly starter (at least not yet), but circumstances could lead to him being in your optimal lineup this week _(50-70% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Stash Him** — This player is worthy of being on your roster, but ideally I want to see more before putting him in a lineup _(25-50% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Sit Him** — This player should not be in your lineup because of lack of recent production, a difficult matchup, or injury _(10-25% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_
  * **Drop Him** — This player shouldn’t be on your roster _(0-10% likelihood of starting for an average fantasy team)_


For simplicity’s sake, this column will focus on a 12-team PPR league with a base lineup of **1 QB, 2 RB, 3 WR, 1 TE, and 1 W/R/T FLEX.** Obviously, different league sizes and formats will change recommendations… and that’s what our [Fantasy Points Front Office](https://www.fantasypoints.com/fphq/import), with its multiple start/sit tools, is for.

### **Jacksonville at Baltimore**
> Thursday, November 5, 2026, 8:15 PM Eastern
##### Quarterbacks
**Trevor Lawrence** (Jax) — The Jaguars are 9.5-point favorites against the (almost literally) QB-less Ravens, so the question is how much Lawrence will have to throw the ball on Thursday night — he had just 14 pass attempts in a blowout of the Colts in Week 8. Schematically, this is generally a positive matchup for Lawrence, but it’s really all about volume. Because of that, I’m viewing him as a low-end QB1. _**[Start Him]**_
**Ravens QBs** (Bal) — _Three_ Ravens QBs suffered injuries in Week 8, and only one of them — **Joe Fagnano** — has a chance to play on Thursday night. Their other options are **Kedon Slovis** and **Austin Reed**. The Jags have allowed the 9th-fewest FPG to opposing QBs. _**[Drop Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**Bhayshul Tuten** (Jax) — The RB5 by FPG over the last four games, Tuten is commanding a borderline bell-cow role. Before **Ameer Abdullah** ’s garbage-time carries, Tuten was above a 70% backfield XFP share in Week 8, which would have put him over that mark in three of the last four games. There’s always the risk the Jaguars will blow out the husk of what once was the Baltimore Ravens, leading to more Abdullah and **Chris Rodriguez** work, but as he did last week, Tuten would be a part of that blowout. _**[Start Him]**_
**Derrick Henry** (Bal) — We know Henry is gamescript dependent, but this week’s [Everything Report](https://www.fantasypoints.com/nfl/articles/2026/the-everything-report-2026-fantasy-football-week-9) says it all: “averages just 13.8 FPG (~RB19) across 18 losses since joining the team in 2024 — with only four performances above 17.0 fantasy points — compared to 21.2 FPG (~RB3) across 24 wins.” And now he’s going to have to play with — at best — a 3rd-string QB. It’s hard to sit Henry, but his runout has been apocalyptically bad, and now he faces the defense giving up the _fewest_ FPG to opposing RBs. _**[Start Him]**_

That could be a recipe for Rodriguez to get some work if you’re desperate — he has 18 touches over his last two games, and has scored in three of his last five. You’ll probably need a touchdown for him to pay off, but it’s certainly within the range of outcomes. The Ravens have given up the 8th-fewest FPG to opposing RBs. Mostly, he’s a high-end handcuff. _**[Stash Him]**_
**Justice Hill** (Bal) — If you’re dying — and I mean _dying_ — Hill’s 23.8% target share led the Ravens last week once **Joe Fagnano** entered the game, and the Ravens are two-score underdogs this week. _**[Stash Him]**_

##### Wide Receivers
**Parker Washington** (Jax) — Washington was a victim of **Trevor Lawrence** throwing the ball only 14 times last week in a blowout of the Colts — he had just 1 catch and 1 rush attempt. The Ravens have given up the 8th-most FPG to WRs, so the matchup is good here, but we might run into the same problem as last week with the Jags two-score favorites. Washington is more of a mid-range WR2, and let’s hope when Lawrence does drop back this week, he looks Washington’s way more. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Zay Flowers** (Bal) — Uhhh… let’s hope the Ravens make a QB trade, because **Joe Fagnano** is unlikely to support quality production from Flowers. From this week’s [Everything Report](https://www.fantasypoints.com/nfl/articles/2026/the-everything-report-2026-fantasy-football-week-9), Flowers: “saw just a 14.3% target share from Fagnano (down from 29.8% in Weeks 1-7) and 0.00 YPRR, compared to 7.84 YPRR with Jackson (WR1 by a huge margin) or 2.14 with Huntley this year (~WR24). In other words, Flowers has survived sub-mediocre QB play this year (18.3 FPG in Huntley’s three starts, thanks mostly to higher team pass volume offsetting worse efficiency), but he likely wouldn’t survive an extended stint from Fagnano.” He’s merely a WR3 this week, but maybe Baltimore will be playing from behind. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Jakobi Meyers** (Jax) — Meyers has surpassed 5 targets just twice this season, has gone over 50 yards receiving just twice, and has scored just twice. Only once have all three happened in the same game. The Jags might not have to throw the ball much at all in this one. _**[Sit Him]**_
**Brian Thomas Jr.** (Jax) — BTJ scored last week, but on his first catch since Week 4. The only reason to hold him at this point is in hopes he gets traded to somewhere like Kansas City. _**[Drop Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**Brenton Strange** (Jax) — Very quietly leads all Jags in targets over their last four games — 30 to **Parker Washington** ’s 28. But because he hasn’t scored a TD over that span, he’s “just” the TE11 by XFPG and TE13 by actual FPG. And like all Jags on Thursday night, the question will be if **Trevor Lawrence** has to drop back enough for any of their pass-catchers to be fantasy-relevant — Strange’s 2 catches and 55 receiving yards tied for the Jags’ lead in Week 8’s blowout of the Colts. He’s a low-end TE1. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Mark Andrews** (Bal) — Andrews has not reached 30 receiving yards since Week 2 and he’s now playing with — at best — a 3rd-string QB. The Jags have allowed the 8th-fewest FPG to opposing TEs. _**[Sit Him]**_
### **Cincinnati vs. Atlanta**
> Sunday, November 8, 2026, 9:30 AM Eastern, Madrid, Spain

**Joe Burrow** (Cin) — Burrow has thrown for 4+ TDs in three consecutive games, 3+ TDs in four of his last five, and has accounted for multiple TDs in six straight. He’s playing at an MVP level. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Michael Penix** (Atl) — I guess we have fair reason to fade Penix in any bad-weather game going forward, after he chucked 4 INT in rainy Tampa in Week 8. Fortunately, the stadium in Madrid where this game is being played has a retractable roof. Penix was obviously atrocious last week, but if you’re looking for a silver lining, the conditions will be better here, the Bengals allow the 12th-most FPG to opposing QBs, and they also play man coverage at the NFL’s second-highest rate, which Penix is much better against than zone (0.36 EPA/DB vs. -0.10). But he’s a low-end QB2. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**Bijan Robinson** (Atl) — I said before the season that if any player in the NFL would have a 1000/1000 year, it’d be Bijan. Well, not only has he not scored since Week 4… but he’s barely getting targets. That needs to change. The Falcons are narrow underdogs, but the Bengals surrender the 9th-fewest FPG to opposing RBs. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Chase Brown** (Cin) — In Week 8, posted top-three marks in XFP (20.3), backfield XFP share (83.0%), and target share (18.8%), and he still ranks top-10 at the position in all those categories on the year. Brown also had his first explosive run since Week 3, his best fantasy score since Week 4 (17.1), and, frustratingly, a 20-yard receiving TD called back for holding. It was an unexpected gamescript, of course, with the Titans jumping out to an early lead. The Falcons allow the 5th-most FPG to opposing RBs and just got run over by the Bucs in a game in which Tampa didn’t even try to throw. Not great! Brown is a low-end RB1. _**[Start Him]**_
**Samaje Perine** (Cin) — His 17% XFP share in Week 8 was his third-lowest of the year. Will probably have to fall into the end zone to pay off playing him. A high-end handcuff. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Wide Receivers
**Ja’Marr Chase** (Cin) — Has scored in five of his last six games and just had his first 100-yard game of the 2026 season. **Joe Burrow** is playing at an MVP level. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Tee Higgins** (Cin) — Higgins is 4th among all WRs in YPRR, 5th in FPG, and 10th in XFPG. That’s with just an average red-zone target rate (47th). He’s looking like _the_ league-winning 3rd-round fantasy pick. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Drake London** (Atl) — Yeah, I think I’m done betting London props for a bit, as my “over” on his line literally washed out in awful Tampa conditions last week. But last week was the first time London fell outside the weekly top 36 in **Michael Penix** ’s six starts this year, and clearly, there were extenuating circumstances. I do worry he’ll catch at least a partial shadow from **DJ Turner** , who has allowed just 11/109/1 in his primary coverage this year — and the TD came when another Bengal defender ran into him. More of a WR2 this week. _**[Start Him]**_
**Jahan Dotson** (Atl) — Has exceeded 50 receiving yards in three of his last four games, but the Bengals are a slot funnel — they allow the 3rd-fewest FPG to opposing perimeter WRs. Dotson runs 78% of his routes from the outside. _**[Drop Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**Mike Gesicki** (Cin) — Gesicki’s 71.7% route share in Week 8 against the Titans was _by far_ his highest of the season — he hadn’t exceeded even 60% in any game this year. So while the fact that he’s scored in five straight games and six of his last seven was undoubtedly fluky, if this usage increase holds, he’s absolutely a viable fantasy TE with a QB playing at an MVP level. Even after holding the Bucs without a single completion last week, the Falcons have allowed the 14th-most FPG to opposing TEs. _**[Stash Him]**_
**Kyle Pitts** (Atl) — Pitts scored in three straight games prior to Week 8, which was a complete and utter washout in Tampa, and not a game I’m willing to make sweeping conclusions based on (outside of the fact that I want to fade **Michael Penix** in the rain). He had 4 targets in Week 8, catching 2 for 6 yards. The Bengals were the worst defense against opposing TEs in 2025 and have not been much better in 2026 — 8th-most FPG allowed to them. Pitts also averages 2.48 yards per route run against man coverage vs. 0.94 against zone coverage — the Bengals run man at the NFL’s 2nd-highest rate. A viable TE1. _**[Stream Him]**_
### **Cleveland at New Orleans**
> Sunday, November 8, 2026, 1 PM Eastern
##### Quarterbacks
> **UPDATE** : **Deshaun Watson** is in concussion protocol and looks unlikely to clear it. If he doesn’t go, the checkdown-happy **Shedeur Sanders** will start. That would mean upgrades for both **Harold Fannin** and **Dylan Sampson** , and a decent downgrade for **Denzel Boston**.
**Tyler Shough** (NO) — Finished as a QB1 in just one of his last four games prior to the bye after ranking in the top 8 in each of the first three games of the season. The Saints clearly want to run the ball better coming out of the bye, obvious by the coaching changes they’ve made, so Shough might not get the 40+ dropbacks he needs to put up fantasy-relevant numbers. On the other hand, a functional run game might make him more efficient, and he could get rookie WR **Jordyn Tyson** into the lineup as early as this week. It’s a tough matchup; the Browns allow the 7th-fewest FPG to opposing QBs. More of a high-end QB2. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Deshaun Watson** (Cle) — After some “competent” play early in the season — more than I expected from Watson, to be fair — his metrics have started to plummet. Watson hasn’t thrown a touchdown pass since Week 5. Over the three weeks since, Watson is 9th worst among 32 qualified QBs (40 dropbacks) in EPA/DB and 5th-worst in pressure-to-sack ratio. Not surprisingly, he’s also tied for the NFL “lead” in sacks we’ve charted as being the QB’s fault over that span (6). He’s also just QB24 in FPG in those three games. He has some really exciting young WRs, but his play just hasn’t been good. You need to hope for some rushing production to make playing him worth it. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**Quinshon Judkins** (Cle) — Judkins hasn’t reached 60% of the backfield XFP share in Cleveland in either of the two games he’s played since **Dylan Sampson** returned to the lineup — his lowest share in the first five games of the season was at 62.5%. On the plus side, his 84.6% designed carry share in Week 8 was his highest of the year — on the downside, he averaged just 2.27 YPC. A large part of Judkins’ role shrinking was because the Browns were down big all game to the Steelers, but the role could continue to shrink if he plods his way to 3.0 YPC or less, as he is often wont to do. The Saints give up the league’s most FPG to opposing RBs, and this 2.5-point spread suggests it’ll be a close enough game to get carries, at least. A “meh” RB2. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Alvin Kamara** (NO) — If **Travis Etienne** doesn’t return from IR this week, Kamara is the only Saint back you can even reasonably consider. Sure, he had just 6 catches for 5 yards against the Steelers in Week 7, but he’s still a viable checkdown option, with 9/92 receiving on 11 targets over his last two games. Still, he’s nothing more than a mid-range RB3/FLEX. _**[Stash Him]**_
**Dylan Sampson** (Cle) — If you need to put Sampson in a lineup, hope the Browns are playing from behind, as they did against Pittsburgh last week. Sampson had just 1 carry, but **Deshaun Watson** spammed checkdowns to him in the 4th quarter — he finished with 6/62 receiving on 6 targets. The Saints are a run-funnel defense, but they have also allowed the 10th-most FPG to RBs through the air. A low-end RB3. _**[Stash Him]**_
**Travis Etienne** (NO) — Eligible to return this week from a hamstring injury, but plugged-in reporters suggest he’s not yet ready. The Saints know their run game isn’t working — they fired run-game coordinator **TJ Paganetti** — but they probably need Etienne to return to reach even a baseline level of competence. **Kendre Miller** has just 76 yards on 28 carries (2.71 YPC) over his last two games. I don’t want Miller in my lineup even if Etienne doesn’t play. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Wide Receivers
**Chris Olave** (NO) — Hasn’t been the volume god his first four games of the year suggested, but he still has 23 targets and 14 receptions over his last three games. Olave absolutely destroys single-high — 3.03 YPRR — and the Browns run the shell at the league’s highest rate. _**[Crown Him]**_
**KC Concepcion** (Cle) — Well, at least I got _something_ right about the Browns’ WRs last week — based on the Steelers’ perimeter-funnel tendencies, I projected a quiet game for Concepcion (let’s not talk about the **Denzel Boston** prop I made). KCC had 3 catches for 37 yards on 3 targets, a season-low target number in any healthy game. But in true **Todd Monken** fashion, Concepcion had a designed target; the Browns simply want the ball in his hands. The good news for Concepcion is the Saints are more vulnerable in the slot, allowing the 9th-most FPG to opposing slot WRs. He’s a viable WR3. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Denzel Boston** (Cle) — I loved Boston against the Steelers’ perimeter-funnel defense last week, and if you were going to tell me he’d get 11 targets, I would have taken out a second mortgage to ladder his prop (at the time I got it, 39.5 yards). Of course, **Deshaun Watson** was a mess in the game, and Boston turned his 11 targets into just 37 yards. While it’s not as extreme as either of the previous two weeks, this matchup suggests it’s more of a Concepcion game — the Saints allow the 9th-most FPG to opposing slot WRs, but just the 16th-most to perimeter WRs. Boston is a low-end WR3. _**[Stash Him]**_
**Devaughn Vele** (NO) — Vele remains the overall WR3 (THREE!!) in routes per game, at 37.4. But his status as a viable lineup-filling option is precarious right now, because rookie **Jordyn Tyson** might make his NFL debut in Week 9. If Tyson doesn’t play, Vele remains a solid if unspectacular, TD-dependent FLEX option. If Tyson plays, I’m waiting and seeing on this rotation. Obviously, add Tyson in all formats where he’s available. _**[Stash Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**Harold Fannin** (Cle) — **Deshaun Watson** isn’t playing well, but at least he’s back to realizing he has a _really good_ tight end. After back-to-back 1-catch games in Weeks 5 and 6, Fannin has a combined 16 catches for 245 yards on 19 targets over his last two contests. And for the first time all year in Week 8, he led the Browns in 1st-read target share. The Saints allow the 6th-most FPG to opposing TEs, and they use Cover 3 at the league’s highest rate — Fannin averages 2.80 YPRR against Cover 3. It’s a smash spot, and his upside is just too high for me to worry about his random no-shows. _**[Start Him]**_
**Juwan Johnson** (NO) — Johnson looked like a legitimate TE1 for about a month, not a bad result for someone who was available in the last round of fantasy drafts in August. But over the Saints’ last three games prior to the bye, he posted just 5/23 receiving on 7 targets combined. He has fallen below a 60% route share in two straight games, and the Saints appear to want to mix-and-match their TEs because Johnson is such a poor blocker. Over those two games, he was 4th at best on the club in first-read target share, and he’s been above 3rd just once all year. If **Jordyn Tyson** makes his debut this week, Johnson might be even further buried. He’s a hold just in case those three games were more of a blip, but I’m concerned. _**[Stash Him]**_
### **Dallas at Indianapolis**
> Sunday, November 8, 2026, 1 PM Eastern
##### Quarterbacks
**Dak Prescott** (Dal) — The Colts are tanking, and this _should_ be a spot for Dak to smash, but consider me puzzled that he is merely QB12 on the year despite the Cowboys having the league’s worst defense. He’s thrown for just 1 TD in five of his last six games. The other concern is that — like the Jaguars last week — the Cowboys are heavy favorites and could just smash the ball down the Colts’ throats with **Javonte Williams**. Still, I’ll play the odds and use Dak as a QB1. _**[Start Him]**_
**Daniel Jones** (Ind) — We have Jones as a low-end QB2 this week despite the glorious matchup. That’s because Jones’ coach **Shane Steichen** has to confirm Jones is the Colts’ starting QB every week after he plays poorly. Last week, **Jacoby Brissett** was a recommended streamer against this Cowboys defense because of the matchup. Brissett played poorly and got benched, opening the door for **Gardner Minshew** to put up the numbers we thought Brissett would. Could the same happen here with Dimes and **Anthony Richardson**? _**[Drop Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**Jonathan Taylor** (Ind) — As of publication, Taylor is still a Colt, which limits his fantasy upside. On the other hand, he faces a Cowboys defense that just allowed **Tyler Allgeier** to run his way to NFC Offensive Player of the Week. You read that correctly — _**Tyler Allgeier**_. If Taylor is in a Colts uniform on Sunday, you’re still starting him. _**[Start Him]**_
**Javonte Williams** (Dal) — Williams had 25 touches in Week 8 against the Cardinals, the second consecutive week he’s touched the ball that many times, but he gained just 57 scoreless yards on those touches. He’s been middling at best in efficiency metrics despite his elite volume, which is why he ranks RB6 in XFPG but RB12 in actual FPG. But I’m not going to get too far into the weeds on these metrics given this spot. The Cowboys are massive favorites, and the Colts have traded away their best defensive lineman (**DeForest Buckner**) and just allowed 44.0 FP to Jaguar RBs a week ago. _**[Start Him]**_
##### Wide Receivers
**CeeDee Lamb** (Dal) — This Cowboys offense just hasn’t been as explosive as we thought, especially given how bad the defense has been, but Lamb has 38 targets over his last three games (which doesn't even include his 21-target Week 4 tilt). _**[Crown Him]**_
**George Pickens** (Dal) — Pickens ran just a 62.2% route share in Week 8 as he battled through shoulder and hamstring injuries — that put him tied with **Ryan Flournoy** and behind TE **Brevyn Spann-Ford**. Yikes. Pickens has 4 or fewer catches in four of 8 games this year, has scored just twice, and has just one 100-yard game. The Cowboys seem to be giving off signal that they want the ball in his hands more (see **Jerry Jones** ’ comments below), but until proven otherwise, Pickens is a volatile WR2. Dallas is coming off its two run-heaviest games of the year, and is a big favorite in Week 9 against the hapless Colts. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Keenan Allen** (Ind) — Allen appears to be the only WR **Daniel Jones** trusts. Since Week 3, he is _19th_ among WRs in targets per game despite ranking 44th in route share, and that’s even with him not separating — he’s 72nd of 108 WRs in both ASS and win rate. Nonetheless, his volume has put him on the cusp of a WR2, and this week, he faces off against a defense allowing the 7th-most FPG to opposing WRs. The key here, of course, is two-fold. He needs Jones to actually play well, and he needs to avoid the coverage of **Joey Porter Jr.** The Cowboys have not been shadowing JPJ, but he’s been incredible this year, allowing just 3 catches for 19 yards in his primary coverage since landing in Big D. He’s more of a WR3. _**[Stash Him]**_
**Josh Downs** (Ind) — Hilariously, Downs ran a season-high 91.9% route share and scored his first TD of the year the week _after_ he requested a trade from the Colts. But otherwise, it was the same old — Downs was getting open (he’s still #4 among all WRs in separation score), and he wasn’t getting targeted (just 5 targets). Please, someone rescue him from this environment. He’s more of a WR4/FLEX until that (hopefully) happens. _**[Stash Him]**_
**Ryan Flournoy** (Dal) — Ran a season-low 62.2% route share in Week 8, and there’s a chance that falls when **KaVontae Turpin** returns to the lineup. Flournoy has exactly four targets in four of his last five games, which usually won’t be enough to get it done for fantasy. But he scored in Week 8 to make up for the lack of volume. He’s a good hold because he could potentially explode if Lamb or Pickens misses time, but he’s really just a back-of-lineup injury or bye filler otherwise. _**[Stash Him]**_
##### Tight Ends
**Tyler Warren** (Ind) — Turned 10 targets into just 4 catches for 16 yards last week — somehow, only 60% of those targets were deemed catchable despite a hilariously low 4.90 aDOT. That just speaks to how badly **Daniel Jones** is playing. Nonetheless, the volume Warren gets makes up for the lack of efficiency — he’s the TE5 by both XFPG and FPG. It’s hard to sit a guy with that kind of role. _**[Start Him]**_
**Brevyn Spann-Ford** (Dal) — Spann-Ford is not worth rostering in anything but dynasty or deep TE-premium formats, but it’s worth noting he ran a 67.6% route share with **Jake Ferguson** out last week, and Ferguson had already been leaking oil with this coaching staff because of his poor play. Keep an eye on this situation. _**[Drop Him]**_
### **Denver at Carolina**
> Sunday, November 8, 2026, 1 PM Eastern
##### Quarterbacks
**Bo Nix** (Den) — After a generally strong performance against the Chiefs last week — his first game with multiple TD passes since Week 3 — Nix now has back-to-back QB1 performances for the first time all year (and just three total). But how much of that was a real difference in play style? Nix went 20/32 for 222 yards with 2 TD passes, but his 4.36 aDOT was actually his second-lowest of the season (after Week 1, also against the Chiefs). Moreover, Nix gained just 4 rushing yards, the seventh time in eight games he’s run for 10 yards or fewer (he had a fluky long scramble TD in Week 7). All this is to say I’m not 100% sure Nix’s play is anything approaching “good,” and I’m still treating him as a mediocre QB streamer in a neutral matchup. _**[Stash Him]**_
**Bryce Young** (Car) — Is Bryce the 2026 version of **Drake Maye** — a schedule merchant? From this week’s [Everything Report](https://www.fantasypoints.com/nfl/articles/2026/the-everything-report-2026-fantasy-football-week-9): “Young has posted his two worst yardage totals and taken 11 combined sacks in back-to-back tough matchups against Tampa (second-fewest EPA/DB allowed) and Green Bay (fifth-fewest). While Young remains a matchup-based streamer in 1-QB leagues, his play as of late has got me a little concerned about **Tetairoa McMillan** and **Jalen Coker** ’s rest-of-season outlook.” The problem for this week? The Broncos are also a bottom-10 matchup by EPA/DB. I think he’s more of a decent QB2 in superflex. _**[Stash Him]**_
##### Running Backs
**Chuba Hubbard** (Car) — Had an absurd 98.1% share of the backfield’s XFP in Week 8. That’s the 6th-most of any RB in any game this year. An unimpeachable role. The Broncos have given up the 10th-most FPG to opposing RBs. _**[Crown Him]**_
**Jonah Coleman** (Den) — Last week against the Chiefs, with **RJ Harvey** on IR and **JK Dobbins** ineffective, Coleman posted 61% of backfield carries (including five of five inside the 10), and scoring 20.4 fantasy points on 21.9 XFP (3rd-most on the week). It truly appears he’s taken over this backfield — he has both the early-down work and the passing-down work (50% route share). The Broncos could always trade for **Jonathan Taylor** and **Sean Payton** could always be up to his other shenanigans, but Coleman allows them to do more things than Dobbins does. A solid RB2 against a defense that allows the 2nd-most FPG to opposing RBs. _**[Start Him]**_
**J.K. Dobbins** (Den) — Played just 1 snap in the second half of last week’s game against the Chiefs. He’s topped 50 rushing yards just once. And with just 27 receiving yards on 37 routes this season, Dobbins might be the most touchdown-dependent player in fantasy football. The bad news in that regard? **Jonah Coleman** got every Broncos red-zone carry last week. It’s a great matchup, but Dobbins has no upside. _**[Sit Him]**_
##### Wide Receivers
**Tetairoa McMillan** (Car) — McMillan runs 76% of his routes from the perimeter — 65% from the LWR alignment. Over the last two years, he’s averaged 9.71 FPG when Wentz takes 5 or more snaps, and 8.22 when he doesn’t. But unlike **Justin Jefferson** and **Jordan Addison** , he was actually starting to build some momentum with **Kyler Murray** — he has been the TE8 by XFPG. _**[Stream Him]**_
**Dalton Kincaid** (Buf) — Had his first monster game since Week 2 last week, posting 4/86/1 on 6 targets. He’s still the YPRR king among TEs, ranking #1 at 2.75. Like **Khalil Shakir** , Kincaid is a big-time producer against the blitz, averaging 2.62 YPRR when **Josh Allen** has extra defenders sent his way (league average 1.47). Unfortunately, the Vikings allow the fewest FPG to TEs, often because teams will have to keep them in to block against their pressures. _**[Stash Him]**_
