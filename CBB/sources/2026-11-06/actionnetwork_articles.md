As mentioned in the title, "Fade Hot Shooting" is a fade system. In college basketball, teams coming off unusually efficient shooting performances tend to regress in their next game, especially when playing again within a short turnaround.
When a team posts a high field goal rate and strong offensive efficiency, the market often overvalues that performance, assuming it reflects a sustained offensive rhythm.
In reality, hot shooting is rarely repeatable over consecutive games, particularly with limited rest and quick travel.
Fatigue, tighter defensive focus from opponents, and natural shooting variance contribute to a decline in scoring consistency.
Fading teams in this situation captures value against inflated lines built on temporary offensive spikes rather than sustainable play quality.

### Michigan Basketball
There will be parts of [Michigan](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/michigan-wolverines) basketball that look an awful lot like last season's defending national champions did. That's specifically true in the backcourt, where Elliot Cadeau and Trey McKenney make up one of the best duos the sport has to offer.
Add Roddy Gayle Jr. into it, and Michigan will start three players who closed the game against UConn when confetti started falling in the national title game last April.
But there's a lot more that will feel brand new for Michigan. Replacing three lottery picks in one frontcourt is as hard as it sounds.
Moustapha Thiam replaces Aday Mara theoretically, but the talented [Cincinnati](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/cincinnati-bearcats) transfer floats to the perimeter too often and still has a lot to prove in the physicality department.
Thiam missed Michigan's opener, and his status is unknown coming into this game. J.P. Estrella will try to help in Morez Johnson Jr.'s old role, but Estrella hasn't looked fully comfortable playing more on the perimeter offensively.
Undeniably though, Michigan's biggest loss will be felt on the sidelines. Dusty May built most of this roster, but his magic fairy dust went with him to Dallas, leaving Mike Boynton to pick up the pieces.
He did as good of a job at player retention in mid-June as anyone could have hoped, but this will be quite a test for Boynton against the best X's and O's coach in all of college basketball.
Michigan will need star performances from all of its starters, and it'll have to avoid foul trouble too.
Michigan doesn't project as a deep team this season, with the loss of projected starter Brandon McCoy Jr. for the season. The deeper into the bench this game goes, the more the advantage belongs to UConn.

### UConn Basketball
Call me crazy, but this may be the best roster Dan Hurley has built at [UConn](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/connecticut-huskies) yet. There's no 7-foot-2 Donovan Clingan erasing everything at the rim, but there are plus athletes and NBA skillsets everywhere you look.
In the backcourt, Silas Demary Jr. and Braylon Mullins both possess first-team All-American upside. Demary, who was hobbled in the National Championship game and seeking revenge, is particularly a nightmare matchup for Cadeau physically.
Mullins turned down being a mid-first round pick in the NBA draft for more ruthless winning.
But UConn's true game wrecker is a name that will be new to college fans this season: freshman wing Colben Landrew. Landrew is built like a linebacker and can guard all five positions. He's a singular defensive force, the likes of which UConn hasn't had on the perimeter since Stephon Castle.
The swing piece for the Huskies is their five-man, Najai Hines. Hines is a physical specimen and a freak shot blocker, but he has to avoid foul trouble.
Oskar Giltay backs him up and is serviceable, but there's no question that Hines staying on the court is the key to UConn hitting its ceiling. Hines is particularly a tough matchup for Thiam, who has a tendency to get pushed off the block by stronger bigs.
UConn can survive foul trouble anywhere else. Between Nik Khamenia, Jayden Ross, Junior County and more on the deep bench, at times, this roster feels like it has two of everything.

## UMass vs Boston College Basketball Preview
![Header First Logo](https://static.sprtactn.co/teamlogos/ncaab/100/umass.png)
### UMass Basketball
The [UMass](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/massachusetts-minutemen) Minutemen looked mighty impressive at the start of the season. They held a solid [Troy](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/troy-trojans) team to 0.88 points per possession and just 60 points, earning a season-opening win.
The Minutemen got a balanced scoring effort. Junjie Wang, a former [San Francisco](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/san-francisco-dons) big who felt like a terrific add in the MAC, scored 18 points with 13 rebounds. Plus, [Northwestern](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/northwestern-wildcats) transfer Jordan Clayton stepped up, scoring 16 points and hitting four 3s.
One area I'm not sold on with UMass is shooting. The Minutemen start Danny Carbuccia at the lead guard spot, a savvy passer and elite defender, but he's not a scorer. At the three, Dwayne Wimbley Jr. shot 18% from deep last season, and Abdullah Ahmed has made zero college 3s.
UMass is best when Carbuccia can attack downhill. It's harder to attack when defenses can sag off and dare UMass to shoot.
I don't know how much I trust UMass' bench, too. Two bench players played 10+ minutes (Noa Zemljic and Cheick Sylla) in the Minutemen's first game. Sylla played spot minutes at the five when Ahmed was off the floor, so expect a steady diet of him. Zemljic scored 11 points in 15 minutes, so his role could grow.
Rim protection could be a big key for UMass, as Ahmed swatted seven shots in 18 minutes.
Between Carbuccia's pesky on-ball defense and Ahmed's rim protection, the Minutemen should defend at a high level.

### Boston College Basketball
[Boston College](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/boston-college-eagles) fans have to be excited about the Luke Murray era. The Eagles beat [Wagner](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/wagner-seahawks) 76-50 in the season opener, a big deal because Boston College struggled to beat mid-majors in the past.
Murray was Dan Hurley's right-hand man for years, and the best [UConn](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/connecticut-huskies) teams defended at an elite level. That seems to be the DNA of Murray's first team in Chestnut Hill, as the Eagles held Wagner to 0.76 points per possession and 50 points.
The positional size from 1-5 is intimidating. The smallest starter is Money Williams at 6-foot-4, with the other four starters standing 6-foot-5 or taller.
Neither of Boston College's two bigs are stars, but Luke Hunger scored nine points with five rebounds and Brandon Benjamin scored 10 points with eight rebounds in game one.
I also think Boston College has some decent offensive upside. Williams starred at [Montana](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/montana-grizzlies), and his scoring chops should translate anywhere. He's adept at getting to the foul line (15 FTA's) and scored 22 points against Wagner.
Although Carbuccia is a good defender, he's an aggressive defender, and Williams tends to make aggressive defenders pay.
Also keep an eye on Ernest Shelton and Zak Smrekar. Shelton is the best shooter on the team and hit three triples to start the season.
Meanwhile, Smrekar, a 20-year-old Slovenian, gives Boston College another 6-foot-6 ball-handling option who can score from all three levels. He's a ceiling booster for this BC team.

## UMass vs. Boston College Pick, Betting Analysis
I'm taking Boston College.
I see the vision with UMass being a middle-tier MAC squad, but the shooting woes push me away from the Minutemen in this matchup.
The Eagles can match UMass' height, and I trust Boston College's guards more, especially Williams against the smaller Carbuccia.

## Missouri vs Saint Louis College Basketball Preview
![Header First Logo](https://static.sprtactn.co/teamlogos/ncaab/100/miz.png)
### Missouri Basketball
Missouri head coach Dennis Gates had a remarkable offseason heading into his fifth campaign at the helm, with the Tigers’ roster undergoing a complete overhaul.
Gates led the Tigers to the NCAA Tournament in three of his first four seasons as head coach in Columbia, with the sole absence coming in 2023.
In one of the more surprising recruiting commitments of the 2026 freshman class, Gates landed the eighth-ranked player in the country, 6-foot-3 guard Jason Crowe Jr.
Crowe is an exceptional athlete and elite natural-scoring lefty combo guard who boasts elite explosiveness and a beautiful 3-point stroke with deep range.
The Tigers’ 18-year-old superstar just put up an impressive 23 points in a 51-point beatdown over Cleveland State.
Crowe leads an elite backcourt that also features fellow true freshman Guard Toni Bryant.
Gates also retained all-SEC forward Mark Mitchell, who picked up right where he left off last season, posting an exceptional 11-point, five-rebound, 10-assist stat line on Tuesday night in just 24 minutes.
Missouri’s frontcourt boasts sizable experience with 6-foot-10 senior forward Trent Pierce returning for his final collegiate season. Pierce adds some much-needed veteran leadership to a rotation oozing with elite young talent.
The Tigers had one of the most successful transfer portal classes in all of college basketball, featuring Bryson Tiller, Jaylen Carey, Jamier Jones and Kennard Davis Jr., amongst others.
This is easily the deepest and most talented lineup Missouri has had in the Gates era, boasting elite length, size and physicality in its frontcourt alongside Crowe.
The Tigers are absolutely loaded from top to bottom and are a fringe top-25 team.

### Saint Louis Basketball
The Billikens enter the season with high hopes after a school-record 29 victories.
In a transfer-portal era defined by complete team overhauls, Saint Louis boasts some unique roster continuity, retaining five players who averaged over nine points per game.
Head coach Josh Schertz has built an elite high-octane offensive system that will largely remain intact this season, and Saint Louis’ lineup chemistry will undoubtedly play a major role in the team’s success, particularly early on in the season.
While Robbie Avila’s eligibility remains in limbo, Saint Louis will have to do without him on Friday, which I am sure the Bills be able to manage given their significant roster retention.
Schertz returned the likes of Trey Green, Kellen Thames, Quentin Jones, Amari McCottry and Ishan Sharma.

## Missouri vs. Saint Louis Pick, Betting Analysis
I am expecting fireworks on Friday night as these two prepare to square off in their first meeting since 2001.
In addition to the enticing "Missouri clash" narrative, we will see an old-school versus new-age duel that pits roster continuity against the modern portal era.
While Saint Louis thrived all of last season largely due to its exceptional 3-point shooting prowess — setting a program record with 379 triples — the Billikens, unfortunately, will run into a Missouri squad boasting freakish length and athleticism that could give Schertz’s bunch problems implementing its high-octane offensive system.
Though Saint Louis has some impressive length on its roster too, with Thames standing at 6-foot-6 with a spectacular 3-point stroke, this Tigers’ lineup has some incredible genetics on its side and could very well put an experienced Billiken squad out of its comfort zone.
The Billikens are undoubtedly gifted offensively, but I expect this to be a very difficult challenge for them on the defensive end of the floor versus a Tiger squad I feel has legitimate Final Four upside with Crowe leading the charge.
While I typically would favor backing the more experienced team this early-on in the season, I'm rolling with Missouri both on the -194 moneyline and to cover its -4.5 spread in what I expect to be a “put the country on notice” type of performance.
As a third betting angle, I feel confident backing Crowe to soar over his points total in what I expect to be another huge performance from the Tigers’ youngster.
