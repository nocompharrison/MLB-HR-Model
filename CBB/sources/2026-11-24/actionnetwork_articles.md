## Creighton vs. Michigan
[![Creighton Bluejays Logo](https://static.sprtactn.co/teamlogos/ncaab/100/crei.png)](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/creighton-bluejays)
Tuesday, Nov. 24
5:30 p.m. ET
ESPN
[![Michigan Wolverines Logo](https://static.sprtactn.co/teamlogos/ncaab/100/mich.png)](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/michigan-wolverines)
Michigan -4.5
[![FanDuel Logo](https://assets.actionnetwork.com/122955_FanDuelSportsBook@1x.jpg)](https://www.actionnetwork.com/online-sports-betting/reviews/fanduel)
By [Sean Paul](https://www.actionnetwork.com/article/author/sean-paul-smock-ii)
The Mike Boynton era in Ann Arbor is off to an inauspicious start, as the Wolverines are 3-2 and are coming off a difficult loss to [Villanova](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/villanova-wildcats) in Philly. The other loss was to [UConn](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/connecticut-huskies), which is clearly better than [Michigan](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/michigan-wolverines), so there's no shame in that.
Usually, if you grab 20 offensive boards, you aren't losing. Michigan gave up 18 to Villanova and went a dreadful 7-for-31 (26%) from deep.
That's the story for Michigan thus far: poor shooting. The Wolverines are 28% from deep, with over 45% of their shots coming from 3. Trey McKenney is Michigan's top shooter, connecting on 37% of his 8.6 attempts per game. Freshman forward Quinn Costello is also close to 40% from downtown.
Meanwhile, [Creighton](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/creighton-bluejays) is also bringing on a new coach — Alan Huss — who mirrors his offensive style after his mentor, Greg McDermott. The Jays want to push the tempo — not too fast, but an opportunistically quick pace — and shoot 3s close to half the time.
The Jays are attempting 3s on 50.2% of their shots under Huss and are hitting 34% of them. They lack a scoring big who the Creighton guards can dump the ball to on post-ups, so shooting is paramount.
Ultimately, though, I'm laying the points with Michigan here. I'll buy some Wolverines stock.
I don't expect them to shoot it well this season, but they have a stout defense and dominate the boards.
Creighton gives up a fair share of offensive boards, too.
This feels like a perfect get-right spot for Michigan.
**Pick:[ Michigan -4.5](https://playbookbot.com/books/fanduel/passthrough?deeplinkId%5B0%5D=ML3242280725&context=playbook-slack-quickslip&sport=basketball)**

Off to an unblemished start this season, [Vanderbilt](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/vanderbilt-commodores) has been propelled by the stellar play of National Player of the Year candidate Tyler Tanner.
Averaging 18.7 points, 6.2 rebounds and 3.5 assists per game, Tanner stands out as one of the nation's fastest guards and a defensive force, snatching 2.5 steals per game.
Meanwhile, the Buckeyes enter this matchup with a 4-2 record, with their only two defeats coming away from home against top-30 KenPom teams in [BYU](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/byu-cougars) and UConn.
Securing a home victory against an undefeated, top-five KenPom opponent would mark a signature non-conference win for head coach Jake Diebler and [Ohio State](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/ohio-state-buckeyes).
Although playing on the road will present a difficult challenge for Vanderbilt, the Dores' superior point guard play and dominance on the glass at both ends will be the difference.
I anticipate a star-caliber performance from Tanner, carrying Vanderbilt to a victory and cover in this matchup.

## Pitt vs Oklahoma College Basketball Preview
![Header First Logo](https://static.sprtactn.co/teamlogos/ncaab/100/pit.png)
### Pitt Basketball
Through the first few weeks of the season, the Panthers look fairly strong. Pitt has already picked up wins over [Penn State](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/penn-state-nittany-lions), [Butler](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/butler-bulldogs) and [Villanova](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/villanova-wildcats), and now looks to secure its fourth top-100 win.
Across those three games, the Panthers haven't shown many flaws. The biggest issues came against Penn State, where they struggled with fouling and defensive rebounding, and Butler, where they had trouble drawing fouls.
Outside of those areas, the Panthers have been nails.
Head coach Jeff Capel has put together a cohesive, well-rounded team early in the season.
![Header First Logo](https://static.sprtactn.co/teamlogos/ncaab/100/okl.png)
### Oklahoma Basketball
Porter Moser has to own one of the hottest seats in college basketball at the moment, especially with a team that has been unable to build commanding leads while enduring what has been the easiest schedule in college basketball.
This is a top-70 team, per Bart Torvik's rankings, but the Sooners' start to the season has been abysmal.
In every game Oklahoma has played, it has been at least a 20-point favorite, yet the Sooners have underperformed in every game.

## Pitt vs. Oklahoma Pick, Betting Analysis
Unless this is one of the biggest get-right spots of all time, Oklahoma has been a corpse all season long.
The Sooners are 0-5 ATS and already have a loss to a bottom-100 team on their resume. Unless they are sleeping in until they take on a top-100 team, we're backing Pitt on the moneyline.
The Panthers have at least been tested a few times this season and have performed at a high level. Capel and his boys have done a great job competing against top-100 competition on both ends of the floor. Pitt has excelled at protecting the basketball, forcing turnovers and defending the interior.
Pitt has also done a great job limiting 3-point attempts this season. That's one area where the Panthers need to step up and prevent the Sooners from beating them. Oklahoma ranks 23rd in 3-point rate but just 184th in 3-point percentage.
It will be crucial for Pitt not to allow Oklahoma to get hot from beyond the arc.

## Maryland vs Tennessee College Basketball Preview
![Header First Logo](https://static.sprtactn.co/teamlogos/ncaab/100/mar.png)
### Maryland Basketball
The Terrapins have encountered a fairly difficult schedule out of the gate, playing both [Georgetown](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/georgetown-hoyas) and [Virginia](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/virginia-cavaliers) and losing both games.
It doesn't help that these games were scheduled back-to-back, giving Maryland no tuneup to clean things up in between.
Now, with two matchups to clean up their mess, the Terrapins draw their third top-60 opponent of the season.
Expect big things from senior forward Pharrel Payne, who leads the team in scoring with nearly 20 points per game.
On top of that, second-year starter Andre Mills is peppering the stat sheet, averaging 13 points, 3.8 rebounds and a team-leading 1.6 steals per game.
This could be a scrappy bunch if they can gel against top-100 opponents.
![Header First Logo](https://static.sprtactn.co/teamlogos/ncaab/100/ten.png)
### Tennessee Basketball
We know Rick Barnes-coached teams are going to have some of the country's best defensive units, and this 2026-27 Tennessee squad is already ranked No. 1 in a handful of defensive categories.
The offense has also looked fairly solid this season, ranking 40th in 2-point percentage and 12th in close 2-point percentage. The Volunteers have struggled from deep, but they haven't relied on the 3-point shot much, ranking 249th in 3-point rate.
Fresh off their domination of [Michigan State](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/michigan-state-spartans), the Vols are eyeing yet another top-50 win to add to their resume.
If this unit can look as good as it did against Tom Izzo and the Spartans, we're going to have one very lethal Tennessee team.
![Header First Logo](https://static.sprtactn.co/teamlogos/ncaab/100/mar.png)
![Header Second Logo](https://static.sprtactn.co/teamlogos/ncaab/100/ten.png)
## Maryland vs. Tennessee Pick, Betting Analysis
The pace of play in this matchup should have very little urgency, as both teams lean toward a much slower, methodical tempo. Haslametrics ranks Maryland as the 204th-fastest team in the country, while Tennessee is the 30th-slowest.
If I were forced to pick a side, it has to be the Volunteers. Sure, their schedule has been relatively easy, but they are suffocating the life out of every offense they encounter.
Tennessee currently sits No. 1 in the country in effective field goal percentage defense, 2-point percentage defense and close 2-point percentage defense.
Maryland's offense loves to attack the rim, owning the 17th-highest close 2-point share, per Bart Torvik. Rick Barnes is going to have his boys clog the lane, forcing the Terrapins to take shots outside the paint. So far this season, Maryland ranks 279th in 3-point percentage and 223rd in 3-point rate.
Even if Tennessee's offense is humming, its defense should be able to keep Maryland's from getting out of hand. However, the Terrapins' defense has excelled at forcing turnovers and guarding the interior.
A near 150 total for a Tennessee basketball game just feels like a ton.

### TCU Basketball
There was a lot of optimism for TCU entering this campaign. Fresh off an NCAA Tournament win and a game that was close at halftime against Duke, the Horned Frogs brought back three starters: Xavier Edmonds, Brock Harding and Micah Robinson.
However, they lost their heart and soul — David Punch — to [Texas](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/texas-longhorns).
And the Frogs' defense has lacked the toughness Punch provided. They rank 51st in defensive efficiency after finishing 22nd last season, with rebounding being their biggest flaw in 2026-27.
The other numbers are close to in-line — if not better than last season's.
Plus, adding Robbie Avila, who's known as a poor defender, isn’t conducive to improving on that end of the floor.
The Horned Frogs will rely heavily on pressure to force turnovers, as they rank 18th with a 24.4% turnover rate. On the flip side, opponents are 32% from 3 (183rd) and 50% on 2s (155th).
In general, the Avila fit felt a bit imperfect and reeks of desperation. TCU barely snuck past UL Monroe in two overtimes. In Avila's debut against Texas A&M, the Avila-led defense gave up 1.20 points per possession in an overtime loss.
On the other end, TCU ranks 74th in offensive efficiency. You won't find a more inefficient high-major team from inside the arc than TCU, which shoots 46%. That's tough living and forces the Horned Frogs to keep up their 38% shooting from 3 and 50%+ free-throw rate.
The Horned Frogs have seven players averaging 10+ points per game, led by Robinson, who's grown to 18 points a night while shooting 43% from deep. The 6-foot-6, 230-pound wing is a real mismatch with his strength and ability to shoot from downtown.
I'm disappointed by Edmonds thus far. He's struggled with a bigger workload, dropping from 57% shooting to 44% this season.
The calculus changes for TCU without a true interior finisher.

### Miami Basketball
Miami lost to [Florida](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/florida-gators) to start the season and then promptly trounced five mid-major schools before the Players Era 16.
Good luck to this inefficient TCU offense because Miami has a vaunted defense. The Hurricanes boast the fifth most efficient defense in the country, while holding teams to 35% from inside the arc, which ranks second nationally.
Not only is Miami a physical defense, but the Canes play smart, sending teams to the line 24% of the time (20th). Plus, the Hurricanes allow offensive boards at a 20% clip. The way to beat Miami is to toss up 3s and say a prayer because there's no good way to top one of the scariest defenses in the country.
I'll admit, Miami has some serious offensive holes. The Canes rank 44th in offensive efficiency, thanks to their strong transition offense and 38% offensive rebound rate.
Things can be a bit messy in the half-court, though, as they shoot 30% from deep, and the only somewhat reliable shooter in the starting lineup is Caleb Gaskins (33%).
If Malik Reneau is back from his shoulder injury, that would help Miami shooting-wise, as he shot 34% from 3 last year.
Shelton Henderson is the best player for Miami, averaging 14.5 points and 8.7 rebounds per game. He's an athletic force with brute strength, but he's a non-shooter.
He's in the ideal system for his skill set under Jai Lucas, though.
