## Pitt vs Penn State College Basketball Preview
![Header First Logo](https://static.sprtactn.co/teamlogos/ncaab/100/pit.png)
### Pitt Basketball
It feels like a make-or-break season for Pittsburgh’s Jeff Capel.
Last year was a mess and Capel constructed this year's roster with a bunch of ACC castoffs and one potential freshman star. To me, that says, "let’s put together a bunch of talent and let’s see if it meshes."
I’m not kidding about the ACC nugget; four of the five starters are ACC transfers: Baye Ndongo (GA Tech), Naithan George ([Syracuse](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/syracuse-orange)), Akir Souare (Syracuse), Jonathan Powell (UNC), and even Jalil Bethea started his career at [Miami](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/miami-fl-hurricanes).
The best of the bunch might be freshman forward Chase Foster, who scored 20+ points in both of the Panthers' first two games. Foster was a top-40 recruit, so this performance isn’t surprising. He should probably be a starter, though.
Ndongo has also been a standout for the Panthers. The 6-9 forward boasts averages of 17 ppg and 13 rpg through two games. He starts at the four but plays most of his minutes at the five, which could lead to foul issues against Penn State's Ivan Juric.
My issue is the Panthers' backcourt. George is a reliable floor general, but scoring isn’t his forte. He’s shooting below 40% from the field and just above 30% from 3 in his 3+ college seasons.
I view the keys to Pitt’s success to be Jonathan Powell, a career sub-40 percent shooter from the field and A’lahn Sumler, who averaged 18 ppg on 49% shooting at [Charleston Southern](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/charleston-southern-buccaneers).
![Header First Logo](https://static.sprtactn.co/teamlogos/ncaab/100/psu.png)
### Penn State Basketball
Penn State is one of the toughest high-major jobs in the country. Between a stronger commitment to football and a lack of NIL, Mike Rhoades has the deck stacked against him. However, I think he put together a better roster than people think.
Rhoades assembled one of the tallest rosters in CBB, ranking 16th in average height. Four of the five Penn State starters stand 6-7 or taller, including the aforementioned 7-footer Juric manning the middle.
Moreover, Penn State’s leading scorer is Roko Prkacin, who averages 20.5 ppg off the bench. Prkacin was once lauded as a potential NBA prospect, so he should end up being one of the Nittany Lions’ best players.
They boast a nice blend of shooting and size. Juric isn’t a shooter, but Josh Reed is 6-8 and shot 36% from 3 last year; Brant Byers is off to a tough start after shooting close to 40% from 3 at Miami (OH) and Thomas Allard is shooting 37% from deep this year.
I expect Penn State to have defensive struggles like it did last year. In the game against [Chattanooga](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/chattanooga-mocs), the Nittany Lions gave up 1.25 ppp and the Mocs shot 62% on 2s.
On the flip side, if Penn State can luck into some missed-shot luck, it should have no problem putting away the rebound.

![Header Second Logo](https://static.sprtactn.co/teamlogos/ncaab/100/psu.png)
## Pitt vs. Penn State Pick, Betting Analysis
Penn State is pretty underrated in the betting market in my view. Are they the most talented team in the Big Ten? No, but Rhoades has a clear style: using their size to win the shot-volume battle at a high tempo that should wear teams down.
On the contrary, what does Pitt want to be? It’s tough to tell with the guards. In games where the guards are hitting their shots, they’ll win some games. But it could get ugly when some of the inefficient scorers take poor shots.
I view these teams as close to even and think the line overvalues Pitt.

## Houston vs Cal College Basketball Preview
![Header First Logo](https://static.sprtactn.co/teamlogos/ncaab/100/hou.png)
### Houston Basketball
Kelvin Sampson is doing Kelvin Sampson things. Houston thrashed [La Salle](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/la-salle-explorers), 94-55, in the season opener. The Cougars held La Salle to 0.73 points per possession and just 33% shooting on 2s.
The Cougars' DNA is the same. They'll trap the post and force teams to turn the ball over or shoot a late-clock 3. On the offensive end, Houston will wear opponents down with its relentless effort on the boards.
Sampson finally has two really good bigs again. Jojo Tugler is back for his senior season, and he's one of the best defensive players in the country.
The newcomer is Delrecco Gillespie, who averaged a double-double at [Kent State](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/kent-state-golden-flashes). Although he didn't notch a double-double in his Houston debut, Gillespie finished with 13 points and seven rebounds.
I'm curious how Houston's guard play comes together because I loved the Dedan Thomas Jr. addition, who averaged 16.5 points with 5.0 assists and just 1.4 turnovers a night at [LSU](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/lsu-tigers) last season. Thomas isn't a great shooter, but he's terrific in the pick-and-roll game and makes winning plays.
It seems like Corey Hadnot II will end up playing a big role, even if it's off the bench. He gives Sampson another ball-handler, as Chase McCarty and Mercy Miller round out the starting lineup as catch-and-shoot wings.
Sampson will tinker to find which combo works best, but the trio of Tugler, Gillespie and Thomas is a dream trio for Sampson.

### Cal Basketball
There’s no sugarcoating it. Mark Madsen has one of the most difficult high-major gigs in the NIL era. The Golden Bears' NIL resources lag behind those of ACC counterparts, making it imperative that Madsen make the most of his team's talent.
The Golden Bears have an unblemished 2-0 record so far, but the wins came against Adams State (Non-D1) and [Radford](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/radford-highlanders).
Thankfully for Madsen, it seems like he found a scorer in Nojus Indrusaitis, who poured in 30 points against Radford and 20 against Adams State.
Then the lack of talent quickly stands out. The next best player is … Jordan Ross? Ross is a perfect sixth man for a team with NCAA Tournament aspirations. Instead, he’s an underwhelming point guard for possibly the least talented high-major team.
The one X-factor with really high upside is Jake Wilkins, who played a decent bench role at [Georgia](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/georgia-bulldogs) last season. Wilkins is the son of NBA legend Dominique Wilkins and he has some of his dad's athleticism. If he can unlock other areas of his game, Cal may have found a gem.
Cal needs big man Lee Dort back, and needs him badly. He averaged 8.1 points and 7.8 rebounds per game last season and had 18 rebounds against Florida State in the ACC Tournament.
Without Dort, Cal has to start Sammie Yeanay, who barely played last season and went 1-for-7 from the field against Radford.

## Houston vs. Cal Pick, Betting Analysis
With Dort's status up in the air, Houston should destroy the undermanned Cal interior. If Madsen has to rely on Yeanay and Mantas Kocanas, he might run out of bigs.
Gillespie and Tugler will get those two in foul trouble quickly.
My big question: How is Cal scoring? Indrusaitis can and will shoot. So will Michael Cooper off the bench. Cal is pretty lean on shooting outside of those two, so how can the Bears score on this vaunted Houston defense?
This feels like a 20+ point win for Sampson's crew.

### Wichita State Basketball
Is this the year Wichita State returns to national prominence? It’s the Shockers' best chance to make the NCAA Tournament in the Paul Mills era.
The Shockers return six of their top seven scorers (TJ Williams is likely out for this matchup). Wichita State should have the continuity advantage in most games it plays during the non-conference slate.
The Shockers boast an electric guard/big tandem with guard Kenyon Giles and big man William Berg. Giles is playing on a TRO, as many Class of 2022 players are, so his availability is subject to change long term. As of now, Giles can play and he dropped 21 points and eight assists against Cal State Northridge.
Why’s that important? Usually, Giles is a pure scorer, but his flashing point guard skills could open up this Shockers offense.
Berg was on another level in the season opener, scoring 26 points with 16 rebounds against CSU Northridge. He took advantage of a smaller Matadors team, but this matchup will be more challenging.
Mills ensured he surrounded the Berg and Giles with shooters. Next to Giles is Jordan Frison, a six-foot guard who can also handle point guard duties along with shooting 45% from 3 last year at [Chattanooga](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/chattanooga-mocs).
The other backcourt starter is Jahari Long, who adds more length next to the smaller Giles and Frison thanks to his 6-foot-5 frame.
Long-term, I’m curious if Wichita State can hold its own defensively with two guards below six-feet. That’s not very common nowadays and it could sting the Shockers.

### USC Basketball
Eric Musselman had a disappointing first two seasons in L.A., finishing 12th in the Big Ten and missing the NCAA Tournament in both seasons.
With less drama and hopefully fewer injuries, the Trojans have the talent to be a consistent top-25 level bunch.
First, can we talk about how massive USC is? All five of its starters are 6-foot-5 or taller and three of them are 6-foot-10 or taller.
And it’s not like the Trojans are sacrificing shooting for size — the Ratliff twins (Adonis and Darius) are both shooting threats. Adonis drilled five in the Trojans ' season-opening win over [Milwaukee](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/milwaukee-panthers).
What a treat it must be for Musselman to have a healthy Rodney Rice. Things went off the rails last season when Rice got hurt, and he could be one of the best point guards in the country this season. He returned to action with a 15-point, six-rebound, nine-assist outing.
The biggest wildcard is Alijah Arenas. He got thrown into a total disaster last season after a long injury layoff. Arenas is the most skilled scorer on the USC roster and gives the Trojans a higher ceiling.
Also, don’t sleep on KJ Lewis. The defensive dynamo could fill valuable minutes defending all these Wichita State guards.

## Wichita State vs. USC Pick, Betting Analysis
I see a potential double-digit win for USC here.
A few angles stand out to me. The main one? USC has the size to keep Giles and Frison in check. I’m not sure how they score without those two going off.
Secondly, the versatility of USC’s frontcourt could put Berg in a tough spot.
Lay the points with the Trojans.
