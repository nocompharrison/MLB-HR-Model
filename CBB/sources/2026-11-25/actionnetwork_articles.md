## Virginia vs. Marquette
[![Virginia Cavaliers Logo](https://static.sprtactn.co/teamlogos/ncaab/100/uva.png)](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/virginia-cavaliers)
Wednesday, Nov. 25
12 p.m. ET
ESPN
[![Marquette Golden Eagles Logo](https://static.sprtactn.co/teamlogos/ncaab/100/marq.png)](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/marquette-golden-eagles)
Virginia -5.5
[![FanDuel Logo](https://assets.actionnetwork.com/122955_FanDuelSportsBook@1x.jpg)](https://www.actionnetwork.com/online-sports-betting/reviews/fanduel)
Virginia has been an analytics darling early in the season, and for good reason. On the surface, it is hard to pick out many weaknesses from Ryan Odom’s Cavaliers.
Virginia ranks fifth in adjusted offensive efficiency, 26th in defense, dominates the boards on both ends, wins the battle at the rim and has tons of experience and continuity. The Cavs are a national title threat.
Against Marquette, many of those traits should show up early and often. Marquette has been an awful defensive rebounding team thus far, ranking 286th nationally at preventing second chances. Virginia rebounds 42.6% of its misses and may exceed that mark here.
On the other end, Marquette relies on turning teams over. The Golden Eagles force turnovers on a quarter of possessions, but with Chance Mallory and Sam Lewis on the ball, that will be hard to do against Virginia.
At some point Nigel James Jr. will break out of his slump for Marquette, but I would be shocked if it happens here.
I like Virginia big.

## Arizona vs. Ole Miss
[![Arizona Wildcats Logo](https://static.sprtactn.co/teamlogos/ncaab/100/ari.png)](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/arizona-wildcats)
Wednesday, Nov. 25
2:30 p.m. ET
ESPN
[![Ole Miss Rebels Logo](https://static.sprtactn.co/teamlogos/ncaab/100/mis.png)](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/ole-miss-rebels)
Arizona -5.5
[![FanDuel Logo](https://assets.actionnetwork.com/122955_FanDuelSportsBook@1x.jpg)](https://www.actionnetwork.com/online-sports-betting/reviews/fanduel)
Arizona has rolled through the first two rounds of the Maui Invitational all the way into the final, where it meets a resurgent team led by Chris Beard that can really pack a punch.
As much as I have been impressed by the Rebels this week, this feels like a challenge that is a bit above their weight class.
Ole Miss is all about forcing turnovers on defense, and the Rebels currently rank first in both forced turnover rate and steal percentage.
But they have also been fortunate, getting BYU without its superstar point guard Robert Wright III in the semifinal and falling behind huge before storming back late.
Arizona will have four players on the floor at once that can handle the ball. The Wildcats start two guards in JJ Mandaquit and Derek Dixon that I trust to make decisions against pressure, and if they find Caleb Holt or Ivan Kharchenkov in advantage situations downhill, good luck.’
I also expect Arizona’s size to bother Ole Miss. The Rebels are making 57% of their 2-pointers, but they take a lot of mid-range shots that Arizona can bother them on.

## Duke vs. UConn
[![Duke Blue Devils Logo](https://static.sprtactn.co/teamlogos/ncaab/100/dukd.png)](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/duke-blue-devils)
Wednesday, Nov. 25
7 p.m. ET
Prime Video
[![Connecticut Huskies Logo](https://static.sprtactn.co/teamlogos/ncaab/100/uconn.png)](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/connecticut-huskies)
UConn ML -128
[![FanDuel Logo](https://assets.actionnetwork.com/122955_FanDuelSportsBook@1x.jpg)](https://www.actionnetwork.com/online-sports-betting/reviews/fanduel)
  

Do I wish the number was more favorable to UConn here? Yes. Do I understand why it’s not? Obviously, yes.
Duke has been the single most disappointing team in college basketball this season. The question is, will that flip? And when?
Not here. Not against Dan Hurley with a week to prep off a road loss to a top-10 team on Nik Khamenia revenge night.
The biggest difference is in the backcourt, where Duke’s guards have been horrific. There’s no point guard on the roster capable of handling Silas Demary Jr. for 40 minutes, and Duke's choppy substitution patterns aren’t helping anything.
Down low, Na'jai Hines is exactly the type of center that Duke wants to avoid. The Blue Devils shy away from physicality and intensity, and every Husky in uniform will play with plenty of both.
Don’t be surprised if Jon Scheyer keeps toying with lineups in this matchup. It’s hard to know what to expect when you don’t know what works.
Take the better team with the better coach over the supposed collection of talent.

## Wake Forest vs. Mississippi State
[![Wake Forest Demon Deacons Logo](https://static.sprtactn.co/teamlogos/ncaab/100/wf.png)](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/wake-forest-demon-deacons)
Wednesday, Nov. 25
7:30 p.m. ET
ESPN2
[![Mississippi State Bulldogs Logo](https://static.sprtactn.co/teamlogos/ncaab/100/msst.png)](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/mississippi-state-bulldogs)
Mississippi State -3.5
[![FanDuel Logo](https://assets.actionnetwork.com/122955_FanDuelSportsBook@1x.jpg)](https://www.actionnetwork.com/online-sports-betting/reviews/fanduel)
Mississippi State has been monstrous on the boards to open the season. It was the Bulldogs' formula against UCF (they out-rebounded the Knights by 20), and it's a great formula for success against Wake Forest. The Deacs rank just 216th nationally in defensive rebounding.
The Bulldogs rank 10th nationally in offensive rebounding and will like their matchups here. Freshman Tristan Reed (younger brother of UConn's Tarris Reed Jr.) has already had four games with four or more offensive boards.
Mississippi State is old and deep, two things that help you a lot during Feast Week. The Bulldogs rank 54th nationally in bench minutes and 28th overall in Division I experience.
When things break down, they still have volume scorer Josh Hubbard, who can take over a game at a moment's notice.
I trust Mississippi State’s answers on the perimeter more than Wake Forest’s.
The Deacs are still looking for a Juke Harris replacement and have yet to find one at this point of the season.

### South Carolina Basketball
Let's start with the Gamecocks' ability to defend the triple, since that will be important in this affair. It has been impressive during their five-game winning streak, as South Carolina ranks 31st nationally in limiting 3-point attempts.

But that's not the only area that South Carolina projects to frustrate USC a bit in this matchup.
The Gamecocks' best offensive ability is getting to the free-throw line, which bodes well against an undisciplined Trojans defense. South Carolina is eighth nationally in free-throw rate, and USC is 227th in defensive free-throw rate allowed.
Lastly, South Carolina is a good rebounding team with good size in the frontcourt. In the event that USC goes a bit cold from downtown, its athletes will crash aggressively.
However, South Carolina should be ready. The Gamecocks are the 25th-best defensive rebounding team in the country through five games.

## Memphis vs. Penn State Pick, Betting Analysis
This game will be set to _Spaceballs_ speed because these teams will be running ludicrously.

### Wake Forest Basketball
Wake Forest opens the Battle 4 Atlantis with plenty to prove. The [Demon Deacons](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/wake-forest-demon-deacons) are 5-1, but they are still chasing their first win over a KenPom top-200 opponent.
They have shown they can take care of business against teams with less talent and size, but Mississippi State will be another test.
The Deacs were overwhelmed in their one game against an SEC team: a blowout loss at [Vanderbilt](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/vanderbilt-commodores) during the opening week.
Everything that could go wrong did go wrong for Wake Forest in that game, as it faltered to 0.77 points per possession while giving up 17 offensive rebounds.
Things could be different for head coach Steve Forbes — who, by all accounts, is one of the most likable men in college basketball — but the fact that I'm leading with that probably says something about the trajectory of his program.
Forbes has yet to make the NCAA Tournament in six full seasons at Wake, and it's only getting harder to sustain good rosters in the NIL era.
Forbes found superstar freshman Quentin Coleman before everyone else, and even earned a verbal commitment from him before he backed out and took his talents to [Illinois](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/illinois-fighting-illini). Star scorer Juke Harris left a featured role at Wake to start the season coming off the bench at [Tennessee](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/tennessee-volunteers).
It has left Forbes with a roster that is low on experience and continuity. Wake has one senior in the playing rotation, and leans heavily on the freshman sibling duo of [Gavin](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/players/gavin-placide-348265-f) and [Gallagher Placide](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/players/gallagher-placide-348264-f) to hold things down in the frontcourt.
The Deacs will be tested against Mississippi State in many of the same ways they were tested by Vanderbilt.

### Mississippi State Basketball
Like Forbes, [Bulldogs](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/mississippi-state-bulldogs) head coach Chris Jans needs a good season badly. Jans is coming off a disastrous campaign that began with the 26th-ranked roster in college basketball and ended with a 13-19 record and the No. 102 ranking on KenPom.
Jans' team is off to a better start to 2026-27, with three wins over KenPom top-150 opponents already, including a true road win over No. 78 [UCF](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/ucf-knights) that will age very well as the season progresses.
Mississippi State has been monstrous on the boards to open the season. It was the Bulldogs' formula against UCF (they out-rebounded the Knights by 20), and it's a great formula for success against Wake Forest.
The Bulldogs rank 10th nationally in offensive rebounding and will like their matchups here. Freshman [Tristan Reed](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/players/tristan-reed-349641-c) (younger brother of [UConn](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/odds/connecticut-huskies)'s Tarris Reed Jr.) has already had four games with four or more offensive boards.
Mississippi State is old and deep, two things that help you a lot during Feast Week. The Bulldogs rank 54th nationally in bench minutes and 28th overall in Division I experience.
When things break down, they still have volume scorer [Josh Hubbard](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaab/players/josh-hubbard-256365-g), who can take over a game at a moment's notice.
Add it all up, and Mississippi State has a clear formula against Wake Forest.
