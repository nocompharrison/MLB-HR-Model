The Lakers open their first season without LeBron James since 2018 as 7.5-point favorites, and the roster behind that number looks the part. Luka Dončić is now the unquestioned engine after averaging 33.5 points, 7.7 rebounds and 8.3 assists last season on .476/.366/.780 shooting, flanked by Austin Reaves (23.3 points, 4.7 rebounds, 5.5 assists) and a completely overhauled frontcourt anchored by Walker Kessler, whom the Lakers landed from Utah in a $130 million sign-and-trade this summer after Kessler posted shot-blocking numbers that put him in rare statistical company with Kareem Abdul-Jabbar and Shaquille O’Neal. With Quentin Grimes slotting in on the wing and a restructured bench featuring Collin Sexton, Matisse Thybulle and Jarred Vanderbilt, Los Angeles enters this opener deeper, healthier and more talented top-to-bottom than at almost any point in its recent post-LeBron transition, and a Dončić-Kessler pick-and-roll combination that Golden State simply doesn’t have the size to match inside is squarely at the center of that advantage.

Golden State, meanwhile, is already down two rotation pieces for the entire season — Jimmy Butler tore his ACL in January and Moses Moody ruptured his patellar tendon in March, both ending their 2025-26 campaigns early — and now Brandin Podziemski, who played all 82 games a year ago, is also out for this opener with a back injury. That leaves Steve Kerr leaning even harder on Stephen Curry, who tweaked his ankle at Sunday’s practice but is expected to play after scoring 24 points on 7-of-14 shooting in his most recent outing, a 115-110 loss to the Clippers. If Dončić and Kessler control the paint from the opening tip and Golden State’s thinned-out wing rotation simply can’t match the Lakers’ size and shot-creation on both ends, Los Angeles should cruise in its first LeBron-less opener in nearly a decade. But if Curry’s ankle holds up and he gets into an early rhythm the way he did in Golden State’s last outing, the Warriors have shown they can hang around behind their superstar alone even with the roster around him stretched thin.

## Deciding Factor: Giannis Antetokounmpo’s Miami Debut vs. Anthony Edwards’ Heat-Killer History

Miami opens its season on ESPN with arguably the most talented frontcourt in franchise history taking the floor together for the first time, and it does so at full strength — Bam Adebayo is off the injury report entirely, with only Vladislav Goldin listed as questionable, meaning Giannis Antetokounmpo makes his Heat debut alongside a healthy Bam rather than easing in around an absence. The numbers behind that pairing are staggering: Giannis is the only player in NBA history to average at least 24 points, nine rebounds, five assists and a block per game for his career (in both the regular season and playoffs), he’s shot 65.1% in the paint for his career while scoring 14.5 points per game there (third-most in 30 years of shot-location data), and last season he posted career highs in effective field goal percentage (63.6%) and true shooting (65.8%) with a staggering 79% of his shots coming at the rim. He’s also led the league in fouls drawn per game in each of the last seven seasons, a weapon that should only be amplified playing off Adebayo’s own interior gravity. Add in reports he averaged nearly a point per minute during Miami’s up-tempo preseason, and the Heat have every incentive to let their new superstar duo simply overwhelm a Minnesota front line early and often as -1.5 home favorites.

Minnesota, meanwhile, brings its own problem for Miami in Anthony Edwards, who owns a 9-3 career record against the Heat including three straight wins — a personal house-of-horrors matchup Miami hasn’t solved even with its roster retooled. The Timberwolves have also reshaped their own backcourt by acquiring LaMelo Ball from Charlotte, pairing him with Edwards and Rudy Gobert in a new-look lineup built around the physicality that’s long been Minnesota’s calling card against this specific opponent, even as they’ll be without Donte DiVincenzo (recovering from the Achilles tear he suffered in the 2026 playoffs) for perimeter depth. If Edwards’ personal dominance over Miami and Minnesota’s physical identity translate early against a Heat team still finding its chemistry with a brand-new superstar in the fold, the Timberwolves have the formula to spoil the home opener as +102 road underdogs. But if Giannis and Bam’s combined interior force proves too much for a Wolves front line adjusting to its own new faces, Miami’s two-way star power should be enough to deliver a statement win to open the season.
