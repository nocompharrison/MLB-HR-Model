  * **Predicting Minutes** : Looking at historical data around minutes played in situational spots and against certain NBA teams, try to predict the minutes each of the players in your lineup will play.   * **Team Chemistry** : For most teams, the chemistry starts with the PG. As the PG is often an extension of the head coach, the team will ebb and flow with how well the PG scores, distributes, and initiates the offense.
  * **Pace of Play** : Pace of play can dictate how many points players can score relative to their baseline. Games that are expected to be played at a fast pace will mean there are more possessions for players to accrue statistics.
  * **Recent Trends** : Whether it's minutes played or how well the SF, PG, and power forward have been performing, following recent trends to build your DFS roster is always a good idea. ## NBA Starting Lineups FAQ
Want to learn more about NBA DFS lineups and ways to play them? Read the following FAQs:
### When are game-day NBA lineups released?
Game-day NBA starters are announced typically 30 minutes before tip-off, though you can make some assumptions based on recently played games. Injuries also come into play. For example, if CLE center Jarrett Allen is battling an injury, his availability will be uncertain until just before the game's tip-off.
### What are the stats to watch when looking up NBA starting lineups?
Some key stats to watch when researching NBA DFS starting lineups are minutes, usage rate, points, rebounds, assists, blocks, and steals.
### How do NBA starting lineup stacks work?
Stacks in basketball-focused DFS lineups work by selecting multiple players from the same team or game to maximize the potential points in a projected high-scoring matchup.
